How to organise a football tournament: steps, format and number of matches

In short

To organise a football tournament, get permission and insurance sorted first, then pick a format: in a round robin, N teams play N×(N-1)÷2 matches; with pools, the number of matches is far lower.

The steps to organise a football tournament

A tournament needs several weeks of preparation. The order below avoids nasty surprises: permission and insurance come before any publicity.

Who can organise one, and what permission is needed?

Rules differ from country to country, and sometimes from region to region. In many places a tournament has to be run through, or approved by, a registered club or the local football association. Before announcing anything, ask your association whether you need authorisation, which forms to use, how far ahead to apply, and whether teams from other regions or countries are allowed. Also check whether the venue needs a booking or a permit from the owner or local authority, especially for public land.

Insurance

Check that someone holds public liability insurance covering the organiser, the volunteers and the players, and keep the paperwork where you can show it. If the tournament is run by a club, ask its insurer in writing whether the policy covers a tournament, and which activities it excludes. Requirements vary by country, so do not assume.

Choosing a format by number of teams

Two formats come up almost every time:

  • Round robin: every team plays every other team once. With N teams there are N × (N − 1) ÷ 2 matches, and each team plays N − 1 matches.
  • Pools: teams are split into groups, each played as a round robin, sometimes followed by placing matches or a knockout stage.

A tournament with 6, 10 or 12 teams

A 6-team tournament

Two pools of 3 give 6 matches. Add the final, the third place match and the fifth place match: 9 matches in total, and each team plays 3 matches.

A 10-team tournament

Two pools of 5 give 20 matches (4 per team). With five placing matches (1st v 1st, 2nd v 2nd, and so on), the total rises to 25 matches and each team plays 5. The table shows the other option, pools of 4 at most, which uses fewer matches.

A 12-team tournament

Three pools of 4 give 18 matches. Another option is four pools of 3 (12 matches), then quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final for the eight qualifiers: 19 matches in total.

Match length, pitches and rules

Match length and team size depend on the age group: see football rules by age group and ball size. The Laws of the Game give 45-minute halves as the standard, but allow shorter matches if the referee and both teams agree before kick-off and the competition rules allow it, which is how most tournaments work. To work out the total time, multiply the number of matches by the length of a match, then divide by the number of pitches available, leaving rest time between two matches for the same team. Publish a tournament rulebook (match length, tiebreakers, fair play). To separate teams level on points, use the table calculator.

Equipment and match day

  • balls of the right size, bibs, cones, suitable goals;
  • match sheets, and a board showing the schedule and scores;
  • a first aid kit and a named person in charge;
  • referees or coaches to run the matches;
  • team lists: for fair draws, the random team generator helps.

Frequently asked questions

How do you organise a tournament with 10 teams?

For example, two pools of 5: 20 pool matches, then placing matches. You can also choose pools of 4 at most, which need fewer matches (see the table).

How do you organise a tournament with 6 teams?

Two pools of 3 give 6 matches, then a final, a third place match and a fifth place match: 9 matches in total.

How many matches are there with 12 teams?

With three pools of 4: 18 matches. A full round robin with one pool of 12 would be 66 matches, which is rarely practical.

Do you need permission to organise a football tournament?

It depends on where you are. Many local football associations require approval for tournaments, so ask yours before announcing anything.

Is insurance necessary?

You should always check that public liability insurance covers the organiser, the volunteers and the players. Requirements vary by country, so ask your association or insurer.
