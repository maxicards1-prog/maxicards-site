Health disclaimer

General guidelines, not medical advice: what to know before you run.

In short: the information and tools on this site are general. They are not medical advice, a diagnosis or a personalized programme.

See a doctor before starting or resuming exercise

Medical advice is recommended before starting running or resuming it after a long break, especially if you are sedentary, over 40, pregnant, overweight, or if you have a condition (heart, lungs, diabetes, high blood pressure, joints) or ongoing treatment.

Many organized races ask for a medical certificate, a health questionnaire or a licence, and the rules differ from one country to another: check with the organizer of your race.

Stop and seek advice if you notice warning signs

  • chest pain, palpitations, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or faintness;
  • pain that persists, gets worse or makes you limp;
  • fever, or strong heat with headache, nausea or confusion;
  • any unusual symptom during or after exercise.

In a serious doubt or an emergency, call your local emergency number (for example 911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the European Union).

What the calculations on this site are worth

The formulas (MAS, maximum heart rate, time prediction) are estimates drawn from groups of people: the gap with your real value can be large. The training plans are general models: they do not take into account your age, your past injuries, your diet or your sleep.

Listen to your body: a plan can be changed, a pain must not be ignored.
