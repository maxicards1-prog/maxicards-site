Beginner 5K plan, 8 weeks

  • Calculated in your browser
  • Free, no limits
  • No sign-up
  • No data sent

Eight weeks, three workouts a week, from run-walk to a continuous 5K. A gradual plan, at your own pace.

How to use it

  1. Read the principles and check that you can walk 30 minutes without discomfort; if in doubt, see a doctor.
  2. If you know your MAS, enter it in the field above: the paces will show. Otherwise, run at a pace where you can talk.
  3. Do 3 workouts a week, never two days in a row, following the run-walk blocks.
  4. Move to the next week when the previous one feels comfortable; repeat it otherwise.

Important: This plan gives general guidelines: it is not medical advice and it does not adapt to your personal situation. See a doctor before starting or resuming exercise, and stop if you feel pain, faintness or any unusual symptom.

Goal: run 5K, or 30 to 35 minutes, without stopping. Length: 8 weeks, 3 workouts per week. Who it is for: Being able to walk 30 minutes at a brisk pace without discomfort. No running experience needed.

The principles of the plan

  • Run-walk intervals: your body adapts gradually, and so do your tendons and joints.
  • Easy pace everywhere: you should be able to talk while running, in short sentences. If you are out of breath, slow down or walk.
  • Three workouts a week, never two days in a row: rest is part of the plan.
  • If a week is too hard, repeat it before moving on: the plan has no deadline.

The plan, week by week

Week 1 : Getting started

  • Workout 1. 5 min brisk walk, then 8 × (1 min easy running 60 to 70% MAS + 2 min walking), then 5 min walking.
  • Workout 2. Same workout: 8 × (1 min running + 2 min walking), with a rest day on each side.
  • Workout 3. Same workout, with 1 extra minute of running at the end if you feel good.

Week 2 : A little longer

  • Workout 1. 5 min walking, then 6 × (2 min running 60 to 70% MAS + 2 min walking), then 5 min walking.
  • Workout 2. Same workout: 6 × (2 min running + 2 min walking).
  • Workout 3. 5 × (3 min running + 2 min walking), after 5 min walking.

Week 3 : Running 3 to 5 minutes

  • Workout 1. 5 min walking, then 5 × (3 min running + 1 min 30 walking), then 5 min walking.
  • Workout 2. 4 × (5 min running + 2 min walking), after 5 min walking.
  • Workout 3. 3 × (5 min running + 1 min 30 walking), then 1 × 3 min running.

Week 4 : Lengthening the blocks

  • Workout 1. 5 min walking, then 3 × (8 min running 60 to 70% MAS + 2 min walking).
  • Workout 2. 2 × (10 min running + 2 min walking), after 5 min walking.
  • Workout 3. 8 min running + 2 min walking + 12 min running.

Week 5 : Blocks of 10 to 15 minutes

  • Workout 1. 5 min walking, then 2 × (12 min running 60 to 70% MAS + 1 min walking).
  • Workout 2. 15 min running + 2 min walking + 10 min running.
  • Workout 3. 20 min continuous easy running, with 5 min walking before and after.

Week 6 : Continuous running

  • Workout 1. 5 min walking, then 22 min continuous running 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Workout 2. 2 × (13 min running + 1 min walking).
  • Workout 3. 25 min continuous running, very easy.

Week 7 : Towards 30 minutes

  • Workout 1. 5 min walking, then 25 min continuous running 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Workout 2. 20 min easy running, with 4 × 30 s a little livelier 75 to 80% MAS (recovery: back to easy pace).
  • Workout 3. 30 min continuous running, very easy.

Week 8 : Goal week

  • Workout 1. 25 min easy, relaxed running 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Workout 2. 15 min very easy running: stay fresh.
  • Workout 3: goal. 5K, or 30 to 35 minutes of running, at your own pace 60 to 70% MAS. Walking a little is not a failure: the aim is to finish happy.

What is this plan based on?

The principles (cautious progression, one long run a week, easier weeks, a two-week taper) are those of the classic running plans: Daniels, Pfitzinger, Higdon.

The taper follows the conclusions of the meta-analysis by Bosquet et al. (2007): about two weeks, volume cut by 40 to 60%, intensity and frequency kept. Full references are on the Sources page. This plan is a general model: it does not replace a coach or a doctor's advice.

Print or keep the plan

Frequently asked questions

Can I go from zero to 5K in 8 weeks?

That is the principle of run-walk plans, such as the 9-week "Couch to 5K" programme of the UK's National Health Service. Everyone progresses at their own pace: if a week is too hard, repeat it.

Do I need to know my MAS for this plan?

No: as a beginner, simply run at a pace where you can talk. MAS is optional and is used to display indicative paces.

What if I miss a workout?

Simply pick the plan up where you were, without making up the missed workout. If you are ill or injured, wait until you are better and, if needed, ask a doctor's advice.

How many rest days?

At least one rest day between two workouts. On non-running days, easy walking or cycling is possible, but rest is part of the progress.

Should I walk during the running blocks?

Yes, when the plan says so. Walking is not a failure: it lets you recover and gradually lengthen your running.
