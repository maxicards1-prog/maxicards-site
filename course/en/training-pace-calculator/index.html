Your MAS gives all your paces: easy running, threshold, race paces and intervals, with a range for each.

How to use it

  1. Enter your MAS in km/h or mph (worked out with the half-Cooper or Cooper test).
  2. Read the table: each line gives a range of paces in min/km and min/mile, and the matching speeds.
  3. For a workout, choose the line that matches its goal: easy, threshold, intervals…
  4. Respect both ends of the range: running faster than the range does not make the workout better.

Formula: pace (s/km) = 3,600 ÷ (MAS × percentage); for example, 65% of an MAS of 15 km/h = 9.75 km/h, or 6:09 min/km (9:55 min/mile). The percentages are coaches' guidelines, not a law: see Sources.

The MAS percentages used

The following zones are the ones most often found among coaches and federations. They overlap a little, and that is normal: the same pace can serve several workouts depending on its length.

  • Easy running: 60 to 70%. The pace of most of your runs; you can talk without difficulty.
  • Marathon pace: about 70 to 80%. Sources range from 65 to 85% depending on the runner's level.
  • Half marathon pace: 75 to 85%.
  • Threshold: 80 to 90%. A "comfortably hard" effort, which you can hold for 20 to 60 minutes depending on the runner.
  • 10K pace: 85 to 90%.
  • Long intervals: 90 to 95%; short intervals: 95 to 105%. Repeats of 30 seconds to 3 minutes, with recovery.

The limits of the method

MAS says nothing about your endurance: two runners with the same MAS do not hold the same percentage in a marathon. That is why the marathon pace ranges are wide.

An MAS estimated with a field test can also vary by 1 km/h from one day to the next: if in doubt, run a little slower than the calculated pace, never faster. For heart rate zones, see the heart rate zones calculator. For ready-made workouts, open the 10K plan.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my training paces?

Multiply your MAS by the percentage you want, then convert the speed you get into minutes per kilometer: pace = 3,600 ÷ speed in km/h, in seconds per km.

What percentage of MAS should I use for easy runs?

Sources generally give 60 to 70% of MAS for easy running. If you are out of breath or cannot talk any more, slow down.

What threshold pace compared with MAS?

The usual range is 80 to 90% of MAS, depending on the definition of threshold used. Beginners stay in the lower part of the range.

What if my MAS changes?

Repeat a test every 6 to 8 weeks or so. After a test, replace the value in the tool: all your percentages are recalculated.

Are these results 100% accurate?

No: they are estimates based on published formulas. They give a good order of magnitude to organize your training, but your form on the day, the terrain, the weather and your experience can change the real result. Use them as a guide, not as a promise.
