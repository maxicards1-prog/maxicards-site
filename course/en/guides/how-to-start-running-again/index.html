How to Start Running Again After a Break

The short answer

To start running again after a long break, restart well below your old level: short runs, easy pace, run-walk if needed, and progress in small steps. After an injury, illness or pregnancy, a health professional's advice comes first.

How to use it

  1. Restart lower than before. Choose short runs at a pace where you can talk, even if you used to run much faster.
  2. Use run-walk. Alternate running and walking phases, for example 2 minutes and 1 minute.
  3. Increase only one thing at a time. Lengthen the duration or add a session, not both at once.
  4. Leave time between sessions. Keep at least one non-running day between two runs at first.
  5. Test again when you are ready. A few weeks later, redo a maximal aerobic speed test to reset your paces.

Why restart slowly?

Your heart and lungs recover fairly quickly, but tendons, bones and muscles adapt more slowly. Restarting at your pre-break level is a common mistake.

Studies on running injuries link too fast a rise in load to a higher risk: in the study by Nielsen et al. (2014), raising distance by more than 30% a week went with more injuries, though it was not statistically conclusive. Take this result as a reason for caution, not as a threshold.

Restarting after several months or years

Think of yourself as a beginner for the first weeks. The guide how to start running and the beginner 5K plan are a good base. The run-walk method is detailed in run 30 minutes without stopping.

After an injury, a sprain or a stress fracture

Your return must be cleared by your doctor or physiotherapist, who knows your injury. Do not try to get back to your old volume: ask them when to restart and at what load. This guide does not replace their advice.

After 50, 60 or older

The principle is the same: gradual progression, easy pace, enough rest. A medical check-up before restarting after a long sedentary period is useful, especially with a history of heart problems, high blood pressure or asthma.

How much should you aim for?

The WHO recommends 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week for adults, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity. You do not have to get there right away: it is a benchmark over time. For running volume, see how many miles per week.

Once you feel comfortable again, a test such as the 6-minute test will help reset your paces.

Pain, unusual breathlessness, chest discomfort, palpitations or feeling faint: stop and see a doctor. This guide is general; it does not replace medical advice.

Sources: Nielsen et al. (2014), J Orthop Sports Phys Ther; WHO (2020), Br J Sports Med (accessed 24/11/2026).

Frequently asked questions

How do I start running again after a long break?

Like a beginner: short runs, easy pace, run-walk if needed, small steps in progression and rest days between sessions.

How do I start running again after a sprained ankle?

After your doctor or physiotherapist agrees, and at the load they give you; do not restart at your old volume.

Can I start running again after 60?

Yes for many people, with gradual progression and, ideally, a medical check-up before starting.

How do I start running again when overweight?

Begin with brisk walking, then run-walk, in short sessions; a medical opinion before starting is recommended.
