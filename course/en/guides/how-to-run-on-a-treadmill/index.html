How to Run on a Treadmill

The short answer

On a treadmill, start gently and set the speed from your pace per mile or kilometer. A 1% incline is the most cited guideline for approximating the energy cost of running outdoors.

How to use it

  1. Warm up. Start with a few minutes of walking, then very slow running (see warm-up).
  2. Convert your pace. The treadmill shows mph or km/h: a 10-minute mile is 6 mph and 6 min/km is 10 km/h. Use the converter for other paces.
  3. Set a 1% incline. It is the research guideline to make up for the lack of air resistance.
  4. Run in the middle of the belt. Look ahead, not at your feet, and stand tall without holding on to the rails.
  5. Finish with a cool-down. Slow down gradually before stopping the belt.

Why a 1% incline?

Outdoors, a runner fights air resistance; on a treadmill there is none, so effort is slightly lower at the same speed.

The study by Jones and Doust (1996, Journal of Sports Sciences), with nine trained runners at speeds from about 6.5 to 11.2 mph (10.5 to 18 km/h), concluded that a 1% grade reproduces the energy cost of outdoor running.

The study is small and later work has debated its results: treat the 1% grade as a guideline, not an exact rule.

Setting the speed: from pace to mph or km/h

The treadmill shows speed, whereas you may think in pace. The conversion is: speed (km/h) = 60 ÷ pace (min/km), or speed (mph) = 60 ÷ pace (min/mile). Example: a 10-minute mile is 6 mph; 6 min/km is 10 km/h (6.2 mph).

The pace-speed converter does it for you, and the pace calculator gives the pace of a session you have already run.

Differences from running outside

  • No wind, hills or turns: the rhythm is very regular.
  • The treadmill sets the pace: you can listen to yourself less than outdoors. So start more slowly than you would outside.
  • For a test, always compare under the same conditions: see the maximal aerobic speed calculator.

Making the session go faster

Vary the phases: alternate a few faster and slower minutes, or follow the run-walk method in run 30 minutes without stopping. Read your treadmill's manual and clip the safety key to your clothing.

If you feel pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness, stop the treadmill and ask a doctor. This guide does not replace the manufacturer's instructions.

Source: Jones and Doust (1996), J Sports Sci (accessed 24/11/2026).

Frequently asked questions

How do I run on a treadmill?

Warm up, convert your pace into mph or km/h, set a 1% incline if you wish, run in the center of the belt and finish with a cool-down.

Is it better to run on a treadmill or outside?

They complement each other: outside you deal with terrain and wind; on a treadmill the pace is steady. A 1% incline brings the effort closer to the outdoors.

Does running on a treadmill help you lose weight?

It uses energy just like running outside; the effect on weight depends on your overall energy balance (see running 30 minutes a day for weight loss).

What speed should a beginner use on a treadmill?

Start at a speed where you can talk, often around 5 to 6 mph (8 to 10 km/h), then adjust to how you feel.
