How to Calculate Your Maximal Aerobic Speed

The short answer

Maximal aerobic speed (MAS, also called vVO2max) is estimated with a field test. In the 6-minute test, divide the distance you covered in meters by 100 to get km/h (or multiply the miles by 10 to get mph). Other tests give close values, never exact ones.

What is maximal aerobic speed?

MAS is the lowest running speed that demands your maximal oxygen uptake (VO2 max). It is expressed in km/h (or mph). It is the base used to set training paces: easy runs, threshold, intervals. For the difference with VO2 max, read VO2 max vs maximal aerobic speed.

The most common field tests

The 6-minute test (half-Cooper)

You run as far as possible in 6 minutes. The rule of thumb is: MAS (km/h) = distance in meters / 100. Example: 1,500 m (0.93 mile) in 6 minutes gives 15 km/h (9.3 mph). In miles, six minutes is a tenth of an hour, so MAS (mph) = miles covered x 10.

This rule is very common among coaches, but we found no validity study for it: treat the result as an estimate. The test is detailed step by step in How to do a 6-minute run test.

The Cooper test (12 minutes)

Published by Kenneth Cooper in 1968 (JAMA), it consists of running for 12 minutes. A frequently quoted formula estimates VO2 max: (distance in m - 504.9) / 44.73. Example: 2,400 m (1.49 miles) gives about 42.4 ml/kg/min.

The original test was done on US military personnel; the formula is less reliable for occasional athletes. Over 12 minutes, the hard part is pacing your effort evenly.

The Léger-Boucher test (Université de Montréal)

Published in 1980, it is a progressive test on a 400 m track, with markers every 25 m. You start at 6 km/h (3.7 mph) and the speed goes up by 1 km/h every 2 minutes, following audio signals. Your MAS is the speed of the last stage you complete. It is more precise than a free timed test because the pace is imposed.

The 20 m shuttle run (beep test)

An indoor variant (Léger et al., 1988): you run back and forth over 20 m to the rhythm of a beep that speeds up. Handy in winter, but the turns lower the speed you measure compared with a straight line.

How accurate is it?

  • A field test gives an estimate, often within about 1 km/h (0.6 mph) depending on the runner and the day.
  • The result depends on motivation, wind, fatigue and your ability to pace the effort.
  • Only a laboratory test truly measures VO2 max.
  • Repeat the test every 6 to 8 weeks, in the same conditions, to track your progress.

What to do with the value

Enter it in our MAS calculator, then in the training paces tool. The percentages of MAS used for each type of workout vary by author: take them as reference points, not laws.

A maximal test is an intense effort. If you are returning to exercise after a long break or have a medical history, ask a doctor before doing it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between MAS and VO2 max?

MAS is a speed (km/h or mph); VO2 max is an oxygen uptake (ml/kg/min). You can go from one to the other with the approximation VO2 max = 3.5 x MAS (in km/h), valid roughly between 8 and 25 km/h (5 to 15.5 mph).

Is the 6-minute test reliable?

It is a practical estimate, not a laboratory measurement. It assumes you pace your effort well over 6 minutes.

How long can you hold your MAS?

A study by Billat et al. (2000) measured an average time to exhaustion of 5 min 47 s at 100% of the speed associated with VO2 max, with large differences between runners.

How often should I repeat the test?

About every 6 to 8 weeks, in the same conditions (track, time of day, rest), so the results are comparable.
