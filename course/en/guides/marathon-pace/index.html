What Pace Should You Run Your Marathon At?

The short answer

Marathon pace is your goal time divided by 42.195 km (26.2 miles): 4 hours gives 9:09 /mile (5:41 /km). To choose that time, rely on your recent races and your training, and be careful with predictions from a 10K.

From goal time to pace

Pace (min/mile) = goal time / 26.2188; pace (min/km) = goal time / 42.195. Here are reference points for an even pace:

How do you choose your goal time?

From a recent race time

The Riegel formula predicts a marathon time from a 10K or a half (see the guide). But a study of recreational runners shows it is often optimistic at the marathon: allow a margin. A recent half, run after real preparation, is a better starting point than a 5K.

From MAS

A frequently quoted range puts marathon pace at about 75 to 85% of MAS, depending on level and the length of the effort; coaches do not agree with each other, and the longer you run, the lower the percentage. For reference, the half marathon is often given at about 80-85% of MAS.

Example: MAS 15 km/h, 78% gives 11.7 km/h (7.3 mph), or 8:15 /mile (5:08 /km). Check with the training paces.

From the training you have done

Your realistic pace depends on your miles per week and your long runs. If you have never run more than 2 hours in one go, be cautious.

A concrete example

A runner clocks 50:00 for 10K, which gives 3:50 by the Riegel formula. But their longest runs last 1 h 30 and they run 30 km (about 19 miles) a week. In that case, a goal of 4:05 to 4:15, about 9:20 to 9:44 /mile (5:48 to 6:03 /km), is more reasonable for a first marathon.

It is not a rule: it is a way to remember that preparation counts as much as a 10K time.

Running your race

  • Start at goal pace or slightly slower: a start that is too fast is paid for in the last six miles.
  • Keep an even pace, even if it means speeding up in the last miles when you have something left.
  • Practice drinking and eating in training before doing it on race day.

On race day, heat, wind and hills change the pace that is possible. If you have any doubt about your health, ask a doctor before signing up for a marathon.

Frequently asked questions

What pace do I need to finish a marathon in 4 hours?

9:09 /mile on average (5:41 /km), or about 6.6 mph (10.5 km/h).

What percentage of MAS is marathon pace?

75 to 85% is often quoted, depending on level and author. It is a reference point, to confirm in training.

Can I predict my marathon from a 10K?

You can, with the Riegel formula, but it is often optimistic. Add a margin and take your preparation into account.

Should I start slower than goal pace?

Many runners gain from starting slightly slower, to avoid running out of energy before mile 18.
