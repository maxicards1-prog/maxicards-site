Why Do My Shins Hurt When Running?

The short answer

Pain along the front or inner edge of the shin when running is often medial tibial stress syndrome, commonly called shin splints. It tends to come from a load that is too high or rose too fast for the leg; only a health professional can say whether that is what you have.

What it is, in general

Medial tibial stress syndrome (MTSS), also called shin splints or "tibial periostitis" in French, is pain along the inner edge of the shinbone that appears with exercise. It is common in runners and military recruits.

Other problems can cause pain in the same spot, for example a stress fracture: a simple self-diagnosis is not enough.

Known risk factors

The critical review published in Sports Medicine (2009) and the meta-analysis by Hamstra-Wright et al. (2015, British Journal of Sports Medicine) study the risk factors. The most often cited are female sex, excessive foot pronation and a history of the same pain.

Several tested factors (such as ankle dorsiflexion) were not retained. Researchers are still calling for better-quality studies: knowledge remains limited.

Training load: the most practical point

A fast rise in volume or intensity is a frequently suggested explanation for running injuries. In the study by Nielsen et al. (2014), runners who raised their distance by more than 30% a week had more injuries, though the difference was not statistically conclusive.

No threshold has been demonstrated, but caution remains useful: increase in small steps (see how many miles per week).

What many runners do while waiting for advice

  • Cut back or stop the runs that hurt, then restart gradually.
  • Choose low-impact activities (cycling, swimming, walking) while the pain gets in the way.
  • Review your progression: too much distance or too many hard sessions at once.
  • Check the state of your shoes (see how to choose running shoes).

When to see someone

See a doctor or physiotherapist if the pain persists, comes back every run, appears at night or at rest, or if one precise spot on the shin is very sore to the touch. Returning after an injury should be planned with a professional; see also how to start running again.

This guide is general and is not a diagnosis. It does not give treatment.

Sources: "Medial tibial stress syndrome: a critical review", Sports Med (2009); Hamstra-Wright et al. (2015), Br J Sports Med; Nielsen et al. (2014), J Orthop Sports Phys Ther (accessed 24/11/2026).

Frequently asked questions

Why do my legs hurt when I run?

Leg pain when running can be normal muscle fatigue or overload; if it is sharp, persistent or localized, get it checked.

What should I do about shin pain when running?

Reduce the load, avoid pushing through pain, and see a doctor or physiotherapist if it persists or returns on every run.

Is pain at the front of the shin when running serious?

Not necessarily, but only an examination can rule out a problem such as a stress fracture.

When can I run again after shin pain?

When it has gone and with a health professional's agreement, restarting gradually.
