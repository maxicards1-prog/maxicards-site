How to Calculate Your Maximum Heart Rate

The short answer

The most reliable formula for estimating max heart rate is Tanaka's (2001): 208 - 0.7 x age. The old 220 - age rule is simpler but less accurate. With either, the individual gap can reach 10 to 12 beats per minute.

The two formulas to know

Max heart rate is the highest number of beats per minute your heart reaches at maximal effort. It decreases with age and varies a lot from one person to another.

  • Fox (1971): 220 - age. Very widespread and simple, but built without a solid statistical foundation and known to underestimate the max heart rate of people over 40.
  • Tanaka, Monahan and Seals (2001): 208 - 0.7 x age. From a meta-analysis of 351 studies (18,712 subjects), validated on 514 people, for healthy men and women (PubMed).

Why it is only an estimate

Even with Tanaka's formula, the standard error is about 10 beats per minute. Scientific reviews note a variability of about 12 beats between people of the same age. Your max heart rate can therefore be clearly higher or lower than the result, without saying anything about your fitness.

It does not improve with training: a very well-trained runner does not necessarily have a higher max heart rate.

Measuring your max heart rate in the field

The most reliable measure remains a maximal effort with a chest strap: a long warm-up, several progressive minutes, then an all-out finish (for example a hill). You record the highest value. It is a very intense effort, only to consider if you are in good health and used to hard exercise.

If you are returning to exercise after a long break, if you are over 40 and sedentary, or if you have a personal or family history of heart problems, ask a doctor before any maximal test.

Using your max heart rate

Once you have a value (calculated or measured), you can split your zones with our heart rate zones tool and read the explanation of the 5 zones. Another method, Karvonen's, also uses resting heart rate: it is covered in that same zones guide.

For your paces, heart rate is only one reference among others: it varies with heat, fatigue, caffeine and sleep. Cross-check it with perceived effort and with your maximal aerobic speed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the max heart rate of a 40-year-old man?

With Tanaka, 208 - 0.7 x 40 = 180 bpm. With 220 - age, it is also 180. Your real value can differ by about 10 bpm.

Does max heart rate go down with training?

No, it depends mostly on age and genetics. It is resting heart rate that generally goes down with training.

Should I use 220 - age or Tanaka?

Tanaka was built on a much larger sample and corrects the bias of 220 - age in people over 40. Neither replaces a measurement.

Can a watch give me my max heart rate?

It can record the highest value of a workout. Wrist sensors are less reliable at high intensity than a chest strap.
