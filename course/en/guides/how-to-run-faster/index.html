How to Run Faster

The short answer

To run faster, run regularly, keep most sessions at an easy pace and add one or two harder sessions a week. The endurance athletes studied spend on average about 80% of their sessions at low intensity and 20% at high intensity.

1. Run regularly

Lasting progress comes first from consistency: several sessions a week for months beat an isolated effort. Set a realistic volume with the guide how many miles per week.

2. Keep most sessions easy

The review by Seiler (2010, International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance) describes top endurance athletes who spend about 80% of their sessions at low intensity and about 20% at high intensity.

In already well-trained athletes, adding still more hard sessions brought no convincing gain according to the same review. For an amateur runner this means: do not run every outing flat out. See easy-run pace.

3. Add a little hard work

One interval session a week, or one session at threshold, trains the body to run faster. Work out paces with the training pace calculator, after measuring your maximal aerobic speed with the MAS calculator. The percentages of MAS per session type are guidelines that vary by author.

4. Work gently on strength and stride

Research on strength training and running technique is nuanced and beyond this guide; the point to remember is not to change your whole stride at once. On cadence, read should you aim for 180 steps per minute?.

5. Sleep and recover

Progress happens during recovery. An occasional lighter week and enough sleep help you string weeks together without injury.

Run faster in one day?

No: no trick replaces weeks of training. On race day, the best "trick" is to start at the right pace and warm up well (see the warm-up). To see what you can aim for, try the race time predictor.

Returning from an injury, illness or persistent pain calls for a doctor's advice before adding intensity.

Source: Seiler (2010), Int J Sports Physiol Perform (accessed 24/11/2026).

Frequently asked questions

How can I run faster without getting tired?

Keep most sessions at an easy pace and raise the load gradually: fatigue comes from hard effort too often.

How can I run faster and longer?

Build duration at an easy pace first, then add one faster session a week.

How can I run a faster 10K?

With regular training, one interval or threshold session and a well-chosen starting pace; see the guide to improving your 10K time.

Can I run faster in one day?

There is no validated trick: progress takes weeks of regular work.
