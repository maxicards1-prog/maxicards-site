What Are the 5 Heart Rate Zones For?

The short answer

Heart rate zones split your heart rate into 5 ranges, from very easy to maximal effort. Each targets a different quality; the exact percentages vary by author, but the logic stays the same.

Heart rate zones

A common 5-zone model

Here is a widely used split, as a percentage of max heart rate. Other sources put the limits a few points higher or lower: keep the logic, not the decimal.

Zone% max HRFeelPurpose
150 to 60%Very easyRecovery, brisk walking
260 to 70%Easy, you can talk without difficultyBase endurance
370 to 80%Steady, short sentencesMarathon pace and tempo
480 to 90%HardThreshold, 10K to half marathon pace
590 to 100%Very hardShort intervals, MAS

Two ways to calculate your zones

As a percentage of max heart rate

You take your max heart rate (measured or estimated, see how to calculate it) and apply the percentages. With 180 bpm, zone 2 runs from 108 to 126 bpm.

With the Karvonen method (1957)

The formula is: target HR = resting HR + % x (max HR - resting HR). It takes your resting heart rate into account, which personalizes the zones. Example: max HR 180, resting HR 60. At 70%: 60 + 0.70 x (180 - 60) = 144 bpm.

The method is based on the work of Karvonen et al. (Ann Med Exp Biol Fenn, 1957); its popularity comes from being simple to apply.

How to split your time between zones

In well-trained junior cross-country skiers, Seiler and Kjerland (2006) observed about 75% of the time in zone 1 (of their own 3-zone model), little time at threshold and about 17% in the high zone. Later reviews describe a split close to 80/20 among elite athletes.

These are observations, not proof that everyone should do the same: but they remind us that most training should stay easy. For zone 2, see easy run pace.

Limits of heart rate

  • It rises with heat, fatigue, lack of sleep and dehydration.
  • It reacts with a delay: in short intervals, it does not reflect the instant effort.
  • Optical wrist sensors are less reliable than a chest strap.

If you have palpitations, chest pain or feel unwell during exercise, stop and see a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

Which zone should I run in to lose weight?

Zones 2 and 3 let you run for a long time, which raises total energy expenditure. No zone burns fat in a magic way: the weekly balance matters more.

Do I really need to run in zone 2?

Most of a runner's sessions benefit from staying easy, as Seiler's observations show. Feel (being able to talk normally) is a good check.

Is the Karvonen method better?

It personalizes the zones thanks to resting heart rate. The two methods give more or less similar zones; consistency over time is what matters.

My heart rate jumps early in a workout. Is that normal?

Yes, especially when cold or in hot weather. Give it 10 minutes before judging your zone.
