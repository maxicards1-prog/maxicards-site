How to Start Running

The short answer

Begin by alternating walking and slow running three times a week, at a pace that lets you talk. The goal of the first month is not performance: it is building the habit without getting hurt.

How to use it

  1. Pick three fixed time slots. Three sessions a week, ideally separated by a rest day, are plenty to start. Put them in your calendar like appointments.
  2. Alternate walking and running. Run for one to two minutes, walk for one to two minutes, and repeat for 20 to 30 minutes. If that is too hard, walk for longer.
  3. Stay in breathing comfort. You should be able to say a full sentence without gasping. If you cannot, slow down, even to something close to a brisk walk.
  4. Progress very gradually. Make the running intervals a little longer from one week to the next, but do not increase total time and intensity at the same time.
  5. Finish with a cool-down. Walk for a few minutes at the end, drink some water, and note how you felt: sensations matter more than the stopwatch.

Why start slowly

Your heart and lungs adapt quickly. Tendons, bones and joints adapt more slowly. Many beginners feel "fit" after two outings and do too much: that is the classic situation in which aches and injuries appear. Running less than you could is exactly what lets you keep going.

What pace to choose

Ignore the stopwatch at first. A suitable pace is one where you could chat with a friend. If you still want a reference, the pace calculator gives you your speed after the session (in min/km and min/mile), and the pace-speed converter helps you compare with a treadmill display in mph or km/h.

Later, your easy-run paces can be calculated from your VMA (maximal aerobic speed).

Progress without getting injured

A Danish study (Nielsen et al., 2014) followed 874 novice runners wearing GPS watches. Those who increased their distance by more than 30% in two weeks had more distance-related injuries than those who increased it by less than 10%.

That does not prove a magic threshold exists, but it supports a simple idea: do not increase everything at once. To go further, read how many miles or kilometers to run per week.

Shoes and gear

You do not need expensive gear for the first month: a comfortable pair of shoes, clothes suited to the weather, and water. When you are ready to buy, see our guide on choosing running shoes.

Your health comes first

If you are over 40, have a heart or lung condition, take medication, are significantly overweight, or are returning after a long break, talk to your doctor first. For a timed race, check with the organizer: a medical form or health questionnaire may be required.

Pain that persists, dizziness or chest discomfort mean you should stop and get medical advice.

What comes next

When you can run 30 minutes continuously, move on to our method for running 30 minutes without stopping, then aim for your first 5K (3.1 miles). You can also follow the beginner 5K plan.

Frequently asked questions

How many times a week should a beginner run?

Three short sessions a week, with at least one rest day between them, are a common and reasonable base for a beginner. Two sessions are also fine if your schedule is busy.

Should beginners walk during their runs?

Yes, it is even recommended. Alternating walking and running lets you last longer without overloading your joints, and helps you progress without getting discouraged.

What is the best time of day to run?

The one you will stick with. Morning, noon or evening all work: what matters is consistency and a light enough meal before you head out.

Can beginners run every day?

That is not recommended. Rest is when your body adapts. Start with one rest day between two sessions.
