How Long Should Your Long Run Be?

The short answer

The long run is the longest workout of the week, run at an easy pace, once a week. A very common coaching rule of thumb is that it should not exceed about 25 to 30% of your weekly mileage.

What is it for?

The long run trains your body to keep running for a long time: muscular endurance, energy management, mental confidence. It is mainly useful to prepare for a half marathon or marathon, but it also has a place for a 10K.

How long?

There is no "scientific" duration. Common reference points:

  • 5K goal: 40 to 60 minutes.
  • 10K goal: 60 to 90 minutes.
  • Half marathon goal: 1 h 30 to 2 h.
  • Marathon goal: about 2 h to 3 h, depending on level.

These ranges are common coaching practices, not validated standards. A slower runner reaches the same duration with fewer miles: think mostly in time, for example with the race time calculator.

What pace?

Easy: the pace of an easy run, about 60 to 75% of MAS (a range that varies by author). You should be able to talk. Some marathon preparation runs include blocks at goal pace, but that concerns experienced runners (see marathon pace).

The 25 to 30% rule

A well-known piece of advice: the long run should not exceed 25 to 30% of the week's volume, otherwise a single effort concentrates too much load. For low volumes (under 30 km, about 19 miles, a week), it often represents more than 30%, which is acceptable if your other runs stay easy.

It is a field rule of thumb, not a study result.

Progressing without getting hurt

The famous "10% rule" (do not increase by more than 10% per week) is not confirmed by research: in the trial by Buist et al. (2008) with novices, a graded 13-week program did not reduce injuries (20.8% versus 20.3% in the standard program).

Nielsen et al. (2014) suggest avoiding increases above 30% from one week to the next, without a clear statistical result. More details in how many miles per week.

Before and after

  • Eat normally the day before and drink regularly.
  • Carry water if the run goes beyond an hour and a half, especially in the heat.
  • Plan an easy day afterward.

With persistent pain, or when returning from an injury, ask a doctor before lengthening your runs.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a long run every week?

Once a week is classic when preparing for a half or a marathon. An easier week every 3 or 4 weeks is common.

Can you walk during the long run?

Yes. Including walking breaks is common, especially for beginners or over very long durations.

How long should my longest run be before a marathon?

There is no scientific consensus. Many plans go up to 2 h 30 to 3 h, or to 30-35 km (19 to 22 miles) for the most experienced.

Should I do the long run fasted?

It is not necessary. Eat what you tolerate and test your pre-race eating in training.
