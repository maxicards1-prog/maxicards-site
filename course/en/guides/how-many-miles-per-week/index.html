How Many Miles Should You Run per Week?

The short answer

It depends on your goal and level: about 6 to 12 miles (10 to 20 km) for a first 5K, 12 to 25 miles for a 10K, 19 to 31 miles for a half marathon and 25 to 43 miles for a marathon. These are common reference points, not standards.

Reference points, not rules

There is no weekly mileage validated by a study for each goal. The values below are common coaching practices for recreational runners; they depend on your experience, your age and the time you have.

GoalWeekly referenceRuns per week
5K (beginner)6 to 12 miles (10 to 20 km)2 to 3
10K12 to 25 miles (20 to 40 km)3 to 4
Half marathon19 to 31 miles (30 to 50 km)3 to 5
Marathon25 to 43 miles (40 to 70 km)4 to 5

Time spent matters as much as distance: a slower runner can run fewer miles for the same duration. Convert easily with the race time calculator.

How do you build up without getting hurt?

The most quoted rule is the 10% rule: do not increase by more than 10% per week. It is handy, but poorly validated:

  • Buist et al. (2008): in novices, a graded 13-week program, based on this rule, did not reduce injuries (20.8% versus 20.3% for a standard 8-week program).
  • Nielsen et al. (2014): an increase of more than 30% in mileage from one week to the next was associated with more overuse injuries (hazard ratio 1.59), but the result was not statistically clear.

Without certainty, it is better to build in steps of 2 to 3 weeks, with a lighter week regularly, and keep your runs easy (easy run pace).

Spreading the volume

  • One long run not exceeding about 25 to 30% of the total, if your volume allows it.
  • A few easy runs, 1 or 2 intense workouts at most (intervals, tempo runs).
  • At least one complete rest day.

Warning signs

Pain that lasts more than a few days, that gets worse with effort or that changes your stride should lead you to ease off and see a professional. Lasting fatigue, disturbed sleep and a rising resting heart rate are also signs that you need to cut back.

Frequently asked questions

How many miles per week for a 10K?

Many runners prepare for a 10K with 12 to 25 miles (20 to 40 km) per week, but the figure varies by level.

How many miles per week for a marathon?

Common plans call for 25 to 43 miles (40 to 70 km), with progressive long runs. There is no official threshold.

Is the 10% rule reliable?

It is handy but not proven: a randomized trial of novices did not show fewer injuries with a graded program.

Can I improve by running less?

Yes, with well-chosen workouts. Consistency matters more than volume for a first goal.

Should I run every day?

No: rest is part of training, especially for beginners. Two to four runs a week are often enough.
