How Long Does It Take to Run 10K?

The short answer

The time to run 10K (6.2 miles) is simply 10 times your pace per kilometer: at 6 min/km (9:39 per mile) it takes 1 hour; at 5 min/km (8:03 per mile), 50 minutes. There is no "normal" time; it depends on your level, your training and the course.

Running 10K in 1 hour or 50 minutes

To run 10K in 1 hour, you need to hold 6 min/km (10 km/h, 9:39 per mile). For 50 minutes, you need 5 min/km (12 km/h, 8:03 per mile). These paces are constant: the key is to start at the right pace rather than too fast. To improve, read how to improve your 10K time.

Predict your time from another race

If you have already run a 5K, the Riegel formula (1981) estimates your 10K time. Enter your time in the race time predictor. It is an estimate: it assumes training suited to the distance. For the principle, see the Riegel formula.

What if I run 10K once a week?

A single weekly run is possible for an occasional runner, but to improve and limit fatigue the effort is usually spread over several sessions. The 8-week 10K plan on this site gives a framework.

If you have not run for a long time, or have a heart condition or pain, start by asking a doctor and by reading the guide to running a 5K.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to run 10K?

At 6 min/km (9:39 per mile) it takes 1 hour; at 5 min/km, 50 minutes; at 7 min/km, 1 h 10. The time depends on your pace.

How can I run 10K in 1 hour?

Hold 6 min/km, which is 10 km/h (6.2 mph), from start to finish without starting faster. A gradual training plan helps you get there.

How can I run 10K in 50 minutes?

You need to hold 5 min/km, or 12 km/h (7.5 mph). That takes regular training; see the guide to improving your 10K time.

Is running 10K once a week enough?

It is possible to maintain fitness, but to improve it is better to spread training across several sessions a week.
