How to Choose Running Shoes

The short answer

Choose the pair you find most comfortable while running. According to Nigg et al. (2015), perceived comfort may be a better guide than technology promises, although that hypothesis remains debated. No brand is superior for everyone.

How to use it

  1. Define your use. Road, trail, track; easy runs, intervals or long races. One pair can suit several uses.
  2. Try them on at the end of the day. Feet swell slightly. Leave a little room in front of the big toe.
  3. Actually run in them. Jog a few minutes in place or in the store if possible. No pain or pressure point should appear.
  4. Pick the most comfortable. If two pairs fit, take the one you prefer, not the most expensive.
  5. Watch for wear. Replace the pair when the cushioning feels dead or the sole is unevenly worn.

What the research shows

Nigg, Baltich, Hoerzer and Enders (2015, British Journal of Sports Medicine) proposed an idea: the runner spontaneously selects the most comfortable shoe, which keeps them on their preferred movement path and could reduce injury risk.

This is called the "comfort filter." The hypothesis has support, but later studies, notably on treadmills, did not confirm the paradigm. In other words: comfort is a serious criterion, without definitive scientific guarantee.

What is not demonstrated

Claims about pronation, stride correction or "perfect" cushioning do not rest on solid general evidence. We therefore recommend no brand and no model, and this site earns no commission on shoes.

The criteria that really matter

  • Fit: suitable volume, heel held in place, toes free.
  • Weight and flexibility: more flexibility and lightness for speed; more cushioning for long runs, according to your preferences.
  • Surface: lugs for muddy trails, a smoother sole for the road.
  • Budget: a high price does not guarantee better comfort.

When to replace a pair

There is no single proven mileage, because lifespan depends on your weight, stride and terrain. Signs of wear: smooth sole, flattened cushioning, new discomfort. Rotating two pairs is a common practice, but without clear proof of an effect on injuries.

Race day

Run in a pair you have already worn several times, never brand new. It is common-sense advice to avoid blisters. See also how to start running, marathon training and the warm-up.

If you are in pain

Repeated pain in the feet, knees or shins is not always solved by changing shoes. See a sports medicine doctor or a podiatrist.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose running shoes as a beginner?

Take the most comfortable pair in a running trial, in the right size, suited to your terrain. Avoid paying for unproven technical claims.

Should I buy a size up for running?

Often a little room in front of the toes is useful, because the foot swells. Try them on at the end of the day and check that no area rubs.

How many miles before I replace my running shoes?

No universal figure is proven. Replace them when the cushioning is flattened, the sole worn or the discomfort new.

Are expensive shoes better?

Not necessarily. Comfort and fit come first, and a pricier pair has not been shown to reduce injuries.
