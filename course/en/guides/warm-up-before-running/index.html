How to Warm Up Before Running

The short answer

A 10-minute routine is enough: walking or a very slow jog, dynamic mobility, then a few progressive strides. Static stretching before running does not reduce injury risk according to the available reviews.

How to use it

  1. Walk or jog for 3 to 5 minutes. At a very easy pace, to raise temperature and heart rate gradually.
  2. Do dynamic mobility. Leg swings, high knees, butt kicks, walking lunges: about 30 seconds per exercise, without forcing range of motion.
  3. Add simple drills. A few bounding strides or knee lifts over about 22 to 33 yards (20 to 30 m) to wake up your rhythm.
  4. Finish with 2 to 4 strides. Over about 87 to 109 yards (80 to 100 m), gradually building speed without sprinting, with a walk recovery.
  5. Start your session. Begin slowly: the first mile also counts as warm-up.

What is a warm-up for?

It raises muscle temperature, speeds up circulation and prepares the nervous system. Its main purpose is to start better, especially before an intense session or a race. For a very easy jog, the start of the session plays the role of warm-up: run the first ten minutes more slowly.

When the warm-up should be longer

The more intense the planned effort, the more careful the warm-up should be. Before intervals or a 5K or 10K race, add strides. Session paces are detailed in training paces as a percentage of VMA and in the intervals guide. For a long race, a short warm-up is enough: save your energy for the distance.

What about stretching?

The question is much debated. A literature review of a single static stretch before running observes a possible negative effect on performance. For dynamic stretching, results are mixed: no clear effect on running economy, sometimes a small performance benefit.

On injuries, the available syntheses conclude that stretching done before exercise does not reduce injury risk. A warm-up is still useful for other reasons, such as the feeling of ready legs. If you like to stretch, do it after the session, without pain.

A simple routine to remember

  1. Walk or slow jog, 3 to 5 minutes.
  2. Dynamic mobility, 3 to 5 minutes.
  3. 2 to 4 progressive strides before a fast session.

Practical tips

  • Adapt the length to the weather: longer in cold conditions.
  • Do not seek exhaustion: the warm-up should leave you fresh.
  • Keep the same routine before every important race, for example your 10K.

Health

If a movement triggers sharp pain, stop. Pain that does not ease with the warm-up should be assessed by a doctor or physical therapist.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a good warm-up before running last?

About ten minutes is enough for most sessions: walking or easy jogging, dynamic mobility, then a few strides if the session is fast.

Should I stretch before running?

The available reviews do not show fewer injuries with stretching before exercise, and long static stretches may hurt performance. Favor dynamic mobility.

Do I need to warm up before an easy jog?

Not necessarily: simply start very slowly for the first 5 to 10 minutes.

How do I warm up before a race?

Do a progressive 10 to 20 minute warm-up with a few strides, and finish shortly before the start. Do not wear yourself out.
