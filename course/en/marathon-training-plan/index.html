Marathon training plan, 12 weeks

Four workouts a week for twelve weeks for a runner with a good base: long runs, marathon pace work and a taper.

How to use it

  1. Check the requirements: 3 to 4 workouts a week for several months, a 1:30 run without difficulty, ideally a half marathon already run.
  2. Work out your MAS and enter it above to show the paces of each workout.
  3. Do the 4 workouts of each week, protecting the long run and the rest days.
  4. Respect the easier weeks 4 and 8 and the taper of the last 2 weeks.

Important: This plan gives general guidelines: it is not medical advice and it does not adapt to your personal situation. See a doctor before starting or resuming exercise, and stop if you feel pain, faintness or any unusual symptom.

Goal: finish a marathon (42.195 km, 26.2 miles) in good shape. Length: 12 weeks, 4 workouts per week. Who it is for: Already running 3 to 4 times a week for several months, doing a 1 h 30 run comfortably and, preferably, having already run a half marathon.

Otherwise, plan a longer preparation (16 to 20 weeks, as in the classic plans of Higdon, Pfitzinger or Hansons).

The principles of the plan

  • Twelve weeks is a short plan: it assumes a solid base. For a first marathon with no base, it is better to start earlier.
  • The long run is the key workout: it builds up to about 2 h 45 to 3 h, which is 28 to 32 km (17 to 20 miles), with an easier week every 3-4 weeks.
  • The last 2 weeks (taper): volume cut by about half, intensity and frequency kept.
  • This plan promises no finish time. Race pace is decided from a recent half marathon or your MAS, never from how you feel on the day.

The plan, week by week

Week 1 : Building the base

  • Easy run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 15 min warm-up, then 6 × 1 min at 95 to 100% MAS (1 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 1 h 20 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 2 : Building up gently

  • Easy run. 50 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 15 min warm-up, then 5 × 3 min at 88 to 93% MAS (1 min 30 recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 1 h 30 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 3 : Threshold

  • Easy run. 50 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Threshold. 15 min warm-up, then 3 × 10 min at 82 to 88% MAS (2 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 1 h 45 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 4 : Easier week

  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 15 min warm-up, then 6 × 400 m at 95 to 100% MAS (1 min 30 recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 35 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 1 h 15 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 5 : Picking the load back up

  • Easy run. 55 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 15 min warm-up, then 5 × 1,000 m at 90 to 95% MAS (2 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 2 h at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 6 : Marathon pace

  • Easy run. 55 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Marathon pace. 15 min warm-up, then 2 × 20 min at 70 to 80% MAS (marathon pace, 3 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 2 h 15 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 7 : High volume

  • Easy run. 60 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Threshold. 15 min warm-up, then 4 × 8 min at 82 to 88% MAS (2 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 2 h 30 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS, with 30 min at marathon pace 70 to 80% MAS at the end.

Week 8 : Easier week

  • Easy run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 15 min warm-up, then 6 × 3 min at 88 to 93% MAS (1 min 30 recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 1 h 30 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 9 : Peak training

  • Easy run. 60 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Marathon pace. 15 min warm-up, then 3 × 20 min at 70 to 80% MAS (3 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 50 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 2 h 45 to 3 h at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS, with 40 min at marathon pace 70 to 80% MAS at the end.

Week 10 : Recovering before the taper

  • Easy run. 50 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 15 min warm-up, then 5 × 1,000 m at 90 to 95% MAS (2 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 1 h 45 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 11 : Taper 1

  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Marathon pace. 15 min warm-up, then 2 × 15 min at 70 to 80% MAS (3 min recovery), then 10 min cool-down.
  • Easy run. 30 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Long run. 1 h 15 at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 12 : Taper 2 and race

  • Easy run. 30 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS, with 4 livelier 20-second strides.
  • Short quality. 15 min at an easy pace, then 4 × 1 min at 90 to 95% MAS (2 min recovery).
  • Rest or jog. Rest, or 20 very easy minutes the day before.
  • Race: marathon. 42.195 km. Start at 70 to 78% MAS for the first 10 km, then hold your pace: the race begins at 30 km.

What is this plan based on?

The principles (cautious progression, one long run a week, easier weeks, a two-week taper) are those of the classic running plans: Daniels, Pfitzinger, Higdon.

The taper follows the conclusions of the meta-analysis by Bosquet et al. (2007): about two weeks, volume cut by 40 to 60%, intensity and frequency kept. Full references are on the Sources page. This plan is a general model: it does not replace a coach or a doctor's advice.

Twelve weeks: is it enough?

The most common plans (Higdon, Pfitzinger, Hansons) last 16 to 20 weeks. A 12-week plan is a short version, realistic only if you already have the base: several months of regular running and a half marathon. If that is not the case, it is better to start earlier with the half marathon plan.

See also the guide on preparing for a marathon and the marathon pace chart.

Frequently asked questions

How many weeks does it take to prepare for a marathon?

Classic plans last 16 to 20 weeks. A 12-week plan suits only an already solid runner who has run regularly for several months.

What is the longest run?

In this plan, between 2:45 and 3:00, that is about 28 to 32 km (17 to 20 miles) depending on pace. You do not need to run 42 km in training.

What pace for the marathon?

Between 70 and 80% of MAS depending on level; choose it from a recent half marathon, with caution, not from a 10K alone. The marathon pace chart helps you set a time.

What should I eat and drink during long runs?

Long runs are also for testing your fueling and your shoes. For personal advice on nutrition, ask a doctor or a sports dietitian.

Can I adjust the plan to 3 workouts?

Yes, by dropping one of the short easy runs. Always keep the long run and the quality workout.
