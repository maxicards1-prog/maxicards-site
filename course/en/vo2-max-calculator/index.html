Enter the distance you covered during the test: the tool gives your MAS in km/h, the matching pace and an estimate of your VO2 max.

How to use it

  1. Do a test on a track or on flat, measured ground: 6 minutes (half-Cooper) or 12 minutes (Cooper), all out but even.
  2. Choose the test you did in the list.
  3. Enter the distance covered, in meters or miles (number of 400 m laps × 400 m, plus the remainder).
  4. Read your MAS, your pace at 100% of MAS and your estimated VO2 max, then go to the training paces.

Formula: half-Cooper: MAS (km/h) = distance in 6 min (m) ÷ 100. Cooper: MAS (km/h) ≈ distance in 12 min (m) ÷ 200; VO2 max (ml/kg/min) = (distance in m − 504.9) ÷ 44.73 (Cooper, 1968). VO2 max ≈ 3.5 × MAS (rule of thumb). Detailed sources on the Sources page.

What is MAS?

MAS, or maximal aerobic speed (also called vVO2max), is the lowest running speed at which your oxygen uptake reaches its maximum. In practice, it is the speed you can hold for about 5 to 7 minutes of very hard running. It is the base used to set all your training paces as percentages.

The term is common in French-speaking countries and in Australia and the UK; in the US the same idea is usually discussed through VO2 max and race-based paces. The VO2 max (in ml/kg/min) is the oxygen uptake; MAS is the running speed that goes with it.

Why divide by 100 or by 200?

Six minutes is a tenth of an hour: the distance covered in 6 minutes, multiplied by 10, is the distance you would cover in one hour at the same speed. Dividing the distance in meters by 100 therefore gives your average speed in km/h.

Twelve minutes is a fifth of an hour: the distance in meters divided by 200 is likewise your average speed over 12 minutes, which French practice takes as MAS. In both cases the average speed of the test is treated as MAS: it is an approximation.

Accuracy and precautions

A badly paced test (starting too fast, uneven effort) underestimates MAS. A test sheet from a French military health school gives a margin of about ± 1 km/h for the half-Cooper.

The calculated VO2 max is only an order of magnitude: Cooper's 1968 study of 115 US Air Force men found a correlation of 0.90 with the laboratory measurement, but the formula suits untrained people poorly. In miles, the same formula is about 35.98 × distance in miles − 11.29.

This test is a maximal effort: do not do it without a 15-minute warm-up, and ask a doctor first if you have any doubt about your health.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate MAS with the half-Cooper test?

Run as far as you can for 6 minutes on flat ground, measure the distance in meters and divide by 100. 1,500 m in 6 minutes gives an MAS of 15 km/h.

What is the formula of the 12-minute Cooper test?

In French practice, MAS is the distance covered in 12 minutes (in meters) divided by 200. Cooper also published a VO2 max formula in 1968: (distance in m − 504.9) ÷ 44.73.

What is the difference between VO2 max and MAS?

VO2 max is the maximal oxygen uptake, in ml/kg/min; MAS is the matching running speed, in km/h. A rule of thumb links them: VO2 max ≈ 3.5 × MAS.

Can I do the test on a treadmill?

Yes, but the distance has to be read from the treadmill, and the results are not strictly identical to those of a track. Many runners add a 1% incline to get closer to outdoor running.

Are these results 100% accurate?

No: they are estimates based on published formulas. They give a good order of magnitude to organize your training, but your form on the day, the terrain, the weather and your experience can change the real result. Use them as a guide, not as a promise.
