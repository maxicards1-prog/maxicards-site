Calculate your heart rate zones

  • Calculated in your browser
  • Free, no limits
  • No sign-up
  • No data sent

Enter your age (and, if you know them, your measured max HR and resting HR): you get your 5 zones in beats per minute.

How to use it

  1. Enter your age. If you have already measured your max HR in a maximal effort, enter it: it replaces the formula.
  2. For the Karvonen method, add your resting heart rate, measured on waking, lying down, before you get up.
  3. Read your 5 zones: each gives a range in beats per minute and its use.
  4. Adjust by feel: a watch or a chest strap gives guidelines, not orders.

Formula: estimated max HR = 208 − 0.7 × age (Tanaka, Monahan and Seals, 2001). The 220 − age formula is older and less accurate. Karvonen method (1957): target HR = (max HR − resting HR) × % intensity + resting HR. See Sources.

Which formula for max heart rate?

The Tanaka formula comes from a meta-analysis of 351 studies (18,712 people) validated on a group of 514 adults, and it was found to be more accurate than 220 − age, which underestimates the max HR of older people.

Even so, the standard error of the estimate is about 10 beats per minute: two people of the same age can have very different max heart rates. Only a measurement during a maximal effort gives the true value.

The 5 zones

  • Zone 1 (50-60%): very easy, warm-up and recovery.
  • Zone 2 (60-70%): easy aerobic running, you can talk.
  • Zone 3 (70-80%): steady pace, close to marathon pace.
  • Zone 4 (80-90%): threshold, hard effort.
  • Zone 5 (90-100%): maximal effort, in short intervals.

This split into 5 zones of 10% is a widespread convention (watches, apps), not a law of physiology: other models exist.

Precautions

Heart rate varies with heat, fatigue, caffeine, stress or dehydration, and it rises slowly at the start of an effort. An estimated max HR has no medical value.

If you have heart disease, take a medicine that acts on the heart, or feel palpitations, chest pain or faintness during exercise, see a doctor before running and do not rely on these calculations. To calculate paces rather than zones, see the training paces from MAS.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my maximum heart rate?

The Tanaka formula gives max HR = 208 − 0.7 × age. At 40: 208 − 28 = 180 beats per minute. The classic 220 − age formula would also give 180; the gap grows with age.

What is the difference between % of max HR zones and Karvonen?

Zones as a % of max HR are calculated directly on max HR. The Karvonen method applies the percentage to the heart rate reserve (max HR − resting HR) and then adds the resting HR: it takes your fitness into account.

How do I measure my resting heart rate?

On waking, lying down, after a few calm minutes, count your pulse for 30 seconds (×2) or use a heart rate watch. Take the average of several mornings.

In which zone should I run to lose weight or improve?

Most runs are done in zones 1 and 2, as easy running. Harder work (zones 4 and 5) is limited to one or two workouts a week. This site does not give medical advice or diets.

Are these results 100% accurate?

No: they are estimates based on published formulas. They give a good order of magnitude to organize your training, but your form on the day, the terrain, the weather and your experience can change the real result. Use them as a guide, not as a promise.
