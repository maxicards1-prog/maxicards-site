Marathon pace chart

What pace should you hold to finish a marathon in 3:30, 4:00 or 4:30? The chart gives the pace, the speed and the key splits.

How to use it

  1. Find your target time in the first column of the table.
  2. Read the pace to hold in minutes per kilometer and per mile, and the speed in km/h.
  3. Note the splits: they let you check that you are running evenly during the race.
  4. For a time that is not in the table, use the pace calculator.

A marathon is exactly 42.195 km (26.2 miles). Each line of the chart gives the pace of a race run at an even pace, and the splits at the checkpoints most runners watch.

Pace is the marathon

In a marathon, a gap of 5 seconds per kilometer means 3 minutes 31 seconds at the finish. A pace that is slightly too fast at the start is paid for very dearly after the 30th km, when carbohydrate stores run low: many runners talk about "hitting the wall" between the 30th and the 35th km.

Hence the classic advice: start at your goal pace, or even a little slower, and let the race begin around the 30th km.

Checkpoints to watch

  • 10K: check that you did not start too fast.
  • Half (21.0975 km): this split should be a little slower than half of your goal time.
  • 30K: the race really starts; keep your pace.
  • 40K: 2.195 km remain, about ten minutes for most runners.

Choosing a realistic time

Choose your goal time from a recent result and specific training. Use the race time predictor with care: beyond the half marathon, it is often optimistic. Marathon pace is generally 70 to 80% of MAS (see the paces).

To prepare, follow the 12-week marathon plan if you already have a good base, and check the half marathon chart to compare your times.

Frequently asked questions

What pace for a 4-hour marathon?

You need to hold about 5:41 min/km (9:09 min/mile), or 10.5 km/h. The half marathon split is 2:00 if the pace is perfectly even.

What pace for a 3:30 marathon?

About 4:59 min/km (8:01 min/mile), or 12.1 km/h. The half is reached in 1:45 if the pace is perfectly even.

Should I run an even pace in a marathon?

An even pace is the most widely used reference. Some experienced runners run the second half a little faster (a negative split), which is harder to achieve than to say.

How many weeks does it take to prepare for a marathon?

Classic plans last 16 to 20 weeks. A 12-week plan, like the one on this site, assumes that you already run regularly and have already run a half marathon.
