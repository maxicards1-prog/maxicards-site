Calculate your race time

  • Calculated in your browser
  • Free, no limits
  • No sign-up
  • No data sent

Enter the pace you plan to hold and the distance: you get the finish time and a table of splits to write down or print.

How to use it

  1. Enter the race distance in kilometers or miles (or tap 10K, half marathon, marathon).
  2. Enter your target pace in minutes and seconds per kilometer or per mile, for example 5 and 30.
  3. Choose how the splits are shown: every kilometer (or mile), or every 5 kilometers (or miles).
  4. Read the finish time, the average speed and the table of splits: note it on your wrist or in your watch.

Formula: finish time = pace (min/km) × distance (km). Splits are the pace multiplied by each intermediate distance. It is an exact calculation, for a perfectly constant pace. See Sources.

What are splits for?

A split is the time your watch should show at a given point of the race. Knowing them helps you avoid the most common mistake, starting too fast: if you see 5:20 at the first kilometer while you are aiming for 5:30, you know right away that you need to slow down.

In a half marathon or a marathon, many runners write their splits at 5K, 10K, 15K and so on on a wristband or a photo of their watch screen.

A constant pace: an ideal, not a rule

The calculation assumes you hold exactly the same pace from start to finish. In practice, hills, wind, aid stations and fatigue make your pace vary. Many coaches advise aiming for an even pace, or even a slightly slower first kilometer.

On a hilly course, accept losing a few seconds on the climbs and getting them back without forcing on the descents.

What if you do not know your race pace?

Start from a recent time on another distance with the race time predictor, or from a goal time with the charts: 10K, half marathon or marathon. To find the pace of a time you already ran, use the pace calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my finish time?

Multiply your pace in minutes per kilometer by the distance in kilometers. At 5:30 min/km over 10K, you get 5.5 × 10 = 55 minutes.

Which splits are useful in a marathon?

Many runners note their times at 10K, at the half marathon (21.0975 km), at 30K and at 40K. These checkpoints let you check that your pace stays even.

Should I run an even pace?

It is the simplest reference, and an even pace is often recommended. On a hilly course or in the wind, it is rather your effort that you should keep constant, and your pace varies a little.

Can I start fast and slow down later?

It is not recommended: a start that is too fast usually costs you a clear slowdown at the end, especially beyond 10K. A cautious first kilometer is better.

Is my data saved?

No. The calculation runs in your browser, on your device. Nothing you type is sent to our servers or saved in your browser.
