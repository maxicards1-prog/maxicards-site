10K pace chart

What pace should you hold to finish a 10K in 45, 50 or 55 minutes? The chart gives the pace, the speed and the halfway split.

How to use it

  1. Find your target time in the first column of the table.
  2. Read the pace to hold in minutes per kilometer and per mile, and the speed in km/h.
  3. Note the splits: they let you check that you are running evenly during the race.
  4. For a time that is not in the table, use the pace calculator.

The 10K is the ideal distance to learn how to manage your effort: short enough to be run hard, long enough to punish a start that is too fast. Each line of the chart assumes a constant pace over exactly 10 km (6.2 miles).

Pacing a 10K well

Over 10K, a gap of 5 seconds per kilometer means 50 seconds at the finish: a 5:00 pace instead of 5:05 saves you almost a minute. That is also why starting "too fast" by 15 seconds per kilometer is so costly: you pay for it in the second half.

  • First 2 km: start 5 to 10 seconds slower than your goal pace, while you settle in.
  • From the 3rd to the 7th km: hold the pace without forcing, with controlled breathing.
  • Last 3 km: this is where you can speed up if you have something in reserve.

10K pace is generally 85 to 90% of MAS: check with the training paces from MAS. To see which 10K your current fitness allows, start from a recent time with the race time predictor, then follow the 8-week 10K plan. To work out the time at any pace, use the running time calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What pace do I need for a 10K in 50 minutes?

You need to hold 5:00 min/km (8:03 min/mile), or 12 km/h. The 5K split is 25:00.

How do I calculate my 10K pace?

Divide your target time by 10. A 10K in 55 minutes is run at 5:30 min/km, or about 8:51 min/mile, which is 10.9 km/h.

What is a good 10K time?

It depends on age, experience and goals: many amateur runners finish between 50 and 65 minutes. The important thing is to compare with your own times.

Should I start faster to save time?

No: a start that is too fast makes you slow down later, and the time lost almost always exceeds the time gained. Aim for an even pace, or a faster second half.
