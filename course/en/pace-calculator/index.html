Calculate your running pace

  • Calculated in your browser
  • Free, no limits
  • No sign-up
  • No data sent

Enter a distance and a time: you get your pace per kilometer and per mile, and your speed in km/h and mph.

How to use it

  1. Enter the distance in kilometers or miles, or tap a button: 5K, 10K, half marathon, marathon.
  2. Enter your time in hours, minutes and seconds (leave hours at 0 if you run for less than an hour).
  3. Read your pace in min/km and min/mile and your speed in km/h and mph: the result updates as you type.
  4. To find a target time on another distance, switch to the race time predictor.

Formula: pace (min/km) = total time ÷ distance (km); speed (km/h) = distance (km) ÷ time (h); pace (min/mile) = pace (min/km) × 1.609344. These are definitions, not estimates: the result is exact. Details on the Sources page.

A worked example

You run 10K in 50 minutes. The time is 3,000 seconds; divided by 10 km, that is 300 seconds per kilometer, a pace of 5:00 min/km. Your speed is 10 km ÷ 0.8333 h = 12 km/h. In miles, 5:00 × 1.609344 gives 8:03 min/mile, or about 7.5 mph.

Pace or speed: which one should you use?

Most runners think in minutes per kilometer (or per mile in the US and the UK), because pace tells you how long each kilometer or mile should take: it is what you check on the road. Speed in km/h or mph is what treadmills show, and it is the unit used for maximal aerobic speed tests.

Both carry the same information, and you can switch between them with the pace converter.

Official race distances

A half marathon is 21.0975 km (13.1 miles) and a marathon is 42.195 km (26.2 miles): these are the exact distances the tool uses when you tap the matching button.

On the course, your GPS watch often shows a little more than the official distance, because it measures the path you actually ran, with its curves and lane changes. For a race, always work out your pace from the official distance.

What next?

Your pace tells you where you are. To know how fast to train, work out your VO2 max and MAS, then your training paces. To see how long a race will take at a chosen pace, use the running time calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my running pace in min/km?

Divide your total time by the distance in kilometers. For example, 10K in 50 minutes gives 50 ÷ 10 = 5 minutes per kilometer, or 5:00 min/km.

How do I convert pace to speed in km/h or mph?

Divide 60 by the pace in decimal minutes. A pace of 5:00 min/km is 60 ÷ 5 = 12 km/h. For mph, divide the km/h figure by 1.609344: 12 km/h is about 7.5 mph.

What is a good pace for a beginner?

There is no universal pace: when you start, run at a speed that lets you talk in short sentences. Many beginners run between 6:30 and 8:00 min/km (about 10:30 to 12:50 min/mile), but the most important thing is being able to keep going for the planned time.

Why does my watch show a different pace?

A GPS watch measures the distance you really covered, with a margin of error, and sometimes shows the instant pace. To compare with your official time, use the official distance of the race.

Is my data saved?

No. The calculation runs in your browser, on your device. Nothing you type is sent to our servers or saved in your browser.
