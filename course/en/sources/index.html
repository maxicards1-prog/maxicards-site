Formulas and sources

Every calculation on the site, its formula, its author, its limit.

Here, for each tool of the site, is the formula used and its source. The sources were checked on 23/11/2026. When a value is a coaches' guideline rather than a research result, it says so. Some sources are in French.

Pace, speed, splits

Formula: pace (min/km) = time ÷ distance; speed (km/h) = distance ÷ time (h); min/mile = min/km × 1.609344; mph = km/h ÷ 1.609344

Arithmetic definitions. International mile = 1,609.344 m (international agreement of 1959). Official distances: half marathon 21.0975 km, marathon 42.195 km (World Athletics). View the source.

Time prediction (Riegel)

Formula: T2 = T1 × (D2 ÷ D1)^1.06

Riegel P.S. (1981). Athletic records and human endurance. American Scientist 69(3):285-290. Form proposed as early as 1977 in Runner's World. Valid mainly from 3.5 to 230 minutes of effort. View the source.

MAS, half-Cooper (6 minutes)

Formula: MAS (km/h) = distance in 6 min (m) ÷ 100

Half-Cooper MAS test sheet, French Football Federation (Normandy district; in French); MAS sheet of the École militaire de santé de Lyon-Bron (in French; stated precision: ± 1 km/h). View the source.

MAS, Cooper (12 minutes)

Formula: MAS (km/h) ≈ distance in 12 min (m) ÷ 200 (French practice)

Common practice in France (test sheets of schools and the army; in French). Original test: Cooper K.H. (1968). View the source.

VO2 max from the Cooper test

Formula: VO2 max (ml/kg/min) = (distance in 12 min (m) − 504.9) ÷ 44.73

Cooper K.H. (1968). A means of assessing maximal oxygen intake: correlation between field and treadmill testing. JAMA 203(3):201-204 (115 US Air Force men; correlation 0.90). View the source.

VO2 max from MAS

Formula: VO2 max ≈ 3.5 × MAS (km/h)

Rule of thumb in common use (energy cost of running close to 1 kcal/kg/km), consistent with the order of magnitude of Léger L., Mercier D. (1984), Sports Medicine 1(4):270-277 (for 15 km/h: 52.5 versus about 53 with their equation). Estimate within ± 10%. The 3.5 × v formula was not found word for word in the abstract of that publication. View the source.

Paces as a % of MAS

Formula: pace (s/km) = 3,600 ÷ (MAS × %); easy 60-70%, marathon 70-80%, half marathon 75-85%, threshold 80-90%, 10K 85-90%, intervals 90-105%

Coaches' guidelines, which vary by source (marathon 65 to 85%, threshold 80 to 90%). Consulted on 23/11/2026 (French-language pages): Salomon Running (pace guide), Campus Coach (specific pace), Protéalpes (target pace methods). View the source.

Max HR (Tanaka)

Formula: max HR = 208 − 0.7 × age

Tanaka H., Monahan K.D., Seals D.R. (2001). Age-predicted maximal heart rate revisited. J Am Coll Cardiol 37(1):153-156 (meta-analysis of 351 studies, 18,712 subjects, validated on 514 subjects; standard error of about 10 bpm). View the source.

Max HR (220 − age)

Formula: max HR = 220 − age (less accurate)

Empirical formula attributed to Fox and Haskell (1970s), with no rigorous original publication; it underestimates the max HR of older adults (Tanaka et al., 2001). View the source.

Heart rate reserve (Karvonen)

Formula: target HR = (max HR − resting HR) × % + resting HR

Karvonen M.J., Kentala E., Mustala O. (1957). The effects of training on heart rate: a longitudinal study. Ann Med Exp Biol Fenn 35(3):307-315. View the source.

Heart rate zones (5 zones of 10%)

Formula: Z1 50-60%, Z2 60-70%, Z3 70-80%, Z4 80-90%, Z5 90-100% of max HR

Common convention (watches, apps, federations), with no value as a physiological law: other models exist.

Training plans: principles

Formula: cautious progression, weekly long run, easier week, taper of 1 to 2 weeks

Daniels J. (2013) Daniels' Running Formula, 3rd ed., Human Kinetics; Pfitzinger P., Douglas S., Advanced Marathoning and Faster Road Racing; Higdon H., novice plans (18-week marathon); NHS "Couch to 5K" programme (9 weeks, run-walk alternation). An easier week every 3-4 weeks is coaches' practice, not scientifically demonstrated. View the source.

Taper

Formula: about 2 weeks, volume cut by 41 to 60%, intensity and frequency kept

Bosquet L., Montpetit J., Arvisais D., Mujika I. (2007). Effects of tapering on performance: a meta-analysis. Med Sci Sports Exerc 39(8):1358-1365; Mujika I., Padilla S. (2003). Med Sci Sports Exerc 35(7):1182-1187. View the source.

Increasing mileage

Formula: "10% rule": a caution guideline, not a law

Nielsen R.O. et al. (2014). Excessive progression in weekly running distance and risk of running-related injuries. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 44(10):739-747: above a 30% increase, higher risk for some injuries, but the result is borderline for statistical significance; the 10% threshold is not demonstrated. View the source.

General limits

All these formulas are estimates built on groups of people: they do not take into account your history, your health or the day of the race. They are used to organize training, never to make a diagnosis. See the health disclaimer.
