10K training plan, 8 weeks

  • Calculated in your browser
  • Free, no limits
  • No sign-up
  • No data sent

Three workouts a week for eight weeks: easy running, quality work and a long run, with an easier week and a taper.

How to use it

  1. Check that you already run 30 minutes without stopping, 2 to 3 times a week.
  2. Work out your MAS and enter it in the field above: the paces of each workout show.
  3. Do the 3 workouts of each week, leaving at least one rest day between two workouts.
  4. The last week is short: arrive fresh on the day of the 10K.

Important: This plan gives general guidelines: it is not medical advice and it does not adapt to your personal situation. See a doctor before starting or resuming exercise, and stop if you feel pain, faintness or any unusual symptom.

Goal: run a 10K and improve your time. Length: 8 weeks, 3 workouts per week. Who it is for: Already running 30 minutes without stopping, 2 to 3 times a week, for at least a month.

The principles of the plan

  • One easy run, one quality workout (intervals or threshold) and one long run a week.
  • Week 4 is an easier week: your body absorbs the work and you start again fresher.
  • In the last two weeks the load drops but the intensity stays: that is the taper.
  • Warm up for 10 to 15 minutes of easy running, then cool down for 5 to 10 minutes, before and after each quality workout.

The plan, week by week

Week 1 : Setting the rhythm

  • Easy run. 30 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 8 × (30 s fast 95 to 100% MAS + 30 s slow jog), then 5 min cool-down.
  • Long run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 2 : First threshold blocks

  • Easy run. 35 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 3 × 6 min at 82 to 88% MAS (2 min jog recovery), then 5 min cool-down.
  • Long run. 50 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 3 : Short speed

  • Easy run. 35 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 6 × 400 m at 95 to 100% MAS (1 min 30 jog recovery), then 5 min cool-down.
  • Long run. 55 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 4 : Easier week

  • Easy run. 30 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 2 × 10 min at 82 to 88% MAS (3 min recovery), then 5 min cool-down.
  • Long run. 45 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 5 : Big quality block

  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 5 × 1,000 m at 90 to 95% MAS (2 min jog recovery), then 5 min cool-down.
  • Long run. 60 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 6 : 10K pace

  • Easy run. 40 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 3 × 10 min at 85 to 90% MAS (3 min recovery), then 5 min cool-down.
  • Long run. 65 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS, with the last 10 minutes a little firmer 70 to 75% MAS.

Week 7 : Last big week

  • Easy run. 35 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 6 × 800 m at 92 to 97% MAS (1 min 30 recovery), then 5 min cool-down.
  • Long run. 60 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.

Week 8 : Taper and race

  • Easy run. 30 min at an easy pace 60 to 70% MAS.
  • Quality. 10 min warm-up, then 4 × 1 min at 92 to 97% MAS (1 min 30 recovery), then 5 min cool-down: short, to stay sharp.
  • Race: 10K. 10 min very easy warm-up, then 10K at 85 to 90% MAS. Start a little slower than your goal pace for the first 2 km.

What is this plan based on?

The principles (cautious progression, one long run a week, easier weeks, a two-week taper) are those of the classic running plans: Daniels, Pfitzinger, Higdon.

The taper follows the conclusions of the meta-analysis by Bosquet et al. (2007): about two weeks, volume cut by 40 to 60%, intensity and frequency kept. Full references are on the Sources page. This plan is a general model: it does not replace a coach or a doctor's advice.

Adapting the plan

The durations and percentages are guidelines: if you feel comfortable, you can add a few minutes of easy running, without touching the quality workouts. If you miss a quality workout, do not make it up the next day: skip it. Choose your goal time with the 10K pace chart, and you can read the guide on improving your 10K time.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to prepare for a 10K?

For a runner who already runs 30 minutes, 8 weeks are enough for a gradual preparation. A complete beginner would rather start with the 5K plan.

How many workouts a week?

Three: one easy run, one quality workout (intervals or threshold) and one long run. It is a good compromise between progress and recovery.

What do the MAS percentages mean?

They show the intensity: 60 to 70% for easy running, 85 to 90% for 10K pace, 95 to 100% for short intervals. Enter your MAS to see them in min/km and min/mile.

What if I have no GPS watch?

Run by time and by feel: easy running where you can talk, threshold "comfortably hard", intervals "hard but even". A 400 m track lets you check your paces.

What if I am injured or ill?

Stop the workouts, see a doctor if needed, and resume the plan gradually once you are better, without trying to make up the missed workouts.
