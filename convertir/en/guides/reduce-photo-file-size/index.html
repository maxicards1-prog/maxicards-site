How to reduce the file size of a photo

You have two levers: shrink the dimensions in pixels and increase the compression.

The most effective approach is often to combine both, in your browser, without uploading the photo.

Updated November 23, 2026

How to do it

  1. Check what is required. Note the size or dimension limit given by the email, the form or the website, and the accepted formats.
  2. Reduce the dimensions. With the resize tool, bring the photo down to a size suited to its use. This is the most powerful lever.
  3. Compress the image. Then run the photo through the image compressor and choose a level that keeps good quality.
  4. Check the result. Look at the resulting file size and check sharpness before sending. Try again with a stronger setting if needed.

Why is a photo large?

A recent phone produces photos of several million pixels, far more than a screen or a web page displays. Every pixel is stored: the more there are, the heavier the file. So reducing the weight takes two separate actions.

Lever 1: reduce the dimensions

To view a photo on screen or insert it in an email, you do not need the camera's full resolution. With the resize tool, bring it down to reasonable dimensions: the weight drops sharply, often with no visible difference on screen. Keep the original if you want to print later (see the resolution needed for printing).

Lever 2: compress

The image compressor lightens the file by accepting a slight loss of detail. A moderate level already cuts the weight a lot; beyond that, defects (blocks, blur) can appear. Try it, compare, adjust.

Lever 3: change the format

  • A photo saved as PNG is often very heavy: PNG to JPG shrinks it noticeably.
  • For your own website, JPG to WebP lightens it further, since WebP is lighter than JPEG at comparable quality according to MDN.
  • An iPhone photo in HEIC is already efficient, but may be rejected: convert it to JPG, see the dedicated guide.

Depending on the use

For an email

Email services limit attachment size; the exact limit depends on your provider and changes over time, so check its official help. Reducing the dimensions is usually enough.

For an online form

Read the instructions: maximum size, allowed formats, sometimes required dimensions. Follow them to the letter to avoid a rejection. If the site asks for JPG, convert first.

For a website

Match the image width to the space where it is displayed, then compress and pick a lightweight format. This speeds up page loading.

Limits of the tool

Our tool handles images up to 80 MB, directly in the browser, with no file upload. It does not make an image sharper: shrinking is possible, but enlarging a small photo does not create detail.

Frequently asked questions

Does reducing a photo's size hurt its quality?

Reducing dimensions and compressing both remove information. With moderate settings the difference is often hard to see on screen, but always keep the original.

What is the size limit for an email?

It varies by provider and may change. Check your email service's official help for the current value.

My file is still too large. What can I do?

Reduce the dimensions further, increase the compression, or convert to JPG if the photo is a PNG.

Are my photos sent over the internet?

No. Processing happens in your browser and the files stay on your device.
