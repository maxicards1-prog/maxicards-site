What resolution do you need to print an image?

The common benchmark is 300 dpi (dots per inch): to print a 4 x 6 inch photo you need about 1,200 x 1,800 pixels.

Updated November 23, 2026

DPI and PPI: close cousins

DPI stands for dots per inch. For digital images, people often say PPI (pixels per inch) and use the two terms interchangeably. The number says how many of the image's pixels are spread over one inch (2.54 cm) of paper. The more there are, the finer the print; too few, and the image looks blurry or pixelated.

The benchmark: 300 dpi

For a quality print viewed up close (photo, document, card), people commonly aim for 300 dpi. It is a rule of thumb, not a law: for a large print viewed from a distance, such as a poster or a banner, a lower resolution is generally enough. When in doubt, ask the print shop for its recommendation.

The calculation: from inches or centimeters to pixels

The formula is simple:

pixels needed = size in inches x dpi (or: size in cm ÷ 2.54, then x dpi)

FormatSizePixels at 300 dpi
4 x 6 in photo4 x 6 in (about 10 x 15 cm)1,200 x 1,800
Letter8.5 x 11 in2,550 x 3,300
A421 x 29.7 cm2,480 x 3,508
A329.7 x 42 cm3,508 x 4,961

The reverse calculation: how large can you print?

You have an image of 3,000 x 2,000 pixels. At 300 dpi, that gives 10 x 6.67 inches, or about 25.4 x 16.9 cm. Beyond that, printing is still possible, but sharpness drops. To find your image's dimensions, look at its properties (right-click then Properties on Windows, or Get Info on a Mac).

A common myth: the "72 dpi" field in a file

A file may show "72 dpi" in its information. That is only a label that does not change the number of pixels. What matters is the total number of pixels compared with the printed size. Changing the dpi value without resampling does not make the image sharper.

What if the image is too small?

Artificially enlarging an image does not create detail: the result is often blurry. It is better to find the original at full resolution, or to print smaller. In the other direction, if your image is much larger than necessary, resizing and compression lighten the file for sending to the print shop, without going below the required pixels. Key points

  • Keep the photo at full resolution until you have chosen the print size.
  • Avoid repeated JPG recompression.
  • Check the print shop's guidelines (file format, margins, color profile).

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need 300 dpi?

It is the common benchmark for a print viewed up close. For a large format seen from a distance, a lower resolution is often acceptable.

Are DPI and PPI different?

Strictly, DPI counts printer dots and PPI counts image pixels, but in everyday use the two terms are used interchangeably.

How many pixels do I need to print on A4?

At 300 dpi, about 2,480 x 3,508 pixels.

Is a phone photo enough to print?

Often yes for a small or medium size, since phone photos contain a lot of pixels. Use the formula to be sure.
