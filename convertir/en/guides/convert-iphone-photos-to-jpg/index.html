How to convert iPhone photos from HEIC to JPG

How to do it

  1. Open the converter. Go to the HEIC to JPG page of this site. You can drop several photos at once.
  2. Drop your HEIC photos. On an iPhone, pick them from your photo library. On a computer, choose the files from the folder where you copied them.
  3. Start the conversion. The conversion runs in your browser, so your photos never leave your device.
  4. Save the JPGs. Download each converted photo, then send it or upload it wherever you need.

Why your photos are HEIC

On recent iPhones, the camera saves photos as HEIC by default (Apple's flavor of HEIF). The file is smaller than a JPG of similar quality, but not everything can read it: an online form, an older program or a PC without the right extension may reject it. To understand the format in detail, read What is HEIC?

Convert photos you already took

The simplest way is the HEIC to JPG converter. It works on iPhone, Android, Windows and Mac, right in the browser. If you need transparency or lossless quality, choose HEIC to PNG instead (larger files). For a document you need to send, HEIC to PDF puts your photos in a single file.

A bonus: converting re-encodes the image, which removes its metadata, including the GPS location. To go further, see how to remove the location from a photo.

Make future photos JPG

On the iPhone, open Settings, tap Camera, then Formats. Two choices appear:

  • Most Compatible: new photos are saved as JPEG (and videos as H.264), which more devices and programs can read.
  • High Efficiency: new photos are saved as HEIF (and videos as HEVC), which takes up less space.

The "Most Compatible" choice only appears on iPhones and iPads that can capture in HEIF or HEVC. It does not change photos you already took: they stay HEIC, which is why the converter is useful.

Which one should you pick?

If you often send photos to people with a computer, to an official website or to a classifieds site, "Most Compatible" saves you many conversions. If storage space on the iPhone matters more, keep "High Efficiency" and convert when needed. It is a trade-off: slightly larger JPG files against fewer problems opening them.

Official source: Apple support page about HEIF and HEVC formats.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting HEIC to JPG reduce quality?

JPG uses lossy compression, so a very slight difference can exist, rarely visible to the eye on a photo. To avoid any loss, convert to PNG instead, at the cost of larger files.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion runs in your browser, on your device. No file is sent anywhere.

Why is the Most Compatible setting missing on my phone?

According to Apple, this option only exists on iPhones and iPads that can capture in HEIF or HEVC. On an older device, photos are already JPEG.

Can I convert several photos at once?

Yes. You can drop several HEIC files together and download the JPGs one by one.
