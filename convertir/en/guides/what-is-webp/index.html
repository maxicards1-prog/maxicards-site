What is WebP, and how to open .webp images?

WebP is a modern image format built to make web pages lighter.

Updated November 23, 2026

A format made for the web

WebP is an image format created to reduce the weight of images on the internet. It can compress with or without loss, handle transparency and even animation. According to MDN documentation, a lossy WebP image is on average 25 to 35% smaller than a JPEG, and about 26% smaller than a lossless PNG. Less weight means pages that load faster, which is why so many sites use it.

Why your downloaded images are .webp

When you right-click "Save image as..." on a web page, you get the file exactly as the site served it. Many sites serve WebP to all recent browsers. So you end up with a .webp file even if the image looks like a JPG or a PNG. Reminder: an image found on the internet is still protected by copyright, so make sure you are allowed to use it.

How to open it

  • In a browser: Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari (from Safari 14) can read WebP. Drag the file into a browser window.
  • In an editing or office program: some programs, especially older ones, do not recognize it. In that case, convert it.
  • On a site that asks for JPG or PNG: same solution, convert it.

Convert it

For a photo, WebP to JPG is the most universal choice. For a logo, a screenshot or an image with a transparent background, use WebP to PNG, which keeps transparency. The reverse also exists: JPG to WebP makes your images lighter for your own website. The conversion runs in the browser, without uploading your files.

WebP or JPG: which one to keep?

For publishing on a website, WebP is often an excellent choice. For sharing by email, printing or opening in any program, JPG is still safer. The table in JPG vs PNG vs WebP compares all the common formats.

An animation in .webp

A WebP file can contain an animation, like a GIF. If you convert it to JPG or PNG, you get a still image. So do not expect an animation after the conversion.

Frequently asked questions

Can I rename a .webp file to .jpg?

No. Changing the extension does not convert the image: the content is still WebP and the file may be rejected. You need to actually convert it.

Is WebP lower quality than JPG?

Not necessarily: at a comparable visual quality, it is lighter. Quality mostly depends on the compression setting chosen when the image was created.

My PC won't open it. Is that normal?

It can happen depending on your system version and software. Browsers can read it, and converting to JPG or PNG solves the problem in every case.
