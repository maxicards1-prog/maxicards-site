ChatGPT Prompts for Entrepreneurs

Ten prompts to clarify your offer, structure your documents and move faster, without handing off your decisions.

In short

  1. Clarify a business idea
  2. Run a simple market study
  3. Define a clear offer
  4. Build a quarterly action plan
  5. Write a clear quote
  6. Prepare a 3-minute pitch
  7. Analyze my pricing
  8. Write a difficult business email
  9. Organize my week
  10. Debrief after a failure

As an entrepreneur or freelancer, you play every role: sales, accounting, marketing. An AI can serve as a sounding board to clarify an idea, organize a plan or draft a document.

It replaces neither an accountant, nor a lawyer, nor your customers. The prompts below are for thinking and writing, then you validate with the right people.

  • Describe your real situation, with your constraints.
  • Ask for hypotheses to verify, not certainties.
  • Have numbers, contracts and taxes validated by a professional.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Clarify a business idea

To test an idea before investing time or money.

You are a startup mentor. My idea: [description]. Target customers: [profile]. My skills: [experience]. Starting budget: [amount].
First ask me 8 questions to test the idea. Then list the 5 riskiest assumptions, an inexpensive way to test each one in two weeks, and a clear success or failure criterion.

2. Run a simple market study

To structure your research on a local market or a niche.

I want to launch [business] in [geographic area]. Help me build a market research plan: questions to ask about customers, competitors, prices and channels.
For each question, tell me where to find the information (interviews, observation, public sites, surveys) and how to record it. Do not give market figures: I will look them up myself.

3. Define a clear offer

When your customers do not understand what you sell.

Help me phrase my offer. My business: [description]. My typical customers: [profile]. Their main problem: [problem]. What I concretely do: [list].
Suggest 3 one-sentence versions of my value proposition, 3 offer tiers (essential, standard, complete) with the content of each, and the questions customers ask before buying.

4. Build a quarterly action plan

To go from vague goals to dated actions.

My goal for the next 3 months: [goal]. Time available: [hours per week]. Resources: [tools, budget, help]. Constraints: [list].
Break the goal into monthly milestones, then weekly actions. For each milestone, give a tracking metric and the main risk. Present a table. Tell me if the goal looks unrealistic with my resources.

5. Write a clear quote

To present a service without ambiguity.

Help me write the text of a quote for [service] aimed at [type of client]. Details: description of the work [details], duration [length], price [price], payment terms [terms].
Suggest a clear description of the service, what is included, what is not included, the steps and the timeline. Also list the mandatory information to check with my accountant. Do not give a definitive legal opinion.

6. Prepare a 3-minute pitch

To present your project to a partner or an investor.

I have to present my project [description] to [audience: bank, partner, panel] in 3 minutes. Details: problem [problem], solution [solution], customers [targets], business model [model], need [funding or help].
Write a spoken pitch of about 400 words with a hook, problem, solution, market, model, need and conclusion. Add 5 tough questions I might be asked and the points to prepare.

7. Analyze my pricing

To think through a consistent price list.

I offer [service or product]. My costs: [list with amounts]. My time: [hours]. My income goal: [amount]. My status: [freelancer or company].
Show me how to calculate a minimum price, three methods for setting a price (costs, perceived value, market) and the risks of each. Do the calculations step by step so I can check them. Remind me to validate with an accountant.

8. Write a difficult business email

To chase an unpaid invoice or decline a request with tact.

Write a business email for [situation: reminder about an unpaid invoice, refusing a discount, late delivery]. Facts: [dates, amounts, previous exchanges]. My goal: [goal].
Firm and courteous tone, 120 words maximum, a request for action with a deadline. Give three levels of firmness (friendly, reminder, final notice). Do not cite any law: tell me what I should check.

9. Organize my week

To prioritize when everything seems urgent.

Here are my tasks for the week: [unsorted list]. My available time slots: [schedule]. My priorities: [goals].
Sort the tasks (important and urgent, important not urgent, delegate, drop), then propose a realistic schedule with breaks and a buffer for the unexpected. Tell me what can wait without consequences.

10. Debrief after a failure

To learn from a lost sale or a failed project.

I want to analyze a situation that went badly: [factual description]. My decisions at the time: [list]. Result: [result].
Guide me with questions: what was under my control, what information was I missing, which signals did I ignore? Then help me write 3 concrete lessons and 3 actions to put in place next time.

Validate with professionals

An AI can help you ask the right questions, but it can be wrong about tax, employment and legal rules. Your business status, revenue thresholds, sales tax, contributions and mandatory notices change: check official websites, an accountant or a small business advisor.

Data not to paste

Do not enter customer data, signed contracts, trade secrets, bank details or named accounting documents. To work on numbers, use approximate or fictional values. If your data concerns people, privacy laws apply.

Limits

Market figures quoted by an AI may be wrong or outdated: never use them in a business plan without a source. A plan written with the AI is only valuable if it rests on real conversations with real customers. Test your ideas in the field.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI write my business plan?

It can structure the document and ask you questions, but the numbers, assumptions and sources must come from you. A banker or partner expects verifiable data and real knowledge of the market.

Can I trust it on taxes?

Not without checking. The rules change every year and depend on your situation. Use the AI to prepare your questions, then ask an accountant or consult official websites.

Which prompt should I pick first?

Start by clarifying your offer and your customer. Everything else depends on it: pricing, communication, outreach. Once that base is set, the planning and writing prompts become much more effective.
