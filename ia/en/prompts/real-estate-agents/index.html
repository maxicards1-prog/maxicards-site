ChatGPT Prompts for Real Estate Agents

Ten prompts to write your listings, prepare showings and follow up with clients faster, without neglecting the required disclosures and checks.

A real estate agent writes all day: listings, property descriptions, follow-up emails, showing reports. An AI helps you produce a first draft and structure these texts, based on the real information about the property and the client.

Each prompt below gives a role, a context to fill in between brackets and a response format. You remain responsible for the accuracy of what is published: the AI does not visit the property and does not know your area.

  • Describe only what you have observed on site.
  • Never paste clients' personal data.
  • Review every listing before publication.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Write a listing for a sale

To turn your showing notes into a clear and attractive listing.

You are an experienced real estate agent. Write a for-sale listing of 150 words maximum from this verified information: property type [house, apartment], size [size], rooms [number], neighborhood [neighborhood], floor or lot [details], general condition [condition], strengths [list], weaknesses not to hide [list].
Structure: catchy headline, opening paragraph, room-by-room description, surroundings, conclusion. Do not invent any feature, do not use unjustified superlatives, and leave [to be completed] placeholders for the mandatory disclosures that I will check.

2. Adapt a listing for each channel

To adapt one listing for a portal, social media and a window display.

Here is my approved listing: [paste the listing].
Adapt it into four versions: a 120-word version for a real estate portal, a 50-word social media post with a hook and 3 keywords, a 15-word sentence for an office window, and a 160-character text message to send to buyers registered in my database.
The facts (size, price, location) must stay identical to the original: do not change any number and flag any wording that could seem misleading.

3. Prepare a showing with buyers

To organize the showing and anticipate questions.

You are a real estate advisor preparing a showing. The property: [type, size, condition, neighborhood]. The buyers: [project in general terms: first-time buyers, family, investor], with no names or contact details. Their criteria: [list].
Suggest: a room-by-room tour with the points to highlight, five questions to ask the buyers to understand their priorities, the likely objections with an honest answer to each, and the documents I should have with me.
Format: organized list, one page maximum.

4. Showing report for the seller

To tell a seller about showing feedback with tact.

Write a showing report for a seller. Details: date [date], number of visitors [number], perceived interest [high, medium, low], positive comments [list], negative comments [list: price, repairs, noise], follow-up [offer, second showing, dropped].
Professional, factual and constructive tone, 150 words maximum, without identifying the visitors. Finish with two concrete recommendations (adjusting the presentation, a price point to discuss, simple repairs) phrased as suggestions I will present to the seller.

5. Follow-up email after a showing

To follow up with buyers with a personalized message.

Write a follow-up email to buyers who viewed [type of property] in [city] [number] days ago. What they liked: [details]. What made them hesitate: [details]. Possible next step: [second showing, financing pre-approval from their lender, offer].
Warm but not pushy tone, 120 words maximum, a precise subject line, a single call to action. Suggest a shorter version for a text message. Do not invent any information about the property or the financing terms.

6. Prepare a listing appointment

To win over an owner with solid arguments.

I am meeting an owner who is hesitating between several agencies to sell [type of property] in [city]. My way of working: [real description of your services: photos, showings, follow-up, marketing]. My real strengths: [list].
Prepare my appointment: eight questions to understand their plan and timeline, a 20-minute presentation outline, three common objections (commission, exclusivity, timeline) with honest answers, and the list of documents to ask the owner for.
Do not promise any timeline or guaranteed sale price.

7. Price opinion presentation

To clearly present an estimate based on real data.

I need to present a price opinion for a [type of property] of [size] in [neighborhood]. Here are the comparable sales I found in my sources: [list of properties with size, condition, sale price, date]. My findings on the property: [strengths and weaknesses].
Write a two-paragraph text that explains the price range I propose: [range], citing only the comparables provided. State clearly that it is an estimate, that it depends on the market and on negotiation, and list the assumptions to verify.

8. Reply to a tenant or a landlord

To handle a common request in property management.

You are a property manager. A tenant reports: [problem: leak, heating failure, noise from neighbors, late rent]. Context: [date reported, lease, what has already been done].
Write two letters: a reply to the tenant that acknowledges receipt, explains the planned steps and a timeline, and an information message to the landlord with the options and their consequences. Clear and neutral tone, no specific legal claims: tell me what to check in the lease and in the applicable regulations before sending.

9. Checklist before signing the purchase agreement

To forget nothing in the sales file.

I am a real estate agent and I am preparing the signing of a purchase agreement for [type of property: condo, house, land] in [city]. Build a checklist of the documents to collect from the seller, from the buyer and from third parties (building management, closing agent, inspector), with for each line: the document, who provides it, and a "received on" field.
Finish with the list of points on which I must check the current regulations with a closing attorney or an official source, without you stating what they say.

10. Marketing plan for a property that is hard to sell

When a property stays a long time without an offer.

A property has been on the market for [length] with no offer: [type, city, listing price, weaknesses, number of showings and feedback]. You are a real estate marketing consultant. Analyze the situation in three parts: possible causes to verify, actions to test on the photos, the text and the presentation, and how to raise a possible price change with the seller.
Propose a 30-day plan with simple indicators (number of inquiries, showings). Be cautious: these are hypotheses, not certainties.

Required disclosures and checks

A real estate listing is subject to mandatory disclosures (price, fees, energy performance, and so on). The AI does not always know the exact list or the latest changes: check the official rules from your local real estate regulator or professional association before publishing. Every size, price and feature must come from your documents (inspections, title, measurements), never from the AI.

No discrimination

Never ask the AI to screen or describe clients by origin, family status, religion, health or other prohibited criteria. Write neutral listings and selection criteria based on objective elements tied to the property and the application. Also review the AI's wording: some expressions can suggest a type of tenant or buyer.

Client data and privacy

Do not paste your clients' names, contact details, financial situation or ID documents. Describe them in general terms ("couple with a child, first purchase"). Privacy laws say personal data may only be collected and processed for a specific purpose: do not pass it to a tool without need.

Limits

The AI knows neither the local market, nor current real prices, nor the exact condition of the property. An estimate must come from your sales data. For legal, tax or financing questions, refer your clients to the closing attorney, the mortgage broker or the relevant authority.

Frequently asked questions

Can I publish a listing written by the AI directly?

No. Check every piece of information against your documents, add the mandatory disclosures and make sure nothing is exaggerated or inaccurate. You remain responsible for the published content, even if it was written with automated help.

Can AI estimate the price of a property?

It can help present an estimate, but not determine it without your data. It does not know the recent sales in your area. Use your comparables and your reference tools, then ask it to write the pitch.

Can I paste a buyer's file to get advice?

No. A file contains sensitive personal and financial data. Describe the situation anonymously and in general terms, and leave financing questions to a mortgage broker or a closing attorney.
