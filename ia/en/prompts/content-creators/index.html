ChatGPT Prompts for Content Creators

Ten prompts to prepare your videos, episodes and newsletters, while keeping your voice, your ideas and your point of view.

In short

  1. Find an original angle for a video
  2. Structure a video script
  3. Suggest titles and thumbnail ideas
  4. Prepare a podcast episode with a guest
  5. Turn a recording into episode notes
  6. Write a newsletter issue
  7. Plan a production schedule
  8. Analyze what works on your channel
  9. Proofread and hunt for errors
  10. Reply to a community and handle criticism

Creating for YouTube, a podcast or a newsletter takes steady ideas, a clear structure and a lot of preparation. An AI is useful for finding angles, organizing your notes and proofreading, not for speaking in your place.

Your audience comes for you: your point of view, your stories, your way of telling them. Give those to the AI, then keep what sounds like you. For social media in the broader sense, the "social media marketing" page of the site complements this one.

  • Describe your audience and your tone with examples.
  • Provide your notes and sources, not just a topic.
  • Check every fact before announcing it.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Find an original angle for a video

When the topic is already everywhere and you need to stand out.

I create content about [topic area] for [audience]. Topic of my next video: [topic]. What my audience already knows: [level]. My personal experience on the subject: [experience].
Propose 8 different angles (contrarian take, case study, common mistake, comparison, live experiment, etc.) with for each a one-sentence promise, what the viewer takes away, and the risk of looking like what already exists. Rank them by originality.

2. Structure a video script

To go from scattered notes to an outline that holds attention.

Here are my notes for a [length]-minute video: [unsorted notes]. My audience: [profile]. My tone: [tone].
Organize them into a script: 15-second hook, statement of the promise, 3 to 5 parts with transitions, attention re-hooks at regular intervals, conclusion and a single call to action. Indicate the moments where an image or a demonstration is needed. Add no fact that is not in my notes; list what would need to be verified.

3. Suggest titles and thumbnail ideas

To test several wordings before publishing.

My video is about: [summary in 3 sentences]. Real promise made to the viewer: [promise]. Audience: [profile].
Suggest 10 titles of 60 characters maximum, in different registers (question, result, curiosity, list), without any lie or exaggeration that would betray the content. For the 3 best, describe a thumbnail idea: main element, text of 3 words maximum, emotion. Explain in one line why each might work.

4. Prepare a podcast episode with a guest

To lead a rich and natural interview.

I am hosting a guest (profile: [general description, no name]) on my podcast about [topic area]. Length: [length]. Goal of the episode: [goal]. What my audience wants to learn: [expectations].
Prepare: a 60-second guest introduction to adapt, 12 questions organized in three parts (background, expertise, strong opinions), follow-ups to go deeper, a recurring closing question and three pitfalls to avoid. I will verify the facts about the guest before recording.

5. Turn a recording into episode notes

To publish fast after recording.

Here is the transcript of my episode, with no private data: [paste the transcript]. Length: [length].
Write: a 120-word summary, 6 chapters with approximate timestamps to confirm, 5 key ideas, 3 exact quotes taken from the text, any links or resources mentioned (to verify). Do not reword the quotes and do not invent anything. Flag the passages to cut or that may contain sensitive information.

6. Write a newsletter issue

For a regular newsletter in your own voice.

Write the issue of my newsletter [name or theme] for [readers]. This week's items: [3 ideas, a personal anecdote, a useful link]. My voice: [description and two example sentences].
Structure: subject line (3 versions), personal opening of 3 sentences, one main idea developed in 200 words, two short recommendation blocks, a question to ask readers. Do not add any anecdote: use only mine. Remind me to include the unsubscribe link.

7. Plan a production schedule

To keep a realistic publishing rhythm.

I publish [frequency] on [platform(s)] and I work alone with [hours per week] hours available. My tasks: [research, writing, filming or recording, editing, publishing].
Build a 4-week schedule with themed days, batch production slots, a safety buffer and a minimal version for busy weeks. Estimate the time of each task as a range and tell me what can be simplified or dropped without losing quality.

8. Analyze what works on your channel

To interpret your stats without overinterpreting.

Here are the stats of my last 10 pieces of content: [title, length, views, average retention, subscribers gained or other numbers you have]. My goal: [goal].
Analyze the trends: what holds viewers, what loses them, topics or formats that stand out. Propose 5 hypotheses to test (one variable at a time), how to test them and what would validate each hypothesis. State what the sample does not allow you to conclude.

9. Proofread and hunt for errors

To make it reliable before publishing.

Proofread this script or text before publication: [paste the text]. I am looking for: factual claims to verify (list them one by one with the type of source to consult), sentences that are hard to say aloud, repetitions, passages that betray the promise of the title, legal risks (music, excerpt of a work, image, statements about a person).
Do not rewrite: present a table of excerpt, problem, suggestion. I will make the corrections myself.

10. Reply to a community and handle criticism

To reply to comments with consistency and perspective.

Here are 5 comments received on my latest content: [comments without usernames]. My usual tone with my community: [description].
For each, suggest a reply of 30 words maximum: sincere thanks, an answer to a question, a welcome for reasoned criticism or a calm reply to a hostile comment (without starting a fight). For each comment, say whether it is better to reply, not reply or report. I will keep the decision.

Keep your voice

Content written entirely by an AI looks like everyone else's. What builds loyalty is your experience, your opinions, your mistakes, your proof. Use the AI to organize, proofread and look for angles; tell the stories yourself, in your own words.

Verify and respect rights

Check every fact before announcing it to your audience: an error said on video stays there. For music, images, excerpts and statements about people, respect copyright and the platform's rules. Sponsored content must be clearly disclosed.

Data not to paste

Do not paste private messages, subscribers' addresses, guests' data or confidential information received under agreement. To analyze your stats, use aggregated numbers. An interview transcript contains other people's words: ask for their consent if you give it to an external tool.

Limits

The AI does not know your channel's real statistics and does not predict what will work: only your tests tell you. Keep a small number of experiments going at a time and note what you learn.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to say that I use AI?

The rules depend on the platform and the type of content. Some ask you to label synthetic or sponsored content. Check each platform's rules and be transparent with your audience when it could otherwise mislead them.

Can AI write my whole script?

It can suggest a structure and ideas, but a script that contains neither your experience nor your voice is noticeable. Provide your notes, have it organize the content, then rewrite aloud so it sounds natural.

Can I give it the transcript of my episode?

Yes if it contains no private data or confidential remarks, and if your guests agree. Check the tool's terms of use. Always check the quotes and chapters against the recording.
