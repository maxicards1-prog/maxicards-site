ChatGPT Prompts for Sales Reps

Ten prompts to prepare your meetings, write your outreach messages and handle objections better.

A sales rep spends a lot of time writing: outreach messages, follow-ups, proposals, call notes. An AI speeds up these tasks and helps you prepare your meetings and practice against objections.

  • State the customer profile: size, industry, role.

1. First outreach message

To contact a decision maker by email or on a professional network.

You are an experienced B2B sales rep. Write a first outreach message of 90 words maximum for a [recipient's role] at a [industry, size] company.
My offer: [description in one sentence]. The problem I solve: [problem]. One personalization detail: [public information about the company].
Structure: personalized hook, problem, concrete benefit, simple open question. No flattery, no numerical promise I cannot prove. Suggest 3 subject lines.

2. Prepare a customer meeting

The day before a meeting, so you forget nothing.

Tomorrow I am meeting [role] at [company], in the [industry] industry. Goal of the meeting: [goal]. What I know about them: [information].
Prepare for me: 8 open discovery questions, 3 hypotheses about their challenges to verify, a meeting agenda for [length], and the next step I must secure at the end.
Present the result as an organized list. Ask me any questions you need first.

3. Respond to an objection

To find honest answers to the classic roadblocks.

A prospect tells me: "[objection: it is too expensive, we already have a supplier, not the right time]". My offer: [offer]. Their situation: [context].
Suggest 3 ways to respond: a question to understand the real reason, a rephrasing that shows I listened, and a factual argument.
Calm and respectful tone, no pressure, no misleading argument. Tell me what I should check before responding.

4. Follow up on an unanswered quote

When a customer goes quiet after a proposal.

I sent a quote of [amount or type of service] to [role] [number] days ago, with no reply. Context: [details of the discussion].
Write three follow-ups, each shorter than the last: one that shares useful information, one with a simple question, and a last one that leaves the door open.
Each message: 60 words maximum, friendly tone, no guilt-tripping, a clear call to action.

5. Write a sales proposal

To structure a clear offer after a meeting.

You are a sales manager. Help me write a sales proposal for [client]. Stated need: [need]. My solution: [description]. Price: [price]. Timeline: [timeline].
Outline: the client's context and challenges, proposed solution, expected benefits (no invented numbers), schedule, pricing and terms, next steps.
Format: short headings, simple sentences, about one page. Flag the information you do not know so I can add it.

6. Write up a call summary

To turn your raw notes into usable call notes.

Here are my raw notes from a call with a customer: [paste your notes, without sensitive data].
Turn them into structured call notes: context, stated needs, objections, decisions made, next actions with owner and date. Finish with a 100-word confirmation email to send to the customer. Add no information that is not in my notes.

7. Build a call script

To prepare a cold call.

Write a 2-minute cold call script to sell [offer] to [target]. It must contain: a 10-second introduction, an opening question, two qualifying questions, and a request for a meeting.
Also prepare answers to: "Just send me an email," "I do not have time," "Who gave you my number?"
Natural tone, short sentences, written to be spoken. Remind me of the rules I must follow for cold calling where I operate.

8. Benefit-based pitch

To move from product features to customer benefits.

My product or service: [description and features]. My target: [profile].
Turn each feature into a concrete customer benefit, as a table: feature, benefit, proof I can provide (demo, testimonial, trial), likely objection.
Do not invent any numbers or customer references. If I am missing a proof point, say so, so I can look for one.

9. Qualify a prospect

To decide where to invest your time.

Help me create a qualification scorecard for my prospects in [industry]. I sell [offer] and my sales cycle lasts about [length].
Suggest 6 criteria (budget, decision maker, need, timeline, competition, fit), questions to check each one, and a simple score out of 10.
Present a table and add 3 warning signs that it is better to stop following up.

10. Prepare a negotiation

Before a discussion on price or terms.

I am negotiating with [client] on [point: price, deadline, discount]. My minimum acceptable margin: [threshold]. Their likely requests: [list]. What I can offer in exchange: [list].
Prepare my negotiation: my goal, my limit, 3 concessions to trade rather than give away, 3 sentences to stay on course. Then run a simulation: you play the client, I respond, and you give me feedback.

What to check

An AI does not know your product better than you do. Review every argument before sending it: no promise of results you cannot keep, no invented number or testimonial, no questionable comparison with a competitor. These points can make you liable.

Data not to paste

Do not enter customers' personal data (names, contact details, purchase history), confidential price lists, contracts or trade secrets. Replace them with general descriptions: "director of a small logistics company." Outreach rules (privacy laws, opt-out, do-not-call lists) apply whatever tool you use.

Limits

An overly polished message stands out. Add a real detail about the prospect, your own style and a sincere question. The AI prepares the ground; the sales relationship stays human. Also keep track of what works: messages that get replies are worth more than any generic template.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send a message written by the AI as is?

It is better to review and adapt it. Add a detail specific to the prospect and check that every claim is accurate. A text that is too generic is often ignored, and a mistake about your offer can cost you a sale.

What should I do about customer information?

Do not paste personal data or confidential documents. Describe the situation in general or anonymized terms. Also check your company's policy on the use of AI tools.

Can AI replace a CRM?

No. It helps you write and prepare, but it does not reliably store your prospects. Keep your CRM as the source of truth and use the AI for text and thinking.
