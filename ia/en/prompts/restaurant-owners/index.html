ChatGPT Prompts for Restaurant Owners

Ten prompts to save time on the menu, communication and team management, without ever leaving the allergen question to the AI.

In short

  1. Dish descriptions from your recipe sheets
  2. Reply to a negative customer review
  3. Service schedule for the team
  4. Daily special and menu ideas
  5. Social media posts
  6. Job description and recruiting ad
  7. Service protocol and team briefing
  8. Message for a group or an event
  9. Inventory tracking and waste management
  10. Answers to common customer questions

Between service, orders and the team, a restaurant owner has little time to write. An AI helps you write dish descriptions, replies to reviews and messages to the team, based on what you know about your restaurant.

The prompts below ask for your real information: products, prices, atmosphere, constraints. The AI tastes nothing and does not know your suppliers. It must never be your source on allergens or on food safety.

  • Give the real list of ingredients for each dish.
  • Decide the allergen notices yourself.
  • Review before posting or publishing.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Dish descriptions from your recipe sheets

For a menu that is both appetizing and honest.

You are a copywriter specializing in restaurants. Here are my dishes with their real ingredients: [dish 1: ingredients, cooking method], [dish 2: ...], [dish 3: ...]. Style of my restaurant: [bistro, fine dining, family, street food]. Customers: [description].
Write a description of 20 to 25 words per dish, appetizing and faithful to the ingredients listed. Do not add any ingredient, origin or label that I have not mentioned. Do not mention any allergen: I will handle that information myself from my sheets.

2. Reply to a negative customer review

To keep a professional and calm tone in public.

Here is a review posted on Google about my restaurant: "[paste the review without the customer's name]". What really happened according to me and my team: [version of the facts, fault admitted or not].
Write three replies of 70 words maximum: one that offers a sincere apology, a more factual one that explains the situation without blaming, and one that offers to continue in private. Do not promise any compensation that I have not specified: [possible goodwill gesture]. Never a defensive tone.

3. Service schedule for the team

To organize the week's positions.

Help me build a one-week schedule for my restaurant of [number] seats. Team: [number] people in the dining room and the kitchen, with their positions (no last names). Opening days: [days]. Services: [lunch, dinner]. Known constraints: [unavailability, weekly rest, peak days].
Propose a table by day and by service with the positions to fill. Remind me to check compliance with working hours and rest periods with my local labor regulations and my accountant: state no precise rule.

4. Daily special and menu ideas

To refresh your offering from the products available.

You are a head chef. Here are the seasonal products I have in stock or that my supplier is offering this week: [list]. My food cost budget per dish: [range]. My equipment: [oven, flat-top, fryer]. Maximum preparation time at pickup: [length].
Propose a daily special menu in three courses (starter, main, dessert) with three variations, the list of preparations to do ahead and the waste to limit. For each dish, flag the ingredients to check with the supplier: I will make the allergen list myself.

5. Social media posts

To run your restaurant's account without spending your evenings on it.

I run a [style] restaurant in [city]. Write a calendar of 10 posts for Instagram and Facebook over one month: the signature dish, the team, behind the scenes, an event, a customer testimonial that I have approved, a real offer: [offer].
For each post: text of 40 words, a visual idea to shoot with a smartphone, recommended time to post. Warm tone, no exaggeration, no quality claim that cannot be proven. No photo of a customer without consent.

6. Job description and recruiting ad

To hire a server, a prep cook or a cook.

Write a recruiting ad for a [server, prep cook, cook] position at my [type] restaurant in [city]. Contract: [type, length, hours, days off]. Duties: [list]. Skills expected: [list]. What we offer: [real elements: meals, atmosphere, growth].
Format: hook, duties, profile, conditions, how to apply. Direct and honest tone, no discriminatory criteria. Leave [to be completed] for the pay: I will set it according to the labor rules that apply.

7. Service protocol and team briefing

To align the welcome and the service before opening.

You are a restaurant manager. Create a five-minute team briefing to read before each service at my [type, number of seats] restaurant. It must contain: the specials and suggestions, the items out of stock, the important reservations (without names), the points of attention, a reminder of the attitude to have with customers.
Add a sentence reminding that, for any customer allergy question, the team must consult the official dish sheet and ask the chef, never answer from memory. Format: short list to print.

8. Message for a group or an event

To answer a request for a private event, a company meal or a birthday.

A contact asks me to book [room or restaurant] for [type of event] with [number] people on [date]. My real capacity: [number of seats, hours, possible menus, constraints]. Conditions I require: [deposit, confirmation of numbers, cancellation deadline].
Write a 150-word reply presenting what I offer, the next steps and the information I need to receive, including any allergies or diets to flag before the event. Welcoming and clear tone, with no price commitment that was not provided.

9. Inventory tracking and waste management

To build a simple tool in a spreadsheet.

I run a restaurant of [number] seats and I want to limit my food waste. Help me build a tracking table in a spreadsheet: columns (product, supplier, date received, shelf life stated by the manufacturer, quantity, planned use date, waste), formulas to flag the products to use first, color alerts.
Explain how to set it up step by step. State that the rules on temperature and traceability must be checked with my local health department and my food safety plan.

10. Answers to common customer questions

To prepare a website FAQ or an automatic booking message.

Write an FAQ for the website of my restaurant in [city] with 10 common questions: hours, reservations, groups, access, pets, payment, children, patio, takeout, special diets. Here is my real information: [hours, rules, terms].
Answers of 30 words maximum. For the allergy and diet question, write only an answer that invites the customer to tell us when booking and to talk to the team on site: do not state the presence or absence of any allergen in a dish.

Allergens: never inferred by the AI

Allergen information is a regulatory obligation that engages your responsibility. An AI knows neither your supplier, nor your exact recipe, nor the possible cross-contamination in the kitchen. Build your sheets from the labels and spec sheets of your products, have the chef review them, and check the rules in force with your official food safety authorities. Never ask the AI "does this dish contain this allergen?"

Food safety, prices and menu notices

For food safety, price display and menu notices, rely on the official texts and on your trade association. The AI's advice replaces neither your food safety plan nor an inspection.

Customer and employee data

Do not paste customer names, phone numbers, named reservations, or health or allergy information tied to an identified person. For the schedule, use positions or first names, never payroll data or medical information.

Limits

The AI offers ideas for the menu or for communication, but it does not know what your customers like or what your kitchen can produce. Always taste, test and adjust.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask the AI for the list of allergens in my dishes?

No. It can be wrong and does not know your exact products. Build this information from your spec sheets and your suppliers' labels, then have the chef validate it. Only verified information should be given to the customer.

Can AI create a complete menu?

It can suggest ideas and descriptions, but only you know your costs, your equipment and your team's skills. Use it as a source of inspiration and test every dish before adding it to the menu.

How do I reply to fake reviews with AI?

Have it draft a factual and polite reply, review it and make sure it reveals no information about the customer. For a clearly abusive review, use the reporting procedure of the platform concerned.
