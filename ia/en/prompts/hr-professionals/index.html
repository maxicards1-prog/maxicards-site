ChatGPT Prompts for HR Professionals

Ten prompts to write job postings, job descriptions, onboarding materials and internal communications faster, with respect for people.

  2. Create a job description
  3. Prepare a structured interview
  4. Onboarding plan for a new hire
  5. Write a delicate internal communication
  6. Employee satisfaction survey
  7. Prepare an annual review
  8. Write meeting minutes
  9. Design a training plan
  10. Answer a common employee question

HR teams produce a lot of documents: job postings, job descriptions, welcome guides, internal memos. An AI helps you write a first draft and structure your materials.

HR topics involve people and the law. The prompts below avoid personal data and remind you to check the applicable law. They are for saving time on writing, never for deciding in place of a professional.

  • Work with anonymized cases.
  • Have legal texts validated by a professional.
  • Never give the AI a decision about a person.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Write an attractive job posting

To publish a clear and inclusive ad.

You are a recruiting manager. Write a job posting for the position of [job title] at a company in [industry, size, location].
Details: responsibilities [list], required skills [list], conditions [contract type, working hours, pay or range, benefits].
Structure: company overview, responsibilities, ideal profile, conditions, hiring process. Inclusive writing (neutral wording), requirements strictly tied to the job, no discriminatory criteria. 250 words maximum.

2. Create a job description

To clearly describe an existing or new position.

Help me write the job description for [job title]. Reports to: [role]. Main responsibilities: [unsorted list].
Organize them into blocks: overall purpose, main duties, occasional duties, technical skills, soft skills, working conditions, success indicators.
Use action verbs and separate the essential from the nice to have. Ask me any questions you need first.

3. Prepare a structured interview

So that every candidate answers the same questions.

I am hiring a [job title]. Key skills: [list]. Build a structured interview guide: 8 questions (behavioral and situational), what a good answer looks like, and a scoring grid from 1 to 4 for each skill.
The questions must be strictly about professional matters. Remind me of the topics I must not bring up (family situation, health, origin, opinions, etc.).

4. Onboarding plan for a new hire

To organize an employee's first 3 months.

Create an onboarding plan over [length] for a new hire in the role of [role] in [department]. Team size: [number]. Tools used: [list].
Format: a table by period (before arrival, first week, first month, three months) with actions, owner, the goal of each step, and a feedback checkpoint.
Add a checklist for the manager and a checklist for the new hire.

5. Write a delicate internal communication

To announce a change with clarity and tact.

Write an internal memo announcing: [change: reorganization, new policy, schedule change]. Facts: [dates, reasons, consequences]. Audience: [affected employees].
Transparent and respectful tone, no corporate doublespeak, 200 words maximum. Anticipate employees' likely questions with answers for the internal FAQ. Point out what to validate with the legal department or employee representatives.

6. Employee satisfaction survey

To measure workplace climate in a useful way.

Create an anonymous 12-question survey to measure team satisfaction at [company, size]. Themes: workload, management, communication, career growth, work-life balance.
Mix 1 to 5 scales and 2 open questions. Word the questions neutrally, without bias. Add tips to guarantee anonymity and present the results.

7. Prepare an annual review

To guide a manager through the performance review.

I am [HR role] and I am preparing a guide for managers on the annual review. Propose a 5-step flow, 10 open questions for the employee, advice on giving constructive criticism, and common mistakes to avoid.
The guide must fit on 2 pages, with a practical tone. Remind me that the content must follow the company's agreements and procedures.

8. Write meeting minutes

To turn notes into clear minutes.

Here are my meeting notes, without names of people involved in individual situations: [paste the notes].
Write the minutes: attendees (roles), topics discussed, decisions, actions with owner and due date, open items. Add nothing that is not in my notes. Format: clear list, 1 page maximum.

9. Design a training plan

To structure skill development.

Help me build a training plan for [team or role] that needs to acquire [skill]. Current level: [description]. Budget: [budget]. Time available: [length].
Propose a path in stages combining training, practice and follow-up, with a learning indicator for each stage. Remind me to check possible funding with a relevant organization.

10. Answer a common employee question

To feed an internal FAQ.

I manage HR for a company of [size]. Write an internal FAQ of 8 questions on the topic: [remote work, time off, expense reports, training].
For each answer: 60 words maximum, clear tone, and tell me which details I need to fill in according to our internal rules. Do not cite any specific legal rule: instead, tell me what I should check in the labor laws or collective agreement that apply to us.

No automated sorting of people

Do not use an AI to sort or rank candidates on discriminatory criteria, or to decide on a hire, a sanction or a dismissal. Decisions about people must stay human, based on objective job-related criteria. Discrimination is prohibited at every stage of hiring, and AI tools can reproduce bias.

Data you should never paste

Do not enter named resumes, employee files, or health, payroll, family or union data. This data is protected by privacy laws. Anonymize cases and work on general situations. Check with your data protection officer.

Limits

Labor law changes and depends on your location and on any collective agreement. Have any legal document (policy, warning, termination) validated by a lawyer. The AI helps you write; it does not take on your responsibility.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use AI to select candidates?

Not to decide on your own. Help with writing or organizing is possible, but sorting people with an AI carries risks of bias and transparency obligations. Keep a human evaluation and objective job-related criteria.

What should I do with the resumes I receive?

Do not paste them as is into a consumer tool: they contain personal data. Check your company's policy and the tool's commitments before any use.

Are the generated texts compliant with labor law?

Not necessarily. The AI can be wrong or cite an outdated rule. Have sensitive documents reviewed by a lawyer, and compare them with your collective agreements, company policies and the labor laws that apply to you.
