ChatGPT Prompts for Accountants

Ten prompts to save time on emails, summaries and client education, while keeping control of the numbers and the tax texts.

In short

  2. Explain results to a business owner
  3. Summary note on a tax topic
  4. Internal year-end close procedure
  5. Answer a client question on a common topic
  6. Engagement letter: structure and clarity
  7. Analyze a cash flow table
  8. Welcome email and client onboarding plan
  9. Train a junior colleague
  10. Educational newsletter for clients

Between chasing documents, explaining results to business owners and writing internal notes, an accountant spends a lot of time writing. An AI can prepare clear texts and document structures, not do the accounting for you.

The prompts below call for anonymized cases and numbers that you provide yourself. The AI can make mistakes in calculation and on tax matters: every amount and every reference to a rule must be checked.

  • Anonymize files before pasting anything.
  • Provide the numbers, do not make the AI guess them.
  • Check every tax text against the official source.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Chase a client for missing documents

To get the supporting documents without annoying the client.

You are a staff accountant at an accounting firm. Write a follow-up email to a client who owns a [type of business] to obtain missing documents before year-end close: [list: bank statements, sales invoices, purchase invoices, expense reports].
Period concerned: [period]. Desired deadline: [date]. Explain in two sentences why these documents are needed, suggest a simple way to send them ([firm's tool]) and stay courteous. Three versions: first reminder, second firmer reminder, 160-character text message reminder.

2. Explain results to a business owner

To make financial statements understandable.

I am presenting a company's results to an owner who is not an accountant. Here are the figures I have validated myself: revenue [amount], main expenses [list with amounts], net income [amount], cash [amount], change versus last year [change].
Write an explanation in plain language of 250 words: what these numbers show, three points of attention, three questions the owner should ask. Add no numbers and do no calculations: use exactly my data and flag what you cannot conclude.

3. Summary note on a tax topic

To prepare a note to validate, from an official text that you paste.

Here is an excerpt of an official text that I copied myself from the source (the tax authority's website or the law): [paste the excerpt with its reference and date].
Write a one-page summary note for a client who owns a [business structure]: the principle, the conditions, the points of attention, the questions to ask. Rely only on the excerpt. If information is missing or the text refers to other rules, say so at the end of the note under the heading "To verify at the source".

4. Internal year-end close procedure

To formalize the steps of a recurring engagement at the firm.

Help me write the internal annual close procedure for a [type of client: small service business, retail, nonprofit] file at our firm of [number] staff. Our current way of doing it: [unsorted steps].
Turn them into a procedure: objective, owner of each step, documents to obtain, checks to perform (bank reconciliations, cutoff, inventory, fixed assets), approval points, indicative timeline. Format: table, then checklist. Specify where a partner's decision is needed.

5. Answer a client question on a common topic

To answer a question about expenses or tax treatment quickly without improvising.

A client who owns a business asks me: "[question: can I deduct this expense, which structure should I choose, how are my reimbursements treated]". Context of their company, without names: [structure, activity, size, tax regime].
Write a written answer in three parts: what I must check in their file, the general points of the answer to validate, the risks if we get it wrong. State no precise rate, cap or threshold: tell me for each one where to verify it (tax authority website, applicable text).

6. Engagement letter: structure and clarity

To clarify the scope of an engagement with a new client.

I am preparing an engagement letter for a new client [type: tradesperson, professional practice, real estate holding company] who wants an engagement of [bookkeeping, financial statements, payroll, advisory]. My firm applies these terms: [scope, frequency of exchanges, tools, client responsibilities].
Suggest the structure of the letter and write in clear language the sections "what we do," "what we do not do," "what we expect from the client," "schedule of exchanges." Add a list of clauses that my firm will need to have validated by its professional body or its counsel, without drafting their legal content.

7. Analyze a cash flow table

To spot trends and prepare questions for the client.

Here is a monthly cash flow table that I have anonymized: [paste the lines with amounts: receipts, disbursements, balance, over 12 months].
You are a financial analyst. Describe the trends, the risky months, the items that vary the most, and suggest five questions to ask the client to understand these variations. Do not recalculate the totals: work with the figures provided and flag any apparent inconsistency that I must check in the entries.

8. Welcome email and client onboarding plan

To structure the arrival of a new file.

I am an accountant welcoming a new client [profile: new business, taking over from another firm]. Write a 150-word welcome email and a 30-day onboarding plan: documents to obtain, access to set up, first meeting, calendar of upcoming deadlines [list of deadlines that I provide].
Reassuring and professional tone. Do not invent any tax deadline: use only the ones I have given you and mark the others as [to be confirmed on the tax authority's website].

9. Train a junior colleague

To build practical exercises from anonymous cases.

You are a trainer at an accounting firm. Create three practical cases for a junior colleague on the topic [topic: bank reconciliation, sales tax collected and deductible, depreciation]. Each case: a short problem with fictional data, the questions, a detailed solution, common mistakes.
State which texts or standards the colleague should rely on, without quoting their exact content: I will have the references checked. Level: [beginner, intermediate]. I must review the solutions before use.

10. Educational newsletter for clients

To inform your clients regularly and usefully.

Write an accounting firm newsletter of 300 words for small business and self-employed clients. Topic: [topic: quarterly deadlines, organizing your invoices well, preparing for year-end]. Items I validate: [list of verified points, dates and information].
Structure: subject line (3 options), introduction, three practical tips, a reminder of the deadlines that I provide, a call to contact us. Clear tone, no jargon. Add no tax rule that was not provided and include the mandatory unsubscribe notice.

Numbers and texts to verify

An AI can make calculation errors and cite an outdated rate, threshold or date. No amount and no tax rule generated by the AI should be used without checking. Verify at the source: your tax authority's official website and the text of the law. Give it the validated numbers rather than asking it to calculate them, and keep a record of your sources in the file.

Professional secrecy and privacy

Your clients' data (identity, bank accounts, pay, personal wealth) is confidential. Do not paste it into a consumer tool: anonymize, round, remove names and numbers. Check the rules of your professional body and your firm's policy, and remember that privacy laws expect a specific purpose for any processing of personal data.

Responsibility

The advice given to the client engages the firm, not the tool. Review, sign and stand behind every document. The AI helps write and structure, not decide on an accounting or tax treatment.

Limits

Regulations change: a recent text may not be known to the AI. For a particular situation, rely on official guidance and your peers.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI make a journal entry or file a return?

It can explain a principle or propose a control structure, but the entry and the return are up to you. It can be wrong on an account, a rate or a date. Check everything with your accounting tools and the official texts.

Can I send a client's general ledger to the AI?

No, not as is. It contains confidential and personal data. If you need to analyze numbers, anonymize them and stay within the rules of your firm and your profession.

How do I cite a tax text without risk of error?

Copy the excerpt yourself from the official source and paste it into the prompt, with its reference. Ask the AI to rely only on that text and review the result by comparing it with the source.
