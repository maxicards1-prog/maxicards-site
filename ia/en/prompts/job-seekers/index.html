ChatGPT Prompts for Job Seekers

Ten prompts to showcase your background, tailor your resume to each posting and prepare for interviews.

In short

  1. Improve your resume bullet points
  2. Tailor your resume to a job posting
  3. Write a cover letter
  4. Simulate a job interview
  5. Prepare your answer to "tell me about yourself"
  6. Answer the tough questions
  7. Prepare your questions for the recruiter
  8. Write a cold application message
  9. Follow up after an interview
  10. Take stock of your skills

Looking for a job means writing a lot of personalized text: resumes, cover letters, follow-up messages. An AI helps you structure, reword and practice, as long as you give it your real experience.

Never ask it to invent an experience or a degree: a recruiter will spot it quickly, and it is risky for you. The prompts below highlight what you have actually done.

  • Paste the job posting to tailor your documents.
  • Give facts: responsibilities, results, tools.
  • Proofread and keep your personal tone.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Improve your resume bullet points

To make your experience more concrete and readable.

You are an experienced recruiter. Here is one experience from my resume: [job title, company, duration, tasks performed].
Rewrite it as 3 to 5 bullet points starting with an action verb, focused on what I did and on results. Do not invent any numbers: if data is missing, ask me so I can fill it in.
Constraints: short sentences, no empty jargon, a one-page resume.

2. Tailor your resume to a job posting

Before applying, to bring out the right keywords.

Here is the job posting: [paste the posting]
Here is my current resume: [paste your resume, without contact details]
Compare them. List the skills in the posting that I already have, those I have but do not highlight enough, and those I am missing.
Then propose specific changes to the resume, without adding any experience I have not had.

3. Write a cover letter

To get a personalized first draft from your own material.

You are a job search coach. Write a cover letter for the [job title] position at [company], 250 words maximum.
My real strengths: [3 experiences or skills]. What I know about the company: [specific details]. Why this job motivates me: [sincere reason].
Structure: opening hook, what I bring, why this company, conclusion offering a conversation. Professional and natural tone, no stock phrases. I will review it and adapt it to my style.

4. Simulate a job interview

To practice in conditions close to the real thing.

You are a recruiter for the [job title] position at a [industry] company. Run an interview with me: ask one question at a time, wait for my answer, then give brief feedback (strengths, one thing to improve) before the next question.
Alternate between questions about my background, situational questions and motivation questions. After 8 questions, give me a summary with three areas to improve. Start when you are ready.

5. Prepare your answer to "tell me about yourself"

To build a clear two-minute spoken introduction.

Help me prepare my interview introduction. My background: [education and experience]. The target job: [job title]. My main strength: [strength].
Propose a two-minute spoken introduction, about 250 words, with a past, present, future structure. Write it in a spoken style, with short sentences and no empty phrases.
Add three possible opening lines.

6. Answer the tough questions

To prepare your sensitive points: gap in your resume, departure, salary.

I have an interview for [job title]. Here is a delicate point in my background: [gap of [duration], layoff, career change, lack of experience].
Suggest three honest and positive ways to talk about it, without lying or criticizing a former employer. For each, give an example sentence. Also tell me what it is better not to say.

7. Prepare your questions for the recruiter

To show your interest at the end of the interview.

I have an interview for [job title] at [company], in the [industry] industry. Suggest 10 relevant questions to ask the recruiter, sorted into four categories: the role and its responsibilities, the team, growth, and the hiring process.
Avoid questions whose answer is in the job posting. For each question, say what it helps me understand.

8. Write a cold application message

To contact a company that has not posted an opening.

Write a cold application message of 120 words maximum for [company], addressed to [recipient's role]. My profile: [profile in one sentence]. What I am looking for: [type of job]. One specific thing that connects me to the company: [detail].
Direct and respectful tone, one clear request (a 15-minute conversation), no generic phrasing. Also suggest a short subject line.

9. Follow up after an interview

To say thank you and follow up without being pushy.

Write a thank-you message after an interview for [job title] that took place on [date] with [interviewer's role]. Topics we discussed that stood out to me: [points]. I want to reinforce one strength: [strength].
Length: 80 words. Warm and professional tone. Offer a second follow-up version to send [number] days later if I have no reply.

10. Take stock of your skills

To clarify a plan or a career change before applying.

I want to take stock of my job search. My background: [summary]. What I like to do: [list]. What I want to avoid: [list]. Constraints: [location, minimum salary, schedule].
First ask me 8 questions to get to know me better. Then propose 5 consistent careers or industries, with the transferable skills for each and any training worth checking with a career counselor or your local employment office.

Stay honest

Do not invent a job, a degree or a number. A reference check or a precise interview question is enough to reveal a lie. Ask the AI to better express what you did, not to create what you did not do.

Personalize

Recruiters recognize generic letters. Always add a specific detail about the company and a sincere reason. Read aloud: if a sentence does not sound like you, rewrite it.

Protect your data

Remove from your resume the information the AI does not need: address, phone number, email, date of birth. Do not paste confidential documents from your current employer. If a posting contains people's contact details, do not copy them.

Limits

The AI does not know the local market or the exact culture of a company. For employment law, benefits or assistance programs, talk to a career counselor or your local employment office. Rights and procedures change: always check official information.

Frequently asked questions

Can recruiters tell a letter was written by AI?

Often, when it is generic. A letter with specific facts, a real connection to the company and your own way of speaking works better. Use the AI to structure and proofread, then rewrite in your own words.

Can I paste my full resume into the AI?

It is better to remove your contact details and sensitive information before pasting it. The professional content is enough for the AI to help you. Also read the tool's terms of use.

Can AI choose a career for me?

It can suggest ideas based on your answers, not decide for you. Test these ideas against reality: job profiles, job shadowing, conversations with professionals and a career counselor.
