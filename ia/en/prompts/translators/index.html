ChatGPT Prompts for Translators

Ten prompts to help around translation: terminology, proofreading, organization and client relations, without exposing the documents entrusted to you.

In short

  1. Build a subject-matter glossary
  2. Second look at a translation
  3. Rephrase for a specific register
  4. Quote and delivery estimate
  5. Email of questions to the client
  6. Check terminology consistency
  7. Adapt a text to another culture
  8. Write your professional profile
  9. Plan a large team project
  10. Proofread an already translated text

A translator does more than translate: they research terminology, proofread, organize projects and talk to clients. An AI can assist you with these side tasks and give you a second pair of eyes, provided you respect the confidentiality of the documents.

The prompts below use non-confidential or fictional excerpts. A client's text should only be submitted to a tool if your contract and the client allow it. You remain the author and the guarantor of the delivered translation.

  • Check the confidentiality clauses of each assignment.
  • Anonymize the excerpts you paste.
  • Review every AI suggestion before using it.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Build a subject-matter glossary

To prepare the terminology of a technical assignment.

You are a terminologist. I am preparing a translation from [source language] into [target language] in the field of [field: medical, legal, IT, industry]. Here is a list of terms taken from the document, with no confidential passage: [list].
For each term, suggest the usual translation, a possible variant, an example of usage and a confidence level. Present a table. Clearly flag the ambiguous or sector-specific terms that I must check in official sources or in the client's resources, without inventing any reference.

2. Second look at a translation

To spot errors of meaning before delivery.

Here is a short non-confidential passage and its translation. Source text ([language]): [paste the passage]. My translation ([language]): [paste the translation].
Compare them sentence by sentence. Point out possible mistranslations, omissions, additions, false friends, inconsistencies in register and punctuation. For each comment: excerpt, suspected problem, possible alternative. State your degree of certainty: I decide on any change and I will check the meaning in the full context of the document.

3. Rephrase for a specific register

To adjust the tone of a translation to its audience.

Here is a passage already translated into [target language]: [paste a non-confidential excerpt]. It is intended for [audience: general public, specialists, customers of a luxury brand, software users]. The tone requested by the client: [tone].
Suggest three rewordings, respectively more formal, more natural and more concise, keeping the exact meaning. For each, say what changes in terms of register and flag what might not fit the client's style guide. Do not change numbers, proper names or terms in the glossary: [required terms].

4. Quote and delivery estimate

To present a clear offer to a potential client.

Write the text of a quote for a translation assignment from [source language] into [target language], field [field], volume [word count], desired delivery on [date]. Services included: [translation, proofreading, formatting, glossary]. Specific elements: [file format, required CAT tool].
Structure: scope, method, schedule, confidentiality terms, revision after delivery, information to be provided by the client. Leave [to be completed] for the rate and payment terms, which I will set myself. Clear and professional tone.

5. Email of questions to the client

To resolve ambiguities before translating.

I am starting a translation of a [type of document] from [source language] into [target language] and I am running into ambiguities: [list: undefined acronyms, unclear passages, terms with no equivalent, unclear target audience, brand or product names].
Write a polite and efficient email to the client, 150 words maximum, that asks these questions in a numbered format, proposes hypotheses to confirm and explains the impact of the answers on the translation. Reveal no confidential content in the email: refer to passages by page or section number.

6. Check terminology consistency

To check a long document before delivery.

Here is a list of terms with the translations I used in different parts of a document: [table: source term, translations found, section number]. My reference glossary: [list].
Spot the inconsistencies: the same term translated several ways, a glossary term not respected, a different translation depending on the section. For each case, suggest the solution to keep and the list of places to fix. Present the result as a table and flag any decision that must be validated by the client.

7. Adapt a text to another culture

For localizing marketing content or interfaces.

I am localizing content from [source language] into [target language] for the [country] market. Here are elements of the text: [slogans, idiomatic expressions, cultural references, units, date formats]. Audience: [description].
For each element, suggest a natural adaptation, explain why a literal translation does not work, and flag the risks of misunderstanding or negative connotation. State what must be validated by a native speaker from the country or by the client. Do not replace any brand name or legal element without my approval.

8. Write your professional profile

To write your profile on a platform or a directory.

Write a professional bio of 150 words for my freelance translator profile. My languages: [languages]. My fields: [fields]. My real education and experience: [exact details]. My tools: [CAT software]. My way of working: [proofreading, deadlines, communication].
Sober and credible tone, no superlatives. Do not invent any certification, client or year of experience. Suggest two different hooks and a short 40-word version for an email signature.

9. Plan a large team project

To organize a translation split among several people.

I am coordinating the translation of a [word count]-word document from [source language] into [target language] with [number] translators and a reviewer. Deadline: [date]. Tools: [CAT tool, spreadsheet].
Propose a project plan: splitting the document, assignment, creating the shared glossary, style instructions to circulate, a schedule with milestones, a proofreading and quality control process, handling client feedback. Also provide a five-line instruction template to send to the translators, with a confidentiality reminder.

10. Proofread an already translated text

For a linguistic check of your own text, outside confidential content.

Here is a text of [type: public article, presentation material] that I translated into [target language] and whose content can be shared: [paste the text].
Do a linguistic proofreading only: spelling, grammar, punctuation, repetitions, heavy sentences, anglicisms or calques if the source language is [language]. Present the comments as a list (excerpt, problem, suggested fix) without rewriting the whole text. Do not change the meaning: I validate each correction myself.

Confidentiality of client documents

The texts your clients entrust to you are often confidential (contracts, medical records, financial data, unreleased products). Before submitting an excerpt to an AI tool, check your contract, the confidentiality clause and the client's agreement. When in doubt, send nothing: work with anonymized or fictional excerpts, term lists or your own rewordings. Also read the tool's terms of use on data retention.

Personal data

A document may contain names, addresses or sensitive information: remove them before any use. Privacy laws say that processing personal data must have a specific purpose and be limited to what is necessary.

Your responsibility

You remain responsible for the text you deliver. An AI can offer a mistranslation with total confidence, or invent an equivalent or a term. Check terminology in reliable sources (client glossaries, official resources, specialized dictionaries). If your client or your professional association regulates the use of AI, apply those rules.

Limits

The AI does not know the full context of your assignment or your client's style guide. It serves as a second pair of eyes and an organizing assistant, not as a substitute for your expertise as a translator.

Frequently asked questions

Can I paste a client's document into an AI tool?

Only if your contract and the client allow it and the data is protected. When in doubt, hold back: use anonymized excerpts or work on terminology and organization without sending the content.

Can AI replace a computer-assisted translation tool?

No. A CAT tool manages translation memories, glossaries and the consistency of a project. The AI can occasionally supplement term research or proofreading, but it replaces neither the tool nor your judgment.

How do I verify terminology suggested by the AI?

Compare it with the client's glossary, the official resources of the field and reference texts in the target language. Keep a term only if you find it in a reliable source that you have consulted.
