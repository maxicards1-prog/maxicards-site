ChatGPT Prompts for Small Business Owners

Ten prompts to save time on communication, quotes and customer relations, without losing your own way of speaking.

In short

  2. Reply to a customer review
  3. Write a quote for a job
  4. Social media posts
  5. Prepare a promotion
  6. Job ad for an apprentice
  7. Newsletter for loyal customers
  8. Care tips for your customers
  9. Organize inventory management
  10. Handle a complaint

Between the workshop or the shop, the customers and the paperwork, there is little time left to write. An AI helps you write your listings, your replies to reviews and your quotes in a few minutes.

You know your trade better than any AI: give it your real information and always review the result. A few minutes of proofreading is often enough to get a text that is ready to use.

  • State your business, your town and your customers.
  • Give your real prices, hours and services.
  • Publish only what is accurate.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Describe a product in your shop

For your online product pages or your price tags.

You are a copywriter for an independent [type of shop] in [town]. Write an 80-word description for: [product]. Real features: [material, size, origin, how it is made, care]. Customers: [profile].
Warm, honest tone, no exaggeration or superlatives. Highlight what is true: local production, craftsmanship, materials. Finish with a sentence about care or use.

2. Reply to a customer review

To answer online reviews calmly and professionally.

Here is a review left on my online listing: "[review]". What really happened: [facts]. What I can offer: [goodwill gesture or fix].
Write two replies of 50 to 70 words: a warm version and a more reserved one. Polite tone, not aggressive, no over-explaining. If the review is positive, thank the customer in a personal way. Do not make anything up.

3. Write a quote for a job

To present a service clearly to a homeowner.

I am a [trade: plumber, carpenter, painter] and I need to present a quote to a homeowner. Work: [description]. Materials: [list]. Duration: [length]. Price: [price].
Write a clear description of the work, the materials, what is included and excluded, the terms and the timeline. Add a list of the mandatory information to check with my accountant. Simple language, no jargon.

4. Social media posts

To keep your page active without spending hours.

Write 5 short posts for the [network] page of my [shop or workshop] in [town]. Topics: [new arrival, behind the scenes, tip, testimonial, opening hours]. My tone: [warm, direct, funny].
Each post: 40 to 70 words, an idea for a photo to take with my phone, 3 local keywords. No false promises or fake promotions.

5. Prepare a promotion

To announce a clear and honest offer.

I want to run a promotion: [description: 15% off a range, welcome offer, end-of-line clearance]. Dates: [dates]. Conditions: [conditions]. My customers: [profile].
Write: a poster (headline and 3 lines), a message for the shop window, a text for social media and a text message of under 160 characters. Remind me to check the rules on price and discount advertising.

6. Job ad for an apprentice

To recruit an apprentice or a seasonal employee.

I am looking for an [apprentice or employee] for my [type of business] in [town]. Tasks: [list]. Hours: [schedule]. Training I can offer: [details].
Write a clear 150-word ad: who we are, what the person will learn, what we ask for, how to apply. Welcoming tone, requirements strictly tied to the job. I will check the available programs and the contract with the relevant organizations.

7. Newsletter for loyal customers

To stay in touch with your customers.

Write a short monthly newsletter for the customers of my [shop]. This month's content: [new arrivals, event, seasonal tip, opening hours].
Structure: catchy subject line, greeting, 3 short paragraphs, an invitation to visit the shop, sign-off. 150 words, warm tone. Remind me to offer a way to unsubscribe and to write only to customers who have agreed to receive emails.

8. Care tips for your customers

To build loyalty with useful tips you can hand out in person.

I sell [product or service]. Write a care sheet to hand to the customer: 8 simple tips, mistakes to avoid, and signs that show they should contact me.
Format: one page, short headings, clear sentences. Give only general, cautious advice: I will add the technical details specific to my trade and my products.

9. Organize inventory management

To think through a simple restocking system.

I manage a stock of [type of products] in a [size] shop. My problems: [stockouts, overstock, expired products]. My sales vary with [season, day, events].
Propose a simple method to track my stock with a spreadsheet: columns to include, reorder threshold, counting frequency, useful indicators. Explain how to set it up in one week, without paid software.

10. Handle a complaint

To respond calmly to an unhappy customer.

A customer makes the following complaint: [summary, no name]. The facts: [what happened]. My position: [what I can accept or refuse].
Write a courteous written reply of 120 words that acknowledges the inconvenience, explains the situation, offers a specific solution and a timeframe. Add two tips for the face-to-face conversation. For consumer rights, tell me what I should check with an official source.

What to check

Review every text before publishing it: prices, hours, materials, warranties. An AI can add a false detail with total confidence. Avoid any claim you cannot prove ("the best," "lifetime guarantee," unjustified origin or label): it can expose you to penalties.

Data not to paste

Do not paste customers' names, addresses, phone numbers or purchase histories. To answer a review or a complaint, describe the situation without identifying the person. Your business information (margins, suppliers, special rates) stays confidential.

Limits

The rules on quotes, sales, warranties and price display are specific. Check with your local small business association, chamber of commerce or consumer protection agency. The way you talk to your customers is your strength: edit the texts so they sound like you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay to use these tools?

The free versions of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are enough for these prompts, with usage limits. The terms change: check each service's website for the current offer.

Can the AI reply to my customer reviews on its own?

It can prepare a reply, but it is up to you to review and publish it. A review calls for sincerity: adapt the text to the real facts and to your own way of speaking.

Can I trust it with my quotes?

It helps write the description, not set prices or guarantee legal compliance. Check the mandatory information, the calculations and the payment terms before sending anything to a customer.
