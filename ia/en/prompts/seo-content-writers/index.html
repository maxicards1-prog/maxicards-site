ChatGPT Prompts for SEO and Web Writing

Ten prompts to plan, write and edit web content that is useful to readers first and to search engines second.

In short

  1. Understand search intent
  2. Detailed article outline
  3. Write an introduction that hooks
  4. Title tags and meta descriptions
  5. Rewrite a text to be clearer
  6. Create a useful FAQ
  7. Proofread and edit an article
  8. Optimize an existing article
  9. Descriptive product page
  10. Internal linking ideas

Good web content answers a real question, precisely. An AI helps you structure an article, find angles and proofread, but it replaces neither your expertise nor fact-checking.

Search engines favor content that is useful, original and reliable. The prompts below therefore push the AI to rely on your information, not to produce generic text.

  • Define the reader and their question before writing.
  • Provide your own information, examples and sources.
  • Check every fact before publishing.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Understand search intent

Before writing, to know what the reader expects.

I want to write an article that answers the query "[query]". My site is aimed at [audience].
Analyze the search intent: what is the searcher looking for (to learn, compare, buy, solve a problem)? What questions do they have? What level of knowledge do they have? Which subtopics must the article cover?
Present the result as a list, then suggest 3 possible angles to stand out without repeating what everyone else writes.

2. Detailed article outline

To start from a clear structure.

You are a web writer specializing in [field]. Create an outline for a [number]-word article on: [topic]. Main keyword: [keyword]. Reader: [profile].
Format: H1 title, introduction (promise in two sentences), 5 to 7 H2 headings with, under each, the points to cover and the type of proof to provide (example, statistic to source, testimonial).
Finish with a conclusion with a call to action and 3 questions for an FAQ.

3. Write an introduction that hooks

To start strong without cliches.

Write 3 introductions of 70 to 90 words for an article titled "[title]", aimed at [reader]. Reader's problem: [problem].
Each version uses a different approach: direct question, concrete situation, precise observation (no invented numbers). Clearly state what the article offers. Avoid phrases like "in today's digital age" or "in an ever-changing world".

4. Title tags and meta descriptions

To optimize your snippets in search results.

For a page about [topic] with the main keyword "[keyword]", suggest 5 title tags of 50 to 60 characters and 5 meta descriptions of 140 to 160 characters.
The keyword must appear naturally; each description must make people want to click without a misleading promise. Count the characters of each suggestion and note it in parentheses.

5. Rewrite a text to be clearer

To lighten a heavy or technical paragraph.

Rewrite the following text so that a [profile] reader understands it on the first read: [paste the text]
Constraints: sentences of 20 words maximum, active voice, technical terms explained in a few words, no information removed or added. Keep a [tone] tone. List the main changes at the end of your answer.

6. Create a useful FAQ

To answer readers' real questions.

For a page about [topic], suggest 8 questions that people really ask, sorted from most common to most specific. For the first 4, write an answer of 40 to 60 words, clear, direct, no filler.
For answers that need verification (regulations, statistics), write "to verify" and say what type of source to consult.

7. Proofread and edit an article

For an editorial review before publishing.

You are a copy editor. Read the following article: [paste the article]
Point out: spelling and grammar mistakes, repetitions, confusing sentences, passages that are too long, claims without proof, and information that seems doubtful and should be checked. Present a table: excerpt, problem, suggested fix. Do not rewrite the whole thing.

8. Optimize an existing article

To update content that is losing traffic.

Here is an article published [length of time] ago on [topic]: [paste the article]
Audit it: outdated parts, reader questions not covered, structure to improve, missing examples, possible internal links to [pages of the site]. Propose an update plan ranked by priority. Do not change the facts: tell me which ones I need to verify.

9. Descriptive product page

To describe a product without copying the manufacturer.

Write a 150-word product description for [product]. Real features: [list]. Target audience: [profile]. Main use: [use].
Structure: hook, concrete benefits, features as a list, who it is for, limitations to know. No unproven promises, no empty superlatives. Original text, [tone] tone.

10. Internal linking ideas

To connect your pages logically.

Here is the list of pages on my site with their topic: [list of titles and topics]. Here is a new article: [title and summary].
Suggest 5 internal links to place in this article (target page, natural anchor sentence, recommended spot) and 3 existing pages that should link to it. Choose descriptive anchors, never "click here".

Check the facts

An AI can cite a study, a number or a rule that does not exist. Every factual claim must be verified with a reliable source before publishing. For health, finance or legal topics, have a specialist review the content.

Write for the reader, not for an engine

Mass-publishing generic texts adds no value. Add your experience, examples and data you own. Useful, original content remains the best investment. Avoid repeating the keyword artificially.

Data not to paste

Do not paste confidential client information, unpublished strategies or personal data. Also protect licensed content that you are not allowed to share with a third party.

Limits

The AI reproduces common phrasing: you will need to personalize it. It does not know your real search data; use your tracking tools to see the queries and performance of your site.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google penalize content written with AI?

What matters is how useful and good the content is for the reader. Mass-produced text with no added value risks ranking poorly. Content that is reviewed, verified and enriched by your expertise is not penalized for that reason alone.

How do I avoid generic text?

Provide your examples, your data, your experience and the precise profile of the reader. Ask the AI to structure or reword, then add what only you can say: real cases, nuances, field feedback.

Can AI choose my keywords?

It can suggest ideas, but real search volumes come from tracking tools. Use it to explore angles and questions, then check with your site statistics.
