ChatGPT Prompts for Parents

Ten prompts to organize family life, support homework and prepare your conversations with school, without ever exposing your children's information.

In short

  1. Organize the family's week
  2. Help with homework without doing it for them
  3. Explain a concept to a child
  4. Invent activities based on time and mood
  5. Plan the week's meals
  6. Write a message to the teacher
  7. Prepare a parent-teacher conference
  8. Set up routines
  9. Talk about a delicate subject with your child
  10. Set limits on family screen use

Between schedules, homework, activities and messages from school, a parent's mental load is heavy. An AI can help you organize, find ideas and explain better without your child just copying an answer.

These prompts are for you, the adult. Describe situations in general terms, with no first name, no school name and no photo: your children did not choose to be in an online tool.

  • Use "my 9-year-old," not their name.
  • For homework, ask for hints, not answers.
  • Check health and education information with professionals.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Organize the family's week

To spread out schedules, trips and chores without forgetting anything.

I am the parent of [number] children aged [ages]. Here are our fixed constraints for the week: [work hours, school, activities, trips]. Chores to share: [groceries, meals, cleaning, appointments].
Propose a weekly schedule as a table, with the transition times of the day, a fair split between [number] adults, a buffer for the unexpected and a time of free time for the family. Flag the time slots where there are conflicts and suggest solutions.

2. Help with homework without doing it for them

To guide a child who is stuck, while letting them search.

My [age, grade] child is stuck on this [subject] homework: [problem copied]. They have already tried: [what they attempted].
Do not give the answer. Suggest 4 questions I can ask them, increasingly guided, a starting hint, and a way for them to check their own answer. Also explain the concept to me in simple terms so that I understand before helping. I will present your suggestions to them in my own words.

3. Explain a concept to a child

To find words suited to their age.

My [age] child asks me: "[child's question]". I am not sure I can explain it well.
Give me an explanation on three levels: one simple sentence, an analogy with everyday life, a small example or an experiment to do at home with common materials. Add common misunderstandings and 3 questions to check that they understood. Say whether the subject is controversial or whether experts disagree.

4. Invent activities based on time and mood

For weekends and vacations, without a big budget.

I am looking for activities for [number] children aged [ages] for [length] in [type of place: apartment, house with a yard, city]. Budget: [amount]. Weather: [weather]. Interests: [list].
Suggest 10 ideas sorted by preparation time, with materials needed, duration, the children's level of independence, and a variation for the youngest or oldest. Point out the safety precautions to plan for crafts or outdoor activities.

5. Plan the week's meals

To balance, save time and reduce waste.

Create a meal plan for [length] for a family of [number] people including children aged [ages]. Cooking time on weeknights: [length]. Allergies or diets to respect: [general list]. What we already have: [foods]. Budget: [amount].
Give the plan, the shopping list grouped by aisle and an idea for prep ahead on the weekend. For questions about the child's diet or allergies, remind me to ask a doctor or a dietitian.

6. Write a message to the teacher

To communicate with school clearly and courteously.

I want to write to my child's teacher ([grade] class) about: [topic: a difficulty, an absence, a question about instructions, a meeting request]. Facts: [precise details, no names]. What I would like: [request].
Write a message of 120 words maximum, courteous, factual, without blame, with a clear request and a proposed time slot. Add a shorter version for the school planner or messaging app. I will add the names myself and review before sending.

7. Prepare a parent-teacher conference

To ask the right questions without stress.

I am meeting the teaching team about my [age, grade] child in [length]. What I notice at home: [general observations]. What I would like to understand: [points].
Prepare: 8 open questions to ask, a way to describe my observations without blaming, the information to ask for about the support available at the school, and a list of what I should write down during the meeting. State that I will verify the programs with the school.

8. Set up routines

To calm mornings, homework time and bedtime.

I want to improve the following routine: [morning, bedtime, homework] for my [age] child. Current difficulties: [description]. What we have already tried: [list].
Propose a routine in simple steps, a visual chart to make together, three ways to involve the child in the choices, and a way to react calmly when they refuse. Make no diagnosis: if the difficulties persist or worry me, tell me which health or education professional to turn to.

9. Talk about a delicate subject with your child

To prepare a hard conversation: conflict, loss, bullying, screens.

I want to talk to my [age] child about [topic: screens, a fight between friends, a family change, bullying]. Context: [general description]. What worries me: [concerns].
Help me prepare this conversation: right time and place, opening sentences, open questions, how to listen without interrupting, what it is better not to say, signs that show it is time to ask for help. Remind me of the official hotlines or organizations to consult depending on the seriousness, which I will verify.

10. Set limits on family screen use

To set clear rules that everyone accepts.

I want to set rules for screens in my family: children aged [ages], devices [list], difficult moments [list]. Values that matter to us: [list].
Propose a draft family agreement with 8 simple rules, a discussion moment with the children to build it together, attractive alternatives to screens, and a way to review the rules every three months. Take the different ages into account. I will check the official recommendations for each age group.

Protect your children's information

Do not enter a first name, last name, school, photo, report card, or health or behavior information that could identify your child. Describe the situation in general terms ("an 8-year-old in third grade"). School messages and documents often contain identities: review before pasting.

The age to use these tools

These prompts are written to be used by you, the adult. If your child wants to use an assistant themselves, check the service's rules. For ChatGPT, OpenAI's official help says the service is not meant for children under 13 and that 13 to 18-year-olds must have a parent's or guardian's consent (source: help.openai.com). The terms change: reread the page before deciding, and check the rules of the other services you use.

Learn, do not do it for them

For homework, use the AI to understand the concept and find guiding questions. If the child copies an answer, they learn nothing and the teacher will notice. Also check the AI's answers: it can be wrong, even at an elementary level.

Health, education and limits

The AI replaces neither a doctor, nor a psychologist, nor a teacher. For a health, learning or behavior problem, bullying or a serious concern, turn to a professional or to the school.

Frequently asked questions

Can my child use ChatGPT alone?

It depends on their age and the service's rules. OpenAI's official help says ChatGPT is not meant for children under 13 and requires a parent's consent for 13 to 18-year-olds. Go along for the first uses and set rules together.

How do I stop them from copying the answers?

Use the AI yourself to prepare guiding questions and explanations, then ask your child to put it in their own words. Have them explain their reasoning aloud: it is quickly clear whether they understood or copied.

Can I paste the school's message to get a reply?

It is better to remove all names, of the child and of the teachers, and the name of the school, then summarize the content. Write the reply with the AI's help, review it, and add the names yourself when you send it.
