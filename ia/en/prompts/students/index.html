ChatGPT Prompts for Students

Ten prompts to understand better, review and practice, without letting the AI do the work for you.

In short

  1. Explain a concept simply
  2. Make a study sheet
  3. Practice with an interactive quiz
  4. Create a study schedule
  5. Get feedback on a text without a rewrite
  6. Understand a solved exercise
  7. Prepare an oral presentation
  8. Summarize a research paper
  9. Learn with flashcards
  10. Find ideas for a project

An AI is an excellent private tutor if you ask it to explain, quiz you and correct you. It becomes a trap if you ask it to write your assignment: you learn nothing, and most schools ban it.

The prompts below are designed for learning. Replace the brackets with your course, your level and your goal.

  • Paste excerpts from your own course for reliable answers.
  • Ask to be quizzed, not just to read an answer.
  • Always check against your course notes or textbooks.

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details, then paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Le Chat. To adapt a prompt, use the prompt generator.

1. Explain a concept simply

When a point from class is still unclear after several readings.

You are a patient tutor. I am a student in [major, level] and I do not understand the following concept: [concept].
Explain it in three steps: a definition in two sentences, an everyday analogy, and a concrete example solved step by step.
Finish with three questions to check that I understood, without giving the answers right away. Wait for my answers before correcting.

2. Make a study sheet

To condense a chapter into one clear sheet.

Here is an excerpt from my [subject] course: [paste the text]
Create a one-page study sheet: key definitions, formulas or dates to remember, a diagram described in words, and 3 classic traps.
Use only the content provided. If information seems missing or doubtful, point it out instead of making something up.

3. Practice with an interactive quiz

To test your knowledge one question at a time.

You are a [subject] examiner at [level]. Quiz me on the chapter: [chapter].
Rules: ask only one question at a time, wait for my answer, tell me whether it is right, correct it in one sentence, then move on to the next. Alternate between recall, calculation and reasoning questions.
After 10 questions, give me a summary: what I have mastered and what I need to work on.

4. Create a study schedule

A few weeks before exams, to spread out the work.

Help me build a study schedule. My exams: [list with dates]. I study [number] hours a day on weekdays and [number] on weekends. My hardest subjects: [list]. Constraints: [job, sports, commute].
Propose a week-by-week schedule with breaks, spaced review and one rest day.
Present it as a table. Ask me any questions you need first.

5. Get feedback on a text without a rewrite

To proofread an assignment you wrote yourself.

Here is a text I wrote myself for [type of assignment]: [paste your text]
Do not rewrite it. Point out: spelling and grammar mistakes, heavy sentences, repetitions, unclear passages, and arguments that lack evidence.
Present each comment as a list: excerpt, problem, idea for improving it. I will make the corrections myself.

6. Understand a solved exercise

When you have the solution but not the method.

Here is a [subject] exercise and the solution provided by my instructor:
Problem: [paste the problem]
Solution: [paste the solution]
Explain the method to me step by step: why each line is written, which rule is used, and how to recognize this type of exercise. Then give me a similar exercise with different values, which I will solve on my own before showing you my answer.

7. Prepare an oral presentation

To structure a talk when you have already gathered the content.

I have to give a [length]-minute presentation on [topic] in front of [audience]. Here are my notes: [paste your notes]
Propose a three-part outline with a hook and a conclusion, the recommended time for each part, and three questions the audience might ask. Do not write the text of the presentation: give me the structure and ideas that I will adapt in my own words.

8. Summarize a research paper

To approach a long, technical text.

Here is a paper I have to read for my [subject] course: [paste the excerpt]
Summarize it in 150 words: research question, method, main results, limitations. Then list the technical terms with a simple definition.
This summary helps me prepare, and I will read the paper afterward. Say what you cannot claim from the excerpt alone.

9. Learn with flashcards

To memorize for the long term by testing yourself.

Using the following course material: [paste the text], generate 15 flashcards (question on the front, short answer on the back) covering the essential concepts.
Sort them by difficulty and indicate for each one when to review it (the next day, one week later, one month later).
Present the result as a two-column table that is easy to copy into a flashcard app.

10. Find ideas for a project

When you are short of ideas for a thesis or group project.

I am a [major] student and I have to choose a topic for [type of work: thesis, capstone project]. My interests: [list]. Constraints: [duration, group size, access to data].
Propose 6 topic ideas, each with: a research question, a possible method, and a feasibility risk.
Tell me how to check that each idea has not already been covered. I will choose and do the research myself.

Learn, do not delegate

Having an AI write a graded assignment counts as cheating at most schools, and instructors have detection tools and oral follow-up questions to spot it. The prompts on this page are for understanding, practicing and getting feedback. If you use help on work you hand in, follow the rules of your university or school.

Check the answers

An AI can invent a reference, a date or a formula. Always compare its explanations with your course and your textbooks. Never cite a source it gives you without having found it yourself.

Personal data

Do not paste your personal information (identity, student ID, address) or data about classmates. If your work contains data about people you interviewed, anonymize it first.

Limits

AI help does not replace the work of memorizing. Test yourself, hide the answer before reading it, and repeat at regular intervals.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheating to use ChatGPT to study?

No, if you use it to understand, quiz yourself and correct your own work. It becomes a problem when you have it write a graded assignment. Check the rules of your school, which may set limits on the use of AI.

Why paste my course into the prompt?

The answers are then based on your own material and not on general information, which is sometimes inaccurate. This lowers the risk of errors, but check anyway. Do not paste confidential or copyrighted documents.

Can AI replace a teacher?

No. It explains well and is available at any hour, but it can be wrong and does not know your course or your instructors' expectations. Use it as a supplement and bring your doubts to your instructor.
