Job Interview Prep With ChatGPT: Mock Interviews and Feedback

Practicing out loud, with varied questions and honest feedback: AI is a good simulation partner.

In short

It doesn't know your interviewer, but it keeps you from walking in cold.

  1. Reread the posting and the company
  2. Prepare your introduction
  3. Build short stories
  4. Run the mock interview
  5. Prepare your questions for the interviewer
  6. Work on the hard questions
  7. Sleep well and arrive early.

An interview is prepared like an oral exam: you gain by rehearsing out loud, with questions you did not choose. A chatbot can play the interviewer, follow up and spot your vague answers. What it doesn't know is how your real interviewer will react, what their priorities are or what they dislike: don't assume the simulation will look like the interview.

Prepare true answers. AI must never help you embellish or invent an experience: you will have to discuss it in detail, and an inconsistency shows quickly. Anonymize the names of employers, clients and colleagues before pasting text.

How to do it

  1. Reread the posting and the company and note three examples from your background that meet their expectations.
  2. Prepare your introduction: one minute on who you are, what you did, what you're looking for (prompt 1).
  3. Build short stories using the Situation, Task, Action, Result framework known as the “STAR method” (prompt 2).
  4. Run the mock interview: one question at a time, your answers out loud, then written down (prompt 3).
  5. Prepare your questions for the interviewer: about the role, the team, next steps (prompt 4).
  6. Work on the hard questions: a gap in your resume, a departure, a weakness (prompt 5).
  7. Sleep well and arrive early. No AI replaces that.

Six prompts to prepare

1. The one-minute introduction

I have an interview for [position] at a company in [industry]. Here are the facts to use, without proper names: [background summarized in 5 lines].
Ask me three questions to clarify what I want to emphasize. Then help me structure a 60-second introduction in three parts (who I am, what I've done that is relevant, why this job), suggesting an outline and not a script to recite. I'll write it myself.

2. Turn an experience into a STAR story

Here is a real situation I lived at work: [anonymized description].
Help me structure it as Situation, Task, Action, Result. For each part, ask me a question if the information is missing. Don't fill any gap with an invention. At the end, tell me in a few words which quality this story illustrates (independence, rigor, listening...).

3. The mock interview

You are a recruiter for the [position] job at a company in [industry and size]. Run an 8-question interview: a mix of classic questions (background, motivation), situational questions and questions about my experience. Ask ONE question at a time, follow up when my answer is vague, and give no advice during the interview. When I type "end," give me feedback: strengths, answers to develop, verbal tics, and three questions to rework.

4. My questions for the interviewer

For a [position] job at [type of company], suggest 8 relevant questions to ask at the end of the interview: about the content of the job, the team, onboarding, priorities for the first six months, next steps in the process. Avoid questions whose answer is on the company's website. Rank them by when they are most useful.

5. The hard questions

Here is a delicate situation in my background: [e.g., a period without work, a layoff, a career change, frequent job changes].
I want to prepare an honest, short, forward-looking answer. Ask me three questions to find what I can truthfully and positively say. Then suggest a three-sentence answer outline, without hiding facts or criticizing my former employers. I'll write the final answer.

6. Debrief a past interview

I just had an interview for [position]. Here is what I remember: [questions asked and my answers summarized, anonymized]. Tell me which answers were probably the weakest and why, and suggest a way to improve them next time. Make clear that you can't know what the interviewer thought.

The limits of simulation

  • AI is too nice. Explicitly ask it to be demanding, to follow up, and not to compliment every answer.
  • It doesn't see your attitude. Tone, eye contact and posture can only be practiced out loud in front of a friend or by recording yourself.
  • It can be wrong about the company. According to OpenAI's official help center, ChatGPT can produce false or invented answers: never cite a figure or a news item to a recruiter without checking it on the company's website.
  • Salary and employment law. An AI does not replace personalized advice: for pay or contract questions, rely on official government sources in your country or a qualified adviser.

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT really simulate an interview?

Yes, for practicing answers and structuring your ideas. It doesn't replace the reaction of a real recruiter, who is less predictable.

Should I memorize my answers?

No: it is better to master your examples and tell them naturally. A recited answer sounds fake.

Can I tell it what the interviewer is like?

You can specify the industry, the company size and the tone you want, but it doesn't know how your real interviewer will behave.

Is it useful to record my answers?

Yes, it is one of the best ways to spot hesitation and rambling, with or without AI. You can then transcribe your answers (anonymized) to get comments.
