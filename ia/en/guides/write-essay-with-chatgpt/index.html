Essay Help From ChatGPT: How to Get It Without Cheating

AI can help you understand the prompt, defend your outline against objections and spot your weak points.

In short

The one thing not to ask it is to write the essay for you.

  1. Work alone first, on paper
  2. Submit your analysis to the AI
  3. Propose a thesis and an outline
  4. Rework your outline using those objections
  5. Write it alone.
  6. Read it aloud

An essay exists to show that you can build an argument. If the AI writes it, you lose the whole point of the exercise, and you take a real risk: most schools treat an AI-written assignment handed in as your own as academic dishonesty. On the other hand, talking with an AI while you prepare can unblock someone who is going in circles.

First of all, reread your school's policy and your instructor's guidelines on AI. What they allow or forbid overrides any advice here. When in doubt, ask, and say what you used.

What AI can do, and what it should not do

StepReasonable useDo not
Understanding the promptHave the terms rephrased, ask questionsAsk for “the” thesis to use
Finding ideasSurface objections and cases to examineCopy examples without checking them
Building the outlineTest the logic of YOUR outlineAsk for a ready-made detailed outline
WritingNothing, you write aloneHave it write paragraphs
ProofreadingSpot clumsy phrasing and errors, if allowedHave the whole text rewritten

How to do it

  1. Work alone first, on paper: break down the key words of the prompt, jot down first ideas and first examples. Five to ten minutes of your own thinking makes everything after it far more useful.
  2. Submit your analysis to the AI and ask it to look for what you overlooked, not to produce its own.
  3. Propose a thesis and an outline, then ask for objections, the way a skeptical reader would.
  4. Rework your outline using those objections, with your own words and your own references from your course and your reading.
  5. Write it alone. Quotations, authors, dates and examples must come from sources you consulted yourself: an AI can invent a quotation or the title of a book that does not exist.
  6. Read it aloud before handing it in.

Prompts that don't write for you

Analyze the essay prompt

Here is an essay prompt for [subject: philosophy, literature, history...], level [grade or year]: "[prompt]".
Don't give me a thesis or an outline. Ask me five questions that help me analyze the terms of the prompt, spot possible traps (off-topic answers, words with several meanings) and see what is implied. I will answer them one by one.

Stress-test my thesis

Here is my thesis statement for the prompt "[prompt]": [your thesis].
Tell me whether it really follows from the prompt or distorts it, whether it is too broad or too narrow, and whether it truly calls for an argument. Suggest two possible rewordings for me to compare with mine, without choosing for me.

Play devil's advocate

I'm defending the following outline:
I. [idea] - II. [idea] - III. [idea]
Play a skeptical reader: for each part, give me the strongest objection someone could raise, and one question that forces me to sharpen my reasoning. Don't suggest examples or quotations: it's my job to find them in my course.

Check the transitions

Here are the introduction and outline of my essay, written by me:
[paste]
Tell me only where the flow of ideas is weak or where a section repeats the previous one. Don't rewrite anything: one comment per problem, in order of seriousness.

Review a conclusion I wrote

Here is my conclusion: [paste]. Tell me whether it answers the central question ([reminder]), whether it just repeats the sections, and what perspective it could open. Give your comments as questions rather than sentences I could copy.

Pitfalls

  • Invented quotations. An AI can fabricate a sentence attributed to an author, or a book title. According to OpenAI's official help center, ChatGPT can produce false information. Only quote what you have verified in a book or a reliable source.
  • A style that doesn't sound like you. Your teacher knows how you write. A text that is too smooth and generic stands out, and it teaches you nothing.
  • A one-size-fits-all outline. Outlines proposed by an AI are often correct but interchangeable. A good outline answers your specific prompt and your course.
  • Hiding your use. If you used the AI to discuss your outline and your instructor asks you to explain, simply say so.

Frequently asked questions

Is it forbidden to use ChatGPT for an essay?

It depends on the rules of your school and your instructor. Having the AI write the assignment and handing it in as yours is forbidden almost everywhere. Ask what is allowed.

Can ChatGPT give me quotations from authors?

It can, but some are false or inaccurate. Use them only after finding them in a book or a reliable source.

Can an AI fix my spelling and grammar?

Often yes, if your instructor accepts it, but ask for comments rather than a rewrite, so you understand your mistakes and improve.

What if I'm stuck on a prompt?

Ask the AI to ask you questions about the prompt rather than propose an answer. Talking about the topic is often enough to restart your thinking.
