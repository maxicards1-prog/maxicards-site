Why ChatGPT Gives Bad Answers (and How to Fix It)

Vague, too long, invented or off-topic answer: most of the time, the cause is in the request.

In short

Here are eight diagnoses and the fix for each.

  1. Reread the answer and name the flaw: too vague, too long, wrong, off topic, wrong tone?
  2. Find it in the list below.
  3. Add the missing detail to your request, in the same conversation.
  4. If the conversation has become tangled, start a new one with a complete prompt.

When an answer disappoints, the reflex is to blame the tool. Sometimes it really is wrong, but most failures have identifiable causes. This page works as a troubleshooting guide: spot your symptom, apply the fix, try again. It also applies to Claude and Gemini.

How to do it

  1. Reread the answer and name the flaw: too vague, too long, wrong, off topic, wrong tone?
  2. Find it in the list below.
  3. Add the missing detail to your request, in the same conversation.
  4. If the conversation has become tangled, start a new one with a complete prompt.

Cause 1: the request is vague

Symptom: the answer is generic, applicable to anyone. Why: OpenAI recommends being specific about context, outcome, length, format and style; without these, the AI fills in with generalities. Fix:

Your answer is too general. Here is my situation: [3 to 5 lines of real context: who, what, constraints]. Start over taking these elements into account and give three concrete recommendations, each with a first action to take this week.

Cause 2: the audience or level is not stated

Symptom: too technical or too simple. Fix: say who you are writing for: “explain it for someone who has never used a spreadsheet,” “for a busy reader who knows the field.”

Cause 3: no format imposed

Symptom: a wall of text when you wanted a table, or the reverse. Fix: OpenAI advises replacing vague terms with measurable limits, for example “a paragraph of 3 to 5 sentences” rather than “fairly short.” Add: “Answer as a three-column table” or “in 120 words maximum.”

Cause 4: the AI invents facts

Symptom: numbers, quotes, references or names that look plausible but are false. Why: a language model produces plausible text; it does not always check its sources. Fixes: supply the reference text yourself and limit the answer to that text; allow the AI to admit ignorance; check any important information with a reliable source.

Answer only from the text below. If the answer is not in it, write “Information not in the text” instead of inferring it. Each time, quote the sentence that justifies your answer.
###
[reference text]
###
Question: [your question]

Cause 5: too much in a single request

Symptom: part of the instructions is forgotten. Fix: number the steps or split into several messages. Anthropic also recommends writing instructions as sequential steps when order or completeness matters.

Cause 6: negative instructions

Symptom: the AI does exactly what you forbade. Fix: OpenAI recommends saying what to do rather than what to avoid. Replace “no jargon” with “use words an eighth grader would understand.”

Cause 7: the conversation has become confused

Symptom: the AI mixes elements from earlier exchanges or repeats mistakes you corrected. Fix: open a new conversation and paste a complete starting prompt that recaps the essentials. To measure the size of what you paste, you can use the token counter.

Summary of the situation to start from scratch:
- Goal: [goal]
- What has been decided: [decisions]
- What was tried without success: [attempts]
- Constraints: [constraints]
Now suggest an approach different from those already tried and explain in two sentences why it should work better.

Cause 8: the wrong tool or the wrong expectation

Symptom: you ask for very recent information, a long calculation or a file analysis, and the result is weak. Why: depending on the plan, available features vary; the official ChatGPT page, for example, presents several free-plan features as limited. Fix: check the limits of your plan, compare with another tool, or do the important calculation in a spreadsheet. Our guide to choosing an AI helps you find your way.

A three-question diagnosis

  • Would a colleague reading my prompt know what to do, for whom and in what form?
  • Did I give the text or data the AI needs, instead of assuming it knows them?
  • Did I check the facts in the answer before using them?

If you answer no to any of them, that is where to act. To rewrite your prompt, try the improvement tool or reread the five-step method.

What a prompt cannot fix

A prompt does not guarantee accuracy. For a health, legal or money decision, or for an official document, the AI is there to prepare your questions, never to conclude for you. And do not paste personal or confidential data that is not necessary.

Frequently asked questions

Why does ChatGPT make up information?

It produces plausible text from what it has learned, without checking each time. Give it a reference text, allow it to say it does not know, and verify important facts yourself.

Should I restart a conversation that goes off the rails?

Often yes. Open a new conversation with a full summary of the goal, decisions and constraints rather than correcting endlessly.

Is a better prompt enough to get an accurate answer?

No. It improves relevance, format and tone, but the accuracy of facts must always be checked.

Do other AIs have the same flaws?

Yes, these causes apply to all assistants of this type. The differences come mostly from how you word the request.
