How to Translate With ChatGPT

Give the context, choose the tone, ask for uncertain passages and proofread: here is a method for reliable translations, with complete prompts.

In short

  1. Remove sensitive data
  2. State the source and target languages
  3. Give the context
  4. Specify the format
  5. Proofread and have it checked

ChatGPT can translate a message, an email or a short document, and above all adapt it: a more formal tone, a profession's vocabulary, a language level. But a translation sent without review carries risks: mistranslations, false friends, an overly casual phrase. This page gives you a method for getting a clean text and knowing when not to settle for machine translation.

How to do it

  1. Remove sensitive data from the text (names, addresses, numbers). Replace them with placeholders: [Name], [address]. See ChatGPT and personal data.
  2. State the source and target languages, and the variant if it matters (British or American English, Spanish from Spain or Latin America).
  3. Give the context: who writes to whom, for what purpose, with what degree of formality.
  4. Specify the format: translation only, or translation plus notes on ambiguous passages.
  5. Proofread and have it checked: ask for a back-translation (back into your own language) to check the meaning, and have someone who speaks the language review it if the stakes are high.

The basic prompt

You are a professional translator from [source language] to [target language: British English, Spain Spanish…].
Context: [who writes to whom and why: an employee writes to a client to postpone a delivery].
Tone: [professional and courteous / friendly / very formal].
Translate the text in quotation marks, keeping the exact meaning and adapting the polite phrases to the target language.
“[paste the text]”
After the translation, list in three points maximum the passages where several translations were possible, and explain your choice.

Five situations, five prompts

A professional email

Translate this email into [language]. I am [role] and I am writing to [recipient's role] whom I do not know. Tone: professional, courteous, not familiar. Keep the paragraphs and bullet points. End with a closing suited to this language. Email: “[text]”

A message to a friend or for a trip

Translate this message into [language], in a natural, relaxed register, the way a native speaker of [country] would say it. Also give the version written in simple phonetics so I can say it out loud. Message: “[text]”

A technical or administrative document

Translate this text into [language]. Field: [field: rental agreement, appliance manual, school file]. Keep the exact technical terms and put the original term in brackets when it has no direct equivalent. Do not summarize, do not add anything. Text: “[text]”

Understand a text you received

Here is a text in [original language]: “[text]”. Give me: 1) a faithful translation into English, 2) a list of difficult words or expressions with their meaning, 3) the passages whose meaning is uncertain or depends on context.

Check your own translation

Here is a source text and my translation into [language]. Compare them sentence by sentence. Point out mistranslations, omissions, grammar mistakes and unnatural phrasing, then suggest a corrected version. Original: “[source text]” My translation: “[text]”

Translate from an image or a file

OpenAI's official help says you can attach documents, images and other files to ChatGPT to work on their content; accepted images are PNG, JPEG or non-animated GIF, up to 20 MB per image, and for a PDF the tool can analyze both the text and the images. The page does not explicitly mention translation: test it on your file and check that all the text was taken into account, especially fine print and tables. Avoid sending a document containing personal or confidential data.

Adjust the tone and level

  • Tone. Ask for “formal,” “neutral” or “warm” rather than letting the tool guess.
  • Language level. If you are learning the language, ask for a simple version (short sentences) in addition to the natural version.
  • Regional variant. Specify it: some expressions change from one country to another.
  • Consistency. For a long text, split it into parts and remind the tool each time of the vocabulary choices made.

The limits to know

  • Errors are possible, even when the translation looks fluent. A well-written text is not necessarily an accurate one.
  • Numbers, dates, units and proper names must be checked one by one.
  • High-stakes documents (contract, medical file, official administrative procedure, dispute) are a job for a professional translator or, where the procedure requires it, a certified translation. ChatGPT does not replace legal or medical advice.
  • Idioms are often translated word for word. Ask for the natural equivalent, not the literal version.
  • Missing context is the first cause of error: the same word can have several meanings.

To learn rather than translate

If your goal is to improve, use the AI as a teacher: translate first yourself, then ask for a commented correction (last prompt above). For emails, see writing an email with ChatGPT. Frequently asked questions

Does ChatGPT translate as well as a professional translator?

It is very useful for understanding or producing a draft, but it can make mistranslations. For a high-stakes document (contract, official procedure), use a professional.

How do I check a translation made by ChatGPT?

Ask for a back-translation into your own language, have it list the ambiguous passages, check numbers, dates and proper names, and have someone who speaks the language review it if the stakes are high.

Can I translate a document or a photo with ChatGPT?

OpenAI's help says you can attach documents and images (PNG, JPEG, non-animated GIF, 20 MB maximum per image). It does not explicitly mention translation: try it and check the result.

Do I need to give personal information to translate?

No. Replace names, addresses and numbers with placeholders before pasting the text, then put them back yourself in the final version.
