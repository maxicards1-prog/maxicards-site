How to Write a Resume With ChatGPT Without Inventing Anything

ChatGPT can help you put your experience into words and tailor your resume to a job posting.

In short

It doesn't know what you did: you provide the facts, it helps you present them.

  1. Prepare your facts on paper
  2. Anonymize before pasting
  3. First ask the AI for questions
  4. Reword line by line
  5. Tailor it to each job posting
  6. Reread everything, number by number.

A resume is a document of proof: it says what you did, with what results, for what kind of employer. An AI is valuable for wording it (precise verbs, short sentences, readable structure) but it cannot guess it. If you ask for “a sales resume,” it will produce a plausible, empty text. If you give it your facts, it will help you make them readable.

Golden rule: never write an experience, a degree, a language level or a software skill that you don't have. A background check or an interview test will reveal the gap, and a false claim can cost you the job. Public employment services in your country also publish free resume advice and workshops.

How to do it

  1. Prepare your facts on paper: jobs, dates, duties, tools, measurable results when you have them (volume, deadlines, team size).
  2. Anonymize before pasting: remove your name, address, phone, email, date of birth. You will add your contact details yourself at the end.
  3. First ask the AI for questions that dig up the achievements you forget (prompt 1).
  4. Reword line by line rather than in one block (prompt 2). You stay in control of what is written.
  5. Tailor it to each job posting: keywords from the ad, order of sections, experience you put forward (prompt 3).
  6. Reread everything, number by number. Fix any sentence that exaggerates or invents. Have a human read it too.

Six prompts to build your resume

1. The achievements inventory

I'm preparing my resume for a [target job] position. My last job: [title, duration, type of company, without the name]. My main duties: [rough list].
Ask me 8 questions, one at a time, to bring out concrete achievements: results, numbers, problems solved, responsibilities. Don't make anything up: if I don't know, move on to the next. At the end, summarize my answers in a list.

2. Rewrite one experience line

Here is a line from my resume, written by me: "[sentence]".
Suggest three rewrites starting with an action verb, 15 words maximum, without adding any fact I haven't given. If information is missing to make it more precise, ask me instead of inventing it.

3. Tailor the resume to a job posting

Here is a job posting: [paste the ad].
Here is my real experience (no personal data): [list].
Tell me: 1) the five main requirements of the posting, 2) the ones my background already covers, with the matching experience, 3) the ones my background doesn't cover (don't invent them). Then suggest the order in which I should present my experience and which words from the ad I can reuse if, and only if, they truly describe what I did.

4. The summary at the top

From these real elements: [years of experience, fields, 3 strengths proven by facts], suggest three versions of a profile summary of 3 lines maximum for a [position] job. Avoid empty phrases ("dynamic," "motivated") and unverifiable promises. Every sentence must rest on a fact I provided.

5. Format a resume with little experience

I'm [a student / changing careers / without experience in the target field]. Here is what I've done: [internships, summer jobs, projects, volunteering, clubs]. Suggest a resume structure that highlights these for [target position]: which sections, in what order, which transferable skills. Don't create any extra experience.

6. The critical review

Here is my resume, anonymized:
[paste the text]
Read it like a recruiter in a hurry: spot vague sentences, mistakes, date inconsistencies, repetition, and what is missing for the [position] job. List the problems in order of importance, without rewriting the document.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Invented numbers. “Increased sales by 40%” is worth something only if it is true and you can explain it.
  • A resume that is too polished. If every line looks like a template, nothing stands out. Keep at least one detail that is yours alone.
  • Personal data in the prompt. According to OpenAI's official help center, for individual accounts your content may be used to improve models unless you change the setting provided in the data controls. Always anonymize, even if you have that setting on.
  • Not rereading. The same help center says answers can be inaccurate: a date or a job title may be distorted along the way.

Going further

The logical next step is the cover letter, then interview preparation and the LinkedIn profile. The job search prompts offer more templates. To understand what you are sharing, see ChatGPT and personal data.

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT write my resume for me?

It can format it and reword your experience, but it doesn't know your background. Without specific facts from you, it will produce a generic, even false, text.

What information should I not give it?

Name, address, phone, email, date of birth, and names of confidential employers or clients. Add your contact details yourself in the final document.

Can I reuse the words of the job posting?

Yes, when they honestly describe what you did. Never add a skill only because it appears in the ad.

Can a recruiter tell that my resume was made with AI?

It hardly matters: what counts is that every line is true and you can talk about it. A resume that is too generic is often less convincing.
