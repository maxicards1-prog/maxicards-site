Learn English With ChatGPT: Conversation, Correction, Vocabulary

A partner available at any hour, patient, who corrects without mocking: that is what ChatGPT can offer for practicing English.

In short

As long as you frame the conversation well.

  1. Set a precise goal
  2. State your level
  3. Write in English first
  4. Ask for explained corrections
  5. Note your repeated mistakes
  6. Short regular sessions

The main obstacle to progress in English is lack of practice: few chances to write and speak, and a fear of making mistakes. A chatbot removes that fear. It answers at any hour, accepts clumsy sentences and can explain why a sentence is wrong. It does not replace a structured course, a teacher, or practice with real people.

At school, keep the right reflex: AI is for practicing and getting corrections. For a graded assignment, follow your teacher's and your school's rules, and do not hand in a text written by AI.

How to do it

  1. Set a precise goal: talk about your day, prepare a presentation, write a work email, pass a level.
  2. State your level (beginner, intermediate...) and ask the AI to adapt its vocabulary and sentence length.
  3. Write in English first, even badly. You learn by trying, not by copying.
  4. Ask for explained corrections: the faulty sentence, the corrected sentence, the rule in one line.
  5. Note your repeated mistakes and ask for small exercises on them.
  6. Short regular sessions (15 minutes) beat one long session a month.

Seven practice prompts

1. Guided conversation

Let's have a conversation in English about [topic: travel, my job, music...]. My level is [A2/B1/B2]. Use short sentences and simple words. Ask me one question at a time. After each of my answers, first reply naturally, then give me a "Corrections" section: my mistakes, the corrected sentence, and a one-line explanation in [my native language].

2. Role-play a real situation

Play the role of a [hotel receptionist / recruiter / waiter] and speak to me only in English. Situation: [describe]. I need to [goal: book a room, answer an interview question...]. Stay realistic: follow up if I'm vague. At the end, give me three useful phrases I could have used and my three main mistakes.

3. Correct a text I wrote

Here is a short text I wrote in English: [paste your text, with no personal data].
Don't rewrite it. List my mistakes (grammar, vocabulary, word order) with the correction and a one-line explanation. Then tell me which type of mistake I make most often, and give me three exercises to practice it.

4. Vocabulary in context

I want to learn vocabulary about [topic: work, health, travel] at [level] level. Give me 10 useful words or expressions, each with: a translation into [my native language], a simple example sentence, and a common mistake made by speakers of my language. Then give me a short fill-in-the-blank test on these words and wait for my answers.

5. False friends and common traps

Make me a list of 10 false friends between [my native language] and English, from most to least common. For each one: the word, its real meaning, the word in my language that it looks like, and an example sentence. End with a 5-question mini quiz.

6. Prepare a speech

I'm preparing a 3-minute talk in English about [topic]. Here are my notes in English, written by me: [notes].
Don't give me a final text. Tell me whether my structure is clear, which sentences are hard to pronounce or sound unnatural, and ask me five follow-up questions a listener might ask. I'll answer them, then you correct me.

7. Understand a grammar rule

Explain the difference between [present perfect and simple past / will and going to / etc.] with a simple table, three examples for each case and two classic mistakes. Then give me 6 sentences to complete, one at a time, waiting for my answer before correcting.

The limits

  • Possible errors. The AI can sometimes explain a rule wrongly or suggest an unnatural phrasing. According to OpenAI's official help center, its answers can be inaccurate: cross-check with a dictionary, a textbook or your teacher.
  • Too easy. If you paste text in your own language to get a translation, you learn nothing. Try in English first, then get it corrected.
  • It doesn't replace speaking. Pronunciation and understanding different accents also require listening to and talking with real people.
  • Personal data. Write fictional or anonymized texts, with no name, address or number.

Frequently asked questions

Is ChatGPT enough to learn English?

It is a good supplement for practicing and getting corrections, but it does not replace a structured course or conversations with real people.

How do I stop it from switching to my native language too fast?

Explicitly ask it to stay in English, with short sentences suited to your level, and to give explanations in your language only in a &ldquo;Corrections&rdquo; section.

Can I trust it for grammar?

Often, but not always. Check the rules that surprise you in a textbook or a reference dictionary.

Can I use it for my English homework?

To practice and get corrections, yes if your teacher accepts it. For a graded assignment, hand in a text you wrote yourself.
