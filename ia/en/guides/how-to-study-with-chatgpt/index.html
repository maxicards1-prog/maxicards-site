How to Study With ChatGPT Without Cheating or Learning Errors

ChatGPT can be a patient study partner: it explains things another way, quizzes you and checks your answers.

In short

Make it work from your own course material, and stay in control.

  1. Start from your course.
  2. State your level and your goal.
  3. Ask for an explanation first, then an exercise.
  4. Answer before you look at the correction.
  5. Have your answers corrected
  6. Check anything surprising

Studying is not rereading: it is pulling information out of your own head and checking that you understood it. A chatbot is useful for that, because it asks questions, rephrases ten times without getting tired and never judges you. It is poor at one thing: if you ask it to “make the study sheet” for you, you get a clean document and learn nothing.

Basic rule: AI is for understanding, practicing and getting feedback. It never writes an assignment that you hand in as your own. Your school's rules and each teacher's rules on AI come before anything in this guide.

How to do it

  1. Start from your course. Paste a passage from your lesson (a few paragraphs, not a whole chapter) or describe the concept precisely. Without it, the AI answers from general knowledge, which may differ from what your teacher expects.
  2. State your level and your goal. “I'm in 10th grade,” “I have a test on Thursday,” “I don't understand the difference between X and Y.”
  3. Ask for an explanation first, then an exercise. Understanding before practicing keeps you from memorizing mistakes.
  4. Answer before you look at the correction. Ask the AI to give the answer only after yours.
  5. Have your answers corrected and ask it to point out what is missing, not to rewrite it for you.
  6. Check anything surprising in your course or textbook. A ten-minute chat should end with a two-minute check.

Six study prompts to copy

1. Get a concept explained another way

I'm a [grade level] student preparing for a test on [chapter]. I don't understand the concept of [concept]. Here is the excerpt from my course:
[paste the excerpt]
Explain it to me in three steps: one simple sentence, an everyday analogy, then a concrete example. End with a question to check that I understood, and wait for my answer before continuing.

2. Quiz yourself with progressive questions

Using the course material below, ask me ONE question at a time about [chapter], starting easy and getting harder. After each answer from me, tell me whether it is right, correct it in one sentence, then move on to the next. After 10 questions, list the concepts I should review.
Course: [paste your course]
Use only this course: if a question can't be answered from it, say so.

3. Check a study sheet you made yourself

Here is the study sheet I wrote myself on [chapter]:
[paste your sheet]
Compare it with the course below and list: the errors, the important omissions, and the passages that are too vague. Don't rewrite my sheet. Just tell me what to fix, in order of importance.
Course: [paste the course]

4. The “explain it to a classmate” method

I'm going to explain [concept] in my own words, as if you were a classmate who understood nothing. Play that role: ask me naive questions when something is unclear. When I'm done, give me three things: what I explained well, what was imprecise, and what I forgot.

5. Build a realistic study schedule

I have a [subject] test on [date]. I have [number] days left, I can study about [duration] per day, and my weak points are: [list]. Suggest a day-by-day plan with, for each session, a specific goal and an active task (quiz, exercise, explaining out loud). Include a rest day and a final review session the day before.

6. Spot your recurring mistakes

Here are three exercises I did, with my teacher's corrections:
[paste the questions, your answers and the corrections]
Tell me what type of mistake I keep making (method, calculation, vocabulary, reading the question...) and give me two exercises of the same type to redo, without giving me the solution right away.

Mistakes that waste time

  • Asking for a summary and rereading it. Reading a summary feels comfortable but works poorly. Prefer questions, exercises and explaining out loud.
  • Believing everything it says. According to OpenAI's official help center, ChatGPT can produce incorrect answers, and you should check their accuracy. A wrong detail learned the night before a test is expensive.
  • Not stating your level. Without it, the answer may be too advanced or off the syllabus.
  • Pasting personal information. Your course does not need your name, your school or your grades. See the guide on ChatGPT and personal data.

Limits to know

The AI does not know your teacher's exact syllabus, writing requirements or the examples seen in class. For science, check calculations and formulas: a confident mistake is still a mistake. For dates, quotations and references, always go back to your textbook.

Going further

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT replace my teacher?

No. It can rephrase things and let you practice, but it does not know your exact syllabus and can be wrong. Your course and your teacher remain the reference.

Is studying with AI cheating?

Studying is working for yourself: having something explained and quizzing yourself is not handing in work done by someone else. Handing in an assignment written by AI as your own, however, is prohibited almost everywhere. Read your school's policy.

What should I do when ChatGPT contradicts itself?

Go back to your course or textbook, then ask the AI to justify its answer using your text. If doubt remains, ask your teacher.

Should I give it my whole course?

Targeted excerpts work better. You get more precise answers and avoid sharing information you don't need to.
