How to Use Nano Banana in Gemini

Nano Banana is the name of Gemini's image models.

In short

Here is how to create, edit and combine images, with complete prompts and the limits Google announces.

  1. Open Gemini
  2. Choose “Images”
  3. Write the prompt.
  4. Add a reference image
  5. Refine through conversation
  6. Download the result

“Nano Banana” refers to the image generation and editing models built into Google's Gemini app. Everything below relies on Gemini's official help and on Google's announcement. Model names, quotas and menus change: if a label differs, follow the official help.

What the official documentation says

Gemini's help page distinguishes several versions: Nano Banana Lite, tied to Flash-Lite and designed for speed; Nano Banana 2, presented as the default model with better quality and more reliable text rendering; and Nano Banana Pro, which requires a Google AI plan and lets you redo an image with more detail. Once the daily quota of Nano Banana 2 is reached, “redo with Pro” is no longer available.

Google's announcement presents the following features:

  • Consistency of people and pets from one edit to the next;
  • Blending several photos to create a new scene;
  • Successive edits that change a specific part of the image while keeping the rest;
  • Style transfer: applying the color or texture of one image to an object in another;
  • Marking: created or edited images carry a visible watermark and an invisible SynthID watermark, to indicate they are AI-generated.

Google AI plans and their prices are in the plan comparison (checked on 11/23/2026).

How to do it

  1. Open Gemini with your Google account.
  2. Choose “Images” in the side menu, or write your request directly in the conversation.
  3. Write the prompt. Gemini's help advises starting with a verb such as “draw,” “generate” or “create,” describing the style, then detailing the subject and the setting.
  4. Add a reference image if you want to start from a photo (you can upload one).
  5. Refine through conversation: ask for one edit at a time, on the generated image or on an image from your library.
  6. Download the result at full size, or export it to Google Docs.

Editing images requires being 18 or older, according to the official help. Google may remove images that appear to violate its terms, including its prohibited use policy.

Complete prompts to adapt

Create an image from scratch

Create an illustration of a small neighborhood coffee shop in the morning. Style: soft watercolor, warm colors. Foreground: a steaming cup on a wooden table near the window. Background: a quiet street with a slight blur. Horizontal format. No text in the image.

Edit a photo while keeping the essentials

Using the attached photo, keep the person, their pose and their face exactly as they are. Replace only the background with a beach at sunset, with consistent lighting on the face. Do not change the clothes.

Blend two photos

Here are two photos: the first shows [my dog], the second [a mountain landscape]. Create an image where the dog sits in the foreground of this landscape, with realistic scale and consistent lighting between the two. Keep the dog identical to the first photo.

Transfer a style

Apply the texture and colors of the second image (blue and white patterned fabric) to the sofa in the first image. Do not change the shape of the sofa or the rest of the room.

Good practices

  • One change per message. Multiple edits are harder to control.
  • Say what to keep: “keep the face, change only the jacket.”
  • Describe rather than name: light, material, colors, framing.
  • Reread the text in the image if you ask for any: even with rendering announced as more reliable, typos remain possible.
  • Keep good prompts in a document to start again faster. The image prompt generator helps you structure them.

Precautions

  • Image rights. Do not edit or blend a person's photo without their consent, and do not publish that kind of image.
  • Personal data. A photo of family or children is personal data: think before uploading it to an online service and check the service's data settings (see the guide on personal data for the method).
  • Transparency. If you publish a generated image, say that it was generated, especially if it depicts something real. The SynthID watermark helps identify it but does not replace your disclosure.
  • Professional use. Read Google's terms of use before commercial use.

Frequently asked questions

What is Nano Banana?

It is the name of the image generation and editing models built into Google's Gemini app, described in Gemini's official help.

Is Nano Banana free?

Gemini has a free plan, but the quotas and accessible models depend on your plan; Nano Banana Pro, for example, requires a Google AI plan according to the official help.

Can I edit my own photo with Nano Banana?

Yes, Gemini's help describes editing generated or uploaded images, with an age requirement of 18 or older for editing.

Are Nano Banana images recognizable?

Google says created or edited images carry a visible watermark and an invisible SynthID watermark to show they are AI-generated.
