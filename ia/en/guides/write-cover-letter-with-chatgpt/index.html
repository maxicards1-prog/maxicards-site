Write a Cover Letter With ChatGPT Without Sounding Fake

A letter generated in one click is recognizable by its stock phrases.

In short

Used as a thinking partner, though, AI helps you understand the job posting, choose your arguments and proofread.

  1. Read the posting and the company
  2. Have the AI decode the posting
  3. Choose two or three arguments
  4. Ask for an outline
  5. Write the first draft
  6. Read it aloud
  7. Check the names

A cover letter answers a single question: why you, for this job, at this company? Generic templates don't answer it, and letters produced in one click by an AI don't either: they line up phrases (“passionate about,” “with my strong background in”) that tell the reader nothing. AI becomes useful when you bring it facts and use it to think, structure and proofread.

Never let the letter lie: an invented experience, or motivation fabricated for a company you barely know, puts you in trouble at the first interview question. Always reread every sentence, and remove your personal data before pasting a text.

How to do it

  1. Read the posting and the company: website, products, values, news. Note two or three specific things that truly speak to you.
  2. Have the AI decode the posting: what the employer is looking for, the important words (prompt 1).
  3. Choose two or three arguments that rest on facts from your background: an achievement, a result, a situation you lived.
  4. Ask for an outline, not a text: hook, proof, projection (prompt 2).
  5. Write the first draft in your own words, then have it flag cliches and length (prompt 3).
  6. Read it aloud: if you wouldn't say the sentence, rewrite it.
  7. Check the names: company, contact person, job title. A letter that keeps another employer's name is an automatic rejection.

Four prompts for four moments

1. Decode the job posting

Here is a job posting: [paste the ad].
Analyze it: 1) the three main duties, 2) the qualities really expected (distinguish what is written from what is implied), 3) the questions I should ask myself to decide whether this job suits me, 4) three things to look up about the company before writing. Don't write a letter.

2. Build the outline from my facts

I'm applying for the position of [title] at a company in the [industry] sector. Here are my real facts:
- Achievement 1: [specific fact]
- Achievement 2: [specific fact]
- What attracts me to the company: [specific element I found myself]
Suggest a letter outline in four paragraphs (hook, proof 1, proof 2, projection), with for each one a sentence of intent and the facts to use. Add no new facts. No written-out sentences.

3. Hunt the cliches in my draft

Here is my draft letter, written by me, without name or contact details:
[paste the text]
Spot: 1) empty phrases or ones found in every letter, 2) sentences where I say what I am rather than what I did, 3) repetition and errors. Quote each sentence concerned with a question that helps me make it more specific. Don't rewrite anything.

4. Shorten without losing the essentials

My letter is [number] words long and must fit on one page. Here is the text:
[paste]
Tell me which paragraphs or sentences are least useful and why, suggesting what I can delete. I'll make the cuts myself.

What good proof looks like

Compare “I have a sense of customer service” with “I ran the front desk of a store for two summers and handled complaints with my manager's help.” The second sentence is verifiable, concrete, and you can expand on it out loud. When you ask the AI for help, demand the same level of precision: make it ask questions about each claim until it rests on a fact, a situation, a result. If you can't answer, the argument doesn't belong in the letter.

Special cases

  • Unsolicited application. Ask the AI to question you about what you know of the company: if you have nothing to answer, do your research first.
  • Career change. List transferable skills and concrete examples, not intentions.
  • First job. Lean on internships, projects, clubs, summer jobs: real facts are enough.

Pitfalls

  • The “robot” tone. Long sentences, cascading adjectives, mechanical transitions: rewrite in your own voice.
  • Inaccurate company information. The AI can invent figures or values. According to OpenAI's official help center, its answers can be incorrect: check everything on the company's website.
  • Personal data pasted without thinking. Follow the anonymization rule. See ChatGPT and personal data.
  • The same letter everywhere. A letter is useful when it shows that you looked at the company and the job.

Going further

Start with a solid resume, then prepare for the interview. The job search prompts offer more templates, and the prompt improver helps you sharpen a request. For an application to a school or program, see college application essays and ChatGPT instead.

Frequently asked questions

Do I still need a cover letter?

It depends on the posting: some ask for one, others don't. When it is requested, it is often read to judge motivation and writing.

How much time does AI save?

It speeds up analyzing the posting and proofreading, but the gain disappears if you let it write alone: the letter then comes out generic and has to be redone.

Can I have it write in another language?

Yes, to get corrections or suggested phrasing, but keep control of the meaning and have someone who masters the language read it.

Should I mention that I used AI?

Nothing is known to be mandatory in general, but check the instructions of the posting or the company. What matters is that the letter is true and you can stand behind it.
