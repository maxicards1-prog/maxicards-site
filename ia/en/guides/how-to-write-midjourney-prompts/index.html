How to Write a Good Midjourney Prompt

Midjourney responds better to a short, precise prompt than to a long, confused text.

In short

Here is a method to describe an image, adjust it and keep what works.

  1. Write a simple sentence
  2. Generate
  3. Add one element at a time
  4. Specify what you do not want
  5. Add parameters at the end of the prompt
  6. Note the winning prompt

Midjourney is a service that creates images from text. Its official documentation (docs.midjourney.com) says that a prompt is the text or phrase describing the desired image or video, that short, simple prompts generally give the best images, and that parameters are added at the end of the prompt. This guide deliberately gives neither prices nor a list of parameters: access, plans and available parameters change by version, so check Midjourney's official documentation for up-to-date information.

Transparency note: Midjourney's documentation pages refused access to our reading tool on 11/23/2026. The points above come from the public excerpt of the “Prompt Basics” page. For any specific parameter, check it in the documentation before using it.

The principle: a clear subject, a few well-chosen words

A Midjourney prompt is not a conversation. There is no point in writing “please, could you make me…”. It is a description. Keep the essentials: what (subject), how (style, material), where and when (setting, light), how it is framed (composition). Add everything else only if the result is not what you wanted.

How to do it

  1. Write a simple sentence describing the subject: “a wooden house by a lake.”
  2. Generate and look at what is missing compared to your idea.
  3. Add one element at a time: the style, then the light, then the framing. You will know what changed the result.
  4. Specify what you do not want only if the result contains a recurring unwanted element, checking the documentation for the official way to express it.
  5. Add parameters at the end of the prompt, as the documentation provides, after checking their syntax.
  6. Note the winning prompt and what improved it, to reuse it.

A structure to fill in

Here is a template to fill in order. It also works as a draft for other image generators.

[precise subject], [action or pose], [setting], [style or material], [lighting], [mood], [framing]

Filled-in example:

a lone lighthouse on a cliff, approaching storm, rough sea, oil painting, golden light between the clouds, dramatic mood, wide shot

The site's image prompt generator helps you fill in these elements without forgetting any.

Five examples to practice

1. A children's book illustration

a small red fox reading a book under a giant mushroom, fairy-tale forest, gouache children's book illustration, warm colors, soft light, peaceful mood, medium shot

2. A product photo

a glass perfume bottle on a white marble pedestal, plain beige background, studio photography, soft lighting and sharp reflections, luxurious and minimalist mood, centered framing

3. A video game environment

a floating city above the clouds, walkways and lanterns, concept art style, orange and purple evening light, dreamlike mood, very wide shot

4. A stylized portrait (invented character)

portrait of an imaginary sailor with silver hair, determined gaze, worn leather jacket, stormy sky background, classical painting style, contrasting side lighting, bust shot

5. A logo for a project idea

a simple emblem for a coffee shop-bookstore: cup and open book merged, minimalist line work, two colors, white background, flat vector style, centered composition

For a logo or any visual with text, check the result carefully: letters generated by AI often need fixing in an editing program.

Iterate without getting lost

  • Shorten before lengthening. If the result is confused, remove words instead of adding them.
  • Keep concrete words: material, color, light, shot, rather than vague adjectives (“beautiful,” “amazing”).
  • Change a single element between two tries.
  • Use a reference image if the official documentation allows it for your version: it describes how image prompts work.
  • Compare with another tool: ChatGPT and Nano Banana let you edit through conversation, whereas Midjourney works through successive prompts.

Common mistakes

  • Writing a paragraph with ten ideas: the tool keeps only a few.
  • Mixing incompatible styles (realistic photo and pencil drawing at the same time).
  • Forgetting the framing: without it, the subject may fall out of the image or stay tiny.
  • Imitating a living artist or a brand: a matter of copyright and trademark. Describe the style by its characteristics (material, palette, era).
  • Using the result without reading the service's terms for commercial use.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to write Midjourney prompts in English?

English is the safest choice for most users, but you can test other languages on your subject and keep what gives the best result. For parameters, follow the syntax in the official documentation.

Does a long prompt give a better result?

Not necessarily. The official documentation says short, simple prompts generally give the best images. Add details one at a time.

Where can I find Midjourney's prices and parameters?

In its documentation and official pages (docs.midjourney.com). We give no prices here because they change and are not in our 11/23/2026 survey.

Can I use Midjourney images commercially?

It depends on the service's terms and your plan. Read its official terms of use before any commercial use.
