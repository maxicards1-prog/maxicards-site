How to Write a Professional Email With ChatGPT

A good email takes a few lines, but writing it takes time.

In short

ChatGPT can propose a draft as long as it knows who you are writing to, why, and what you expect in return.

  1. Write the goal in one sentence
  2. Describe the recipient
  3. Give the facts
  4. Set the tone and length
  5. Reread and fix

The main trap is asking “write an email asking for an extension” and nothing else. You get a polite, generic and too-long text that the recipient will recognize as such. The method below gives the AI what makes an email useful: context, goal, tone, length.

How to do it

  1. Write the goal in one sentence: what do you expect from the recipient (a reply, an approval, a meeting, a payment) and by when?
  2. Describe the recipient: client, colleague, manager, supplier; whether you know them or not; formal or casual.
  3. Give the facts to include: dates, references, amounts, names. These are your facts, not the AI's.
  4. Set the tone and length: for example “friendly and direct, under 120 words.”
  5. Reread and fix: check the facts, remove filler phrases, add your own way of speaking.

Example 1: follow up with a client who isn't replying

You are an assistant who writes clear, courteous professional emails.
Context: I sent a quote on [date] to [contact's first name] at the company [name]. I haven't heard back in [duration].
Goal: find out whether the quote suits them and set up a 15-minute call this week if needed.
Tone: friendly, formal address, no pressure or guilt-tripping.
Constraints: under 110 words, a specific subject line, a single question at the end.
Give me 2 versions: one very short, one a bit warmer.

Example 2: announce a delay or a problem

Write an email from [my first name, my job title] to [recipient's name, their role] to inform them that [the deliverable] will be delivered on [new date] instead of [original date].
Actual cause to mention in one sentence, without excessive apology: [cause].
What I am doing to limit the impact: [concrete action].
What I expect from them: [confirmation, approval of a new schedule].
Tone: frank, professional, reassuring. Length: 100 words maximum. Suggest a subject line.

Example 3: improve an email you already wrote

Here is an email I wrote: [paste your text, without confidential data].
Rewrite it to be shorter and clearer, keeping the same meaning and my formal tone.
Put the main request in the first sentence.
Then list in three bullet points what you changed and why, so I can learn from it next time.

When it is better to write it yourself

  • A delicate email (disagreement, complaint, bad news): the AI can suggest a structure, but the right words come from you.
  • An email to someone you know well: a text that is too smooth will be noticed.
  • An email with legal or financial commitments: have the right person review it.

Confidentiality

Don't paste into a conversation emails from clients that contain personal data, sensitive business information or company secrets. Replace names with initials or generic labels. According to OpenAI's help page consulted on Nov 23, 2026, conversations on the individual services may be used to train models, and data settings exist: look for the data controls in your settings. Your company may also have rules on using AI: find out. To go further, read our guide ChatGPT and personal data.

The structure of an email that is read quickly

  • A specific subject line: “Quote #[number]: confirmation needed by [date]” beats “Following up on our conversation.”
  • The request in the first sentence, the context after, in two or three sentences.
  • One main request per email. If you have three, number them.
  • A clear deadline and a simple closing line, without a long pleasantry.

Example 4: reply to a complaint

I need to reply to an unhappy customer. Here are the facts, which I know well: [summary of the situation in 4 lines, without name or contact details].
What the company can offer: [goodwill gesture, replacement, refund, deadline].
What I cannot promise: [limits].
Write a calm reply that acknowledges the inconvenience without admitting fault I haven't confirmed, offers the solution and states the next step with a date.
Under 150 words. Also give an even shorter version for a text message.

Frequently asked questions

Does ChatGPT write good emails?

It writes decent drafts, but often too long and too polite. Give the length, the tone and whether to be formal or casual, then reread.

Can I paste a client's email so it replies to it?

Avoid it if the email contains personal or confidential data. Summarize the request in your own words, without names or sensitive figures.

How do I avoid an email that sounds artificial?

Give precise facts, ask for a short text, delete stock phrases and rewrite a few sentences in your own words.
