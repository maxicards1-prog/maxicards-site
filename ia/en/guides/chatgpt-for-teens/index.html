ChatGPT for Teens: Age, Family Rules and Parental Controls

Teenagers already use AI for homework and curiosity.

In short

This guide sums up what the official pages say about age, parental consent and parental controls, then suggests simple rules.

For many parents the question is concrete: can my child use ChatGPT, and under what conditions? The facts below come from OpenAI's official help pages consulted on Nov 23, 2026. They may change: check them before you decide.

Minimum age and parental consent

According to OpenAI's help page on ChatGPT and age, the service is not meant for children under 13. For ages 13 to 18, OpenAI says it requires parental consent before use. The same page adds that OpenAI recommends caution with young people, including those who meet the age requirements, because the service can produce answers that are unsuitable for some ages. For educational use with children under 13, the page says the interaction must be led by an adult.

ChatGPT's parental controls

According to the parental controls help page, the parent invites the teen from ChatGPT's settings (the “Parental controls” section), or the teen invites their parent, and the other must accept. A parent can link several teens; each teen is linked to only one parent. Among the settings described:

  • reduce sensitive content (on by default);
  • decide whether conversations are used to improve models;
  • turn memory, voice mode and image generation on or off;
  • turn on a “study mode” and “study hours” (which don't block access);
  • set “quiet hours” during which ChatGPT cannot be used.

The point to know: these controls do not give access to the teen's conversations or history. Safety notifications may be sent to the parent in some serious cases (for example a serious risk of self-harm), without a transcript; they are not real-time monitoring or a replacement for professional help. In case of immediate danger, contact emergency services. Accounts can be unlinked at any time, and the controls end when the teen turns 18.

How to do it: set up supervised use

  1. Talk before you configure. Ask your child what they expect from it and what they already do.
  2. Check age and consent: no account before 13; parental consent between 13 and 18.
  3. Link the accounts if you wish and set the options together following the help page.
  4. Set simple rules (see below) and write them down.
  5. Check in regularly, without spying: show interest, ask them to give you a demonstration.

Family rules to suggest

  • No personal data: no full name, address, number, photo or information about classmates. Also look up the youth privacy resources published by your country's data protection authority.
  • AI helps you understand, not cheat: asking for an explanation or exercises is useful; having it write an assignment generally breaks school rules. See how to study with ChatGPT.
  • We check: an AI answer can be wrong. For homework, cross-check with the course or a textbook.
  • We don't treat AI as a doctor, a therapist or their only friend: for a health problem, bullying or distress, we talk to a trusted adult or a professional.
  • We know how to ask for help: if an answer makes you uncomfortable, stop and talk about it.

A prompt to try together

I'm in [grade] and I didn't understand [concept]. Explain it to me with simple words and an everyday example.
Then ask me 3 questions to check that I understood, one by one, and correct me gently without giving the answer right away.
Don't do my exercise for me: help me find the method.

For parents: what to tell your teen

A flat ban often pushes kids to work around it. Better to explain why: AI can be wrong, it is not a confidant, and what you write in a conversation should not contain data about yourself or others. Suggest a trial together: ask a question whose answer you know and see together whether the answer is right. This exercise shows better than a speech that a well-written answer is not necessarily accurate.

If your child talks about bullying, distress or situations that worry them, it is for a trusted adult or a professional to take over, not a tool.

Frequently asked questions

At what age can a child use ChatGPT?

According to OpenAI's help page, the service is not meant for under-13s, and 13 to 18 year-olds must have their parents' consent.

Can parents read their teen's conversations?

No, according to the parental controls help page: they give access to neither conversations nor history. They let you set certain options and be alerted in some serious cases.

Are parental controls mandatory?

The help page presents them as an option to link accounts by invitation, which the teen must accept. They don't replace conversation within the family.
