How to Improve Your LinkedIn Profile With ChatGPT

A clear profile is read in a few seconds.

In short

AI can help you find the right words for your headline, summary and messages, as long as you give it true facts.

  1. Define your goal
  2. Gather your facts
  3. Work on the headline first
  4. Write the “About” section
  5. Rework each experience
  6. Polish your outreach messages
  7. Reread and check consistency

An online professional profile is often the first thing a recruiter or client looks at. It must quickly answer three questions: what do you do, for whom, and with what proof? Many profiles fail through excess vagueness (“passionate professional”) or jargon. An AI is effective at rewording, trimming and testing several versions, as long as the substance comes from you.

A profile is public: publish only what is true and what you would accept reading in front of a recruiter, a former colleague or a client. Never add a job, a degree or a result you can't prove. Length limits and sections are the ones shown by the network itself: check them in your profile editor, as they can change.

How to do it

  1. Define your goal: find a job, attract clients, be contacted by recruiters in your field, grow a network.
  2. Gather your facts: jobs, duties, achievements, education, skills. Remove confidential client and team names.
  3. Work on the headline first: it is what people see everywhere, next to your name (prompt 1).
  4. Write the “About” section from your own outline, then have it reviewed (prompt 2).
  5. Rework each experience with facts, not generic responsibilities (prompt 3).
  6. Polish your outreach messages: short, personalized, with a clear request (prompt 4).
  7. Reread and check consistency with your resume: same dates, same titles.

Six prompts for your profile

1. The profile headline

Here is what I actually do: [job, specialty, target audience or industry]. My goal: [be contacted by recruiters at X / find Y clients].
Suggest 8 profile headlines of 120 characters maximum, in different styles (factual, results-oriented, audience-oriented). Avoid cliches ("passionate," "expert"). Add no skill I haven't mentioned. Rank them by clarity.

2. The "About" section

I want to write my "About" section in the first person. First ask me 5 questions: what I do, for whom, how I work, what I'm proud of with a specific fact, what I'm looking for. After my answers, suggest only a 4-block outline with the key sentence of each. I'll then write the text myself and submit it to you for review.

3. Rework an experience

Here is the current description of one of my jobs: [paste, without proper names].
Tell me what is vague or repetitive and ask me three questions to get facts (result, volume, difficulty overcome). Then suggest two rewrites of three lines maximum that use only what I answered. No invented data.

4. A message to connect

I want to contact [type of person: a recruiter in industry X / an alum of my school / a professional in job Y]. My goal: [ask a question about their job / introduce myself for a position]. Here are two things we have in common: [true elements].
Suggest three versions of a message of 4 sentences maximum: some context, a specific reason for contacting them, a simple request that is easy to decline. Polite, natural tone, with no flattery or stock phrases.

5. A post about my work

I want to publish a post about [topic: a finished project, something I learned, a lessons-learned story], aimed at [audience]. Here are the facts: [true list]. Suggest a 4-part outline (hook, context, lesson, question to the reader) and three hook ideas. Don't write the whole post. Avoid any confidential information from my employer and any figure I haven't given.

6. Audit my profile

Here is the text of my profile (headline, about, two experiences), anonymized: [paste].
Play a recruiter in the [industry] sector who spends 20 seconds on it. Tell me what they remember, what they don't understand, and what is missing for me to get [goal]. Rank your comments by importance. Don't rewrite anything.

Mistakes to avoid

  • A sanitized profile. Generated texts all look alike. Keep true, concrete details that set you apart.
  • Revealing confidential information. Client names, internal figures, unannounced projects: put them neither in the prompt nor in the profile.
  • Mass messages. A message that mentions nothing specific about the person is ignored, or even badly received.
  • Assuming the AI knows your industry. According to OpenAI's official help center, its answers can be incorrect: recheck technical terms and job titles.
  • Pasting unnecessary personal data. For individual accounts, OpenAI's help center explains that your content may be used to improve models unless you change the data controls. Anonymize in every case.

Frequently asked questions

Can I let the AI write my whole profile?

It is not recommended: the text would be generic and could contain errors. Provide the facts, ask for questions and reviews, then write it yourself.

What information should I never publish?

Anything confidential (clients, internal figures, unannounced projects) and unnecessary personal information such as your address or phone number.

How long should the summary be?

A few short paragraphs are enough. Respect the limits shown in the network's editor, which can change.

Can AI help me reply to a recruiter?

Yes, to structure a message or shorten it. Keep control of the content, and always reread before sending.
