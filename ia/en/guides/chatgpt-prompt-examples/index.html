12 ChatGPT Prompt Examples to Copy and Adapt

Twelve complete prompts grouped into four everyday uses, with what makes each one work.

In short

Replace the brackets with your own information.

  1. Choose the example closest to your need.
  2. Replace each [bracket] with your real information, without sensitive data.
  3. Send it, then correct in small touches (“shorter,” “more concrete”).
  4. Keep the version that works in a file of your own.

An example is useful when you understand why it works. Each prompt below is followed by a short explanation. They follow OpenAI's advice: give the context, specify the expected result, length and format, and say what to do rather than what to avoid. They also work in Claude or Gemini. Write and rephrase

1. Rephrase a delicate message

Here is a message I want to send to [recipient: a coworker, a landlord…]:
###
[your draft]
###
Rephrase it so it is firm but polite, with no personal blame. Keep all the facts and add nothing. Give me two versions: a short one (3 sentences) and a more detailed one.

Why it works: the draft is isolated by ### delimiters, the facts are protected (“add nothing”), and you ask for two versions to compare.

2. Write a listing

Write a listing to sell [item] on a classifieds site. Real features: [list]. Flaws to mention honestly: [list]. Price: [price]. Format: a title of 8 words maximum, then 5 lines of description, then one line on the terms for local pickup.

Why: the format is quantified (8 words, 5 lines) and you require honesty about flaws, so nothing is invented.

3. Proofread without rewriting

Correct only the spelling, grammar and punctuation of the following text. Change neither the style nor the meaning. Return the corrected text, then a bulleted list of the corrections made.
###
[text]
###

Why: the limit (“only”) stops the AI from rewriting in its own way, and the list of corrections lets you check.

Understand and learn

4. Explain a concept at your level

Explain [concept] to me as if I were [profile: a high school student who has never done accounting]. Use an everyday analogy, then a worked example with numbers. End with three questions to check that I understood, without giving the answers right away.

Why: the profile sets the level, the analogy makes it concrete, and questions without answers turn reading into practice.

5. Summarize a long text

Summarize the text below in 5 points maximum, one sentence each. Then add a “Key takeaway” line and a line “What the text does not say but should be checked.” Use only the information in the text.
###
[text]
###

Why: the line on what is missing encourages critical thinking rather than simple shrinking.

6. Prepare a study sheet

From my course notes below, create a one-page study sheet: key definitions, three examples, and five review questions with the answers separated at the end of the sheet. Point out the passages of the course that you find unclear.
###
[your course notes]
###

Why: you start from your own course, so the content matches what you are asked. The AI helps you practice; your school's rules on using it still apply.

Organize and plan

7. Build a realistic schedule

I have [number] hours per week for [goal: learning the basics of a piece of software] over [duration]. My starting level: [level]. Suggest a week-by-week schedule, with a goal and a concrete deliverable for each session. Include one catch-up week. Format: table.

8. Prepare a meeting

I have to lead a [duration] meeting with [participants] to decide [topic]. Suggest a timed agenda, the questions to ask to reach a decision, and a template for the minutes in three sections: decisions, actions (who, what, when), postponed items.

9. Make a sorted to-do or shopping list

Here, in no particular order, is what I have to do this weekend: [list]. Sort the items by priority (urgent, important, can wait), group those that are done in the same place, and suggest an order of execution for Saturday and Sunday, taking into account [constraint: only one car, stores closed Sunday afternoon…].

Think and decide

10. Compare two options

I am torn between [option A] and [option B] for [goal]. My criteria, in order of importance: [criterion 1, criterion 2, criterion 3]. Present a comparison table on these criteria, then the risks of each option, then the questions I should ask myself before choosing. Do not decide for me.

Why: “do not decide” keeps the decision with you. For health, legal or money topics, the AI does not replace a professional.

11. Play devil's advocate

Here is my project: [description in 5 lines]. Play a skeptical but well-meaning counterpart: give the five strongest objections, ranked from most serious to least serious, and for each one a way to answer it. Stay concrete.

12. Prepare for a difficult conversation

I have to tell [person] that [difficult message]. Help me prepare: three opening lines, the likely reactions, and a calm answer to each. Then play the person: I will reply, and you will react as they would.

When these examples are not enough

If the result is still disappointing, read why ChatGPT gives wrong answers: you will find the most frequent causes there. And to build a prompt step by step, the prompt generator uses the same structure.

Frequently asked questions

Can I copy these prompts into Claude or Gemini?

Yes. They rely on shared principles: context, constraints, format. Answers vary from one tool to another, so compare if the result is important.

What should I put in place of the brackets?

Your real information, but without personal or confidential data if you can avoid it: use generic terms like “my landlord” rather than names.

Why ask for the format in every prompt?

OpenAI recommends specifying length and format to get more consistent, easier-to-use answers. Without a format, the AI chooses for you.
