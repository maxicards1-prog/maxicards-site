How Does Generative AI Work?

A generative AI produces content by choosing the most likely result given its training data.

In short

It does not look facts up, it computes.

Short answer. Generative AI is a system able to create content such as text, code, images, music, audio or video. It was trained on very large amounts of data and works probabilistically: it produces the most likely result given what it learned. It is not a knowledge base, which is why mistakes happen (definitions from the French data protection authority CNIL, checked 26/11/2026).

What AI is, and generative AI in particular

"AI" is the general term for programs that perform tasks that usually require human intelligence. Generative AI is one branch: instead of classifying or predicting, it creates. When it can handle a wide range of tasks, like the large language models (LLMs) behind ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, the CNIL calls them general-purpose systems.

Three steps to understand it

  1. Training. The model is fed very large amounts of data. The CNIL lists data from the internet, licensed third-party sources, conversations written by human trainers, interactions with users and synthetic data. The model learns regularities: how words, images or sounds follow one another.
  2. Your request. Your message, the prompt, is cut into small pieces called tokens (see what are tokens in AI) that the model reads.
  3. Generation. The model writes its answer piece by piece, each time picking what is most likely given your request and what came before. It is not a dictionary lookup: the answer is built on the spot.

How AI was created

We do not retrace the history of the field here, which needs specialist sources. Remember the current logic: researchers design a model, train it on huge collections of data, then teams adjust it, notably with conversations written by human trainers, before offering it to the public as an assistant. Providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google document their own products.

Why it makes mistakes

Because it picks the most likely result, a generative AI can give an answer that is inaccurate but plausible, which the CNIL calls a hallucination. This happens notably on information absent from its training, such as events after its development. OpenAI's help center confirms it for ChatGPT: the model can produce wrong facts, dates or quotes with confidence, and without web search its answers rely on training data with a knowledge cutoff. See why ChatGPT gives wrong answers for causes and fixes.

What this means for you

  • Treat the answer as a draft. OpenAI's help center recommends exactly that, and checking quotes, data and references.
  • Give context. The more precise your request, the better the most likely answer fits your need: see how to write a good prompt.
  • Do not enter personal or confidential data in a consumer service, advice the CNIL also gives. For ChatGPT, read ChatGPT privacy settings.
  • Do not copy answers as they are for important decisions.

Text, image, sound: same principle

For images, the model learns which shapes, colors and styles go with which words, then builds a new image matching your description. For sound and video the logic is similar. In every case what you get is a statistical creation, never a controlled copy of one precise source. That is why you should check what you publish and respect other people's rights.

The limits the CNIL points out

The CNIL notes that the "black box" operation makes it hard to explain results and detect bias. These systems can also be used for disinformation (deepfakes), to create malicious code or to provide dangerous information, and excessive trust without verification can lead to wrong decisions.

Frequently asked questions

How does generative AI work?

It learns from very large amounts of data, then produces the most likely result from your request. It computes probabilities instead of consulting a database of facts.

What is generative AI?

It is a system able to create content: text, code, images, music, audio or video, according to the CNIL's definition.

Why does AI make mistakes?

Because it picks the most likely answer, which can be plausible but wrong. The CNIL calls these hallucinations, especially on information missing from its training.

Is AI dangerous?

The CNIL lists risks: disinformation through deepfakes, malicious code, dangerous information and wrong decisions caused by too much trust. Always verify outputs.

Can I trust what AI says?

Not blindly. OpenAI's help page recommends treating answers as a first draft and checking quotes, data and references.
