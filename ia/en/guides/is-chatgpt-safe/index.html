Is ChatGPT Safe and Accurate?

ChatGPT is useful but not infallible: OpenAI itself recommends checking its answers.

In short

Here is when to trust it and how to verify.

The short answer: ChatGPT is dependable for rephrasing, explaining, structuring and drafting, but not for anything that must be exact: figures, dates, quotations, references, medical or legal advice. OpenAI writes that its answers can be inaccurate or misleading and recommends checking them. Treat it as a fast assistant, not as a source.

What OpenAI says

In its help center, OpenAI explains that the models were trained on very large amounts of human-written text, which makes answers sound natural, but that results can be inaccurate, untrue or misleading. The company advises checking whether answers are correct and offers a “thumbs down” button to report an error. Mistral, another provider, gives the same warning in its help: the tool can produce incorrect answers and you should check them. No current assistant lets you skip proofreading.

One thing to keep in mind: the assistant writes with confidence even when it is wrong. A confident tone is therefore not a sign of accuracy.

When you can rely on it (with a read-through)

  • Rephrasing, shortening or correcting a text you give it: see proofread with ChatGPT.
  • Explaining an idea at a given level, with examples.
  • Proposing an outline, ideas, angles, a list of questions to ask yourself.
  • Summarizing a document you provide: see summarize a document.

When you must always verify

  • Figures, percentages, dates, proper names and quotations.
  • References, links and book titles: a plausible-looking link or source may not exist.
  • Health, law and money: the assistant replaces neither a professional nor an official text.
  • Recent facts: the free plan can search the web, but you need to open the cited sources to check.

How to do it: check an answer in five steps

  1. Ask for sources, then open them yourself. A source that does not open or does not say what is claimed is a warning sign.
  2. Cross-check against an official site: a government body, the product maker, the text of the law, the organization concerned.
  3. Ask the question again another way, or in a new conversation. An answer that changes from one try to the next needs checking.
  4. Ask it to list its uncertainties: “Which parts of your answer are the least certain?”
  5. Read aloud whatever will be published or sent under your name.

To reduce errors, supply the reference text yourself and ask the assistant to use only that. The prompt generator includes this kind of instruction.

And privacy?

Accurate does not mean private. OpenAI says it may use content from individual accounts to train its models, with a way to opt out in the data controls, and asks users not to share sensitive information in conversations. The guide ChatGPT privacy settings covers the settings to know. Options change: look for “Data controls” in your account settings.

Frequently asked questions

Is ChatGPT safe?

Used carefully, yes: the risks are mostly a wrong answer taken as true and sensitive data pasted into the chat. Check answers and never share confidential information.

Is ChatGPT free?

Yes, OpenAI says ChatGPT is free to use, with limits on tools. It is not private by default and not always right, so read the privacy settings and verify what matters.

Is ChatGPT better than Gemini?

It depends on the task. Compare both on your own requests; see our ChatGPT vs Gemini guide.

Is ChatGPT or Claude better?

There is no general answer: test the same request on both and judge accuracy, clarity and format. OpenAI itself recommends checking any assistant's answers.

Is ChatGPT AI?

Yes, it is an artificial intelligence assistant built on a language model, made by OpenAI.
