What Is ChatGPT?

ChatGPT is OpenAI's conversational assistant.

In short

This page explains in plain terms what it can do, how people use it and where its limits are.

The short answer: ChatGPT is an artificial intelligence assistant made by OpenAI. You write (or speak) to it like a person and it answers: it explains, drafts, translates, summarizes and helps you think. There is a free version and paid plans. Its answers can contain mistakes, so you need to read them critically.

What ChatGPT does, in practice

OpenAI's overview page describes three uses. “Chat” is for questions, learning and everyday help: explaining an idea, drafting a message, searching the web, generating an image, comparing options. “Work” is for work tasks: creating or editing documents, slide decks, spreadsheets or PDFs using the tools and context you connect. “Codex” is for coding. For personal use, the first one is what matters most.

OpenAI's help page for the free tier adds that you can search the web for up-to-date information, analyze data, upload images or files in your prompts and create images. Usage limits and the default model change over time: ChatGPT itself shows the limits that apply to your account.

How it works, without jargon

ChatGPT is built on a language model: a program trained on a very large amount of text to produce plausible wording. OpenAI says in its help center that answers can sound very human while sometimes being inaccurate, misleading or untrue, and recommends checking them. So remember: ChatGPT is an excellent assistant for drafts and explanations, not an authoritative source. See why ChatGPT sometimes answers off target.

Free or paid?

OpenAI states that ChatGPT is free to use. The free plan offers broad text chats, with separate limits for file uploads, image creation, voice and some other tools. According to OpenAI's help, ads may appear in the free plan in some countries. The paid plans (Go, Plus, Pro, then Business and Enterprise for organizations) mainly raise the limits and add access to more tools. For prices and a side-by-side view with other assistants, see the AI subscription comparison.

How to start

  1. Open the official site, chatgpt.com, or install the official app (see how to install ChatGPT).
  2. Ask a simple question, as you would ask a colleague: “Explain the difference between a credit and a loan in three sentences.”
  3. Then specify: who it is for, how long, what tone. The guide how to write a good prompt gives a five-step method.
  4. Read the answer, check figures and names, then ask for corrections.

To go further, read how to use ChatGPT, or use the prompt generator to get a well-built request.

What to avoid

  • Pasting personal or confidential data (passwords, client files, medical documents). OpenAI reminds users not to share sensitive information in conversations; see ChatGPT privacy settings.
  • Using an answer without checking it when it affects someone: a figure, a date, a quotation, medical or legal advice.
  • Passing off graded or evaluated work as your own: follow the rules of your school or employer.

Frequently asked questions

Is ChatGPT free?

Yes, OpenAI says ChatGPT is free to use. The free plan has limits (file uploads, images, voice) and may show ads in some countries. See our guide to the free version.

What is ChatGPT Go?

Go is a mid-level paid plan: OpenAI's pricing page presents it as expanded access, with more messages with tools, more uploads, more images and more voice than the free plan.

What is ChatGPT Plus?

Plus is the paid subscription with higher limits, priority access at busy times and broader model and tool access, according to OpenAI's help.

What is ChatGPT work mode?

According to OpenAI's overview, ChatGPT Work completes work tasks from start to finish: creating and editing documents, slide decks, spreadsheets or PDFs with your connected tools and apps.

What is ChatGPT Codex?

Codex is the part of ChatGPT meant for code: OpenAI describes it as a coding agent that helps understand, write, test and review code.

What is ChatGPT used for?

Everyday questions, learning, writing and editing, summaries, translation, images, and with the right plan, work tasks and code. Always check what matters.
