How to Create Images With ChatGPT

Describe, generate, then refine through conversation: here is a simple method for getting usable images, with complete prompts to adapt.

In short

  1. Open a new conversation
  2. Write a prompt with six elements
  3. Start the generation
  4. Correct through conversation
  5. Check the details
  6. Keep the prompt that worked

ChatGPT can produce images from a written description. According to OpenAI's official announcement, this image generation was rolled out by default to Plus, Pro, Team and free users, with limits that vary by plan. Menus and quotas change: check OpenAI's help for the current limits of your plan (plans and prices: plan comparison, checked on 11/23/2026).

What the provider announces

  • Readable text in the image: signs, menus, invitations. Text in other languages is cited by OpenAI as a known limit: always check the spelling you get.
  • Edits through conversation: you refine an image over several messages, and the model tries to keep a character or object consistent from one version to the next. The precision of edits is cited as a limit.
  • Uploaded images can serve as a base or as inspiration.
  • Generation time that can reach up to a minute.
  • Origin marking: images contain C2PA metadata indicating they come from OpenAI.
  • Restrictions: some requests are blocked, with stronger safeguards for images of real people.

How to do it

  1. Open a new conversation and look for the image creation option in the interface (the exact path changes: look at the tools offered near the input box).
  2. Write a prompt with six elements: subject, style, mood, composition, lighting, format.
  3. Start the generation and be patient: it can take up to a minute.
  4. Correct through conversation: one change per message (“keep everything, change the color of the coat to green”).
  5. Check the details: text, hands, objects, likeness. Generate again if needed.
  6. Keep the prompt that worked to reuse it.

The structure of a good image prompt

A vague prompt (“a cute cat”) gives a generic result. A precise prompt gives a result close to your idea. Here is the formula:

Create an image of [precise subject: a ginger cat sitting on a windowsill].
Style: [watercolor illustration / realistic photo / flat vector drawing].
Mood: [calm, winter morning].
Composition: [wide shot, the cat on the left, the blurred city in the background].
Light and colors: [soft morning light, blue and orange tones].
Format: [square / horizontal 16:9 / vertical for a story].
Avoid: [text in the image, extra characters].

The image prompt generator tool fills in this structure for you.

Three complete examples

An illustration for an article

Create an illustration for a blog post about remote work. A person works at a desk near a window, with a plant and a mug. Style: flat vector illustration, palette of blues and yellows, light background. Horizontal 16:9 composition, subject on the right to leave room for the title on the left. No text in the image.

A poster with text

Create a vertical poster for a neighborhood block party. Title in large letters: “Neighbors' Day.” Subtitle: “Saturday, June 14, 6 p.m., Town Hall Square.” Style: colorful illustration, string lights and set tables, warm atmosphere. Make sure the text is readable and has no typos.

Then check every word: typos in the text of an image are the first thing to proofread.

An edit from a photo

Here is a photo of my living room. Keep the room, the furniture and the proportions exactly as they are. Change only the color of the back wall to sage green and add a plant in the right corner. Do not change anything else.

Correct without breaking everything

  • One message = one change. Several changes at once destabilize the result.
  • Say what to keep before what to change.
  • If the image drifts, go back to the version you liked and rephrase from it.
  • For a recurring character, always describe it with the same words (apparent age, clothes, hairstyle).

Common mistakes

  • Asking for too much in a single image. Simplify: a single subject, a simple setting.
  • Not rereading the text generated in the image.
  • Depicting real people: this is limited by the provider, and image rights come into play. Do not use a person's photo without their consent.
  • Using an image without checking the terms of use. For commercial use, read the provider's terms of use.
  • Thinking an AI image is a photo. For a real subject (a place, a product, an event), prefer a real photograph.

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT create images for free?

OpenAI's official announcement indicates a rollout to free users as well as paid plans, with variable limits. Check OpenAI's help for the current quotas of your plan.

Can I edit an existing image with ChatGPT?

Yes, OpenAI announces that you can refine an image through conversation and build on uploaded images. The precision of edits remains a cited limit.

Is the text in images reliable?

OpenAI announces improved text rendering, but cites multilingual text as a limit. Always reread every word.

How can I tell whether an image comes from ChatGPT?

According to OpenAI, generated images contain C2PA metadata indicating their origin.
