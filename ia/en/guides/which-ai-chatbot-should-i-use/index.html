Which AI Chatbot Should You Use? A Method by Need

With several assistants available, the right question is not “which is best” but “which suits what I do.” Here is how to decide.

In short

  1. Write down your three main tasks
  2. Pick two or three assistants
  3. Create a free account
  4. Rate each answer
  5. Check the data settings
  6. Decide after a week

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, Microsoft… The names multiply and each claims to beat the others. This guide offers no ranking: a ranking is out of date as soon as it is published. It offers a decision grid based on what you can check yourself, and a test method.

Step 1: define the need before choosing the tool

Complete this sentence: “I want an AI for [task], about [frequency], with [type of content: text, document, image, spreadsheet].” The more precise the sentence, the simpler the choice. Here are the most common families of needs:

  • Writing and rephrasing: emails, letters, articles, posts.
  • Working on documents: summarizing, extracting, comparing texts you supply.
  • Learning: explaining, quizzing, practicing.
  • Image creation: illustrations, edits, visuals.
  • Practical tasks: translation, planning, spreadsheets.

For each assistant you are interested in, collect the same information from its official page:

The main paid plans are ChatGPT (Go, Plus, Pro), Claude (Pro, Max), Google AI (Plus, Pro, Ultra), Mistral Pro and Microsoft 365 Premium; current prices are in the plan comparison. Each service also has a free plan.

How to do it

  1. Write down your three main tasks with a real example for each (an email to answer, a document to summarize, a concept to understand).
  2. Pick two or three assistants: those you already know or that people around you use, and those already tied to your accounts (Google, Microsoft).
  3. Create a free account on each and paste the same prompt, in a new conversation.
  4. Rate each answer on three points: accurate, usable, suitable tone. Repeat each test twice.
  5. Check the data settings of the chosen assistant before entrusting it with anything.
  6. Decide after a week, looking at whether the free limit blocks you. Only then, look at paid plans.

A reusable test prompt for all assistants

For a fair comparison, copy and paste exactly the same text. This one evaluates several abilities at once:

I am [profile: an undergraduate student, an office employee, a tradesperson…]. Here are three requests, handle them in order:
1) Write a 100-word email to [situation: ask a client to postpone an appointment].
2) Explain in 120 words the concept of [concept you master] to a beginner, with an example.
3) Ask me two questions to better understand my need, then suggest a 4-step action plan to [goal: organize a move].
If any information is missing, say so instead of making it up.

Pay particular attention to the last sentence: a good assistant flags what it does not know instead of guessing. For more elaborate prompts, use the prompt generator.

The pitfalls of choosing an AI

  • Trusting advertising or the latest announced model. A version name is no guarantee on your tasks.
  • Stacking subscriptions. A single well-chosen subscription is almost always enough; keep the others free.
  • Forgetting privacy. An assistant should not receive personal or confidential data; see the guide on personal data.
  • Not checking. Whatever the tool, reread and check numbers, dates and quotes.
  • Choosing for a single one-off need. If you only need a translation or an image, free is probably enough.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI should I choose as a beginner?

Take the free plan of a well-known assistant and test it on your own tasks for a week. There is no need to pay to start.

Should I take several AI subscriptions?

Generally no. A single subscription, if needed, and the other assistants on their free plans to compare an important answer.

How do I know whether an AI is reliable?

None is 100% reliable. Test it on a subject you master, ask it to say what it does not know, and always verify important facts.

Which AI is safest for my data?

Never paste personal or confidential data, whatever the tool, and read its data settings in the official help.
