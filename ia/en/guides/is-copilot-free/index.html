Is Microsoft Copilot Free?

Yes, Microsoft offers Copilot free of charge.

In short

The features inside Word or Excel, however, go through a subscription. Here is the line between the two.

The short answer: yes, Microsoft Copilot is free for chatting, searching and creating images, and sign-in is not required. The Copilot features inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook need a Microsoft 365 subscription (Personal, Family or Premium). For current prices, see our AI subscription comparison.

What is free

Microsoft's support page describes free Copilot as a no-cost assistant that gives direct answers, helps with writing and creates images. Among what is included, it lists help with writing, brainstorming and summarizing, answers based on real-time web search and, in Edge, summaries and insights for web pages. You reach it at copilot.cloud.microsoft, through an app (Windows, macOS, iOS, Android) or in Edge.

Microsoft says sign-in is not required. Signing in with a personal account adds chat history, image creation, longer conversations and voice. The free version is, however, subject to usage limits and availability; Microsoft does not give figures for them on the pages we read, so be wary of precise numbers you find elsewhere.

What needs a subscription

To use Copilot directly in desktop apps like Word and Excel, you need a Microsoft 365 subscription. Microsoft says owners of a Personal, Family or Premium subscription get Copilot in those apps, within a certain amount of usage. On the Family plan, Microsoft's product page says the AI features are available only to the subscription owner.

Prices change and differ by country: our comparison page lists the ones we checked, with the date, next to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Mistral.

What about work or school?

Microsoft says Copilot Chat, the web-grounded assistant, is available at no additional cost for eligible work or school accounts. Access to work data (files, email, chats) requires an extra paid license. Your IT administrator decides what is turned on.

How to do it: use it for free

  1. Open copilot.cloud.microsoft, the Copilot app or Copilot in Edge.
  2. Use it without signing in to test, or sign in with a personal account to keep history and create images.
  3. Write a complete request: goal, context, format. The prompt generator builds one for you.
  4. If you hit a limit, wait or compare with another free option: is Gemini free, the free version of ChatGPT.

Before you pay

Start by noting what you really lack. If you want to draft or summarize in Word or analyze a spreadsheet in Excel, the subscription makes sense; if you only want to chat or search, the free version is often enough. Either way, do not paste confidential data into an assistant and read the answers critically: see privacy settings for AI chatbots.

Frequently asked questions

Is Copilot free?

Yes, the consumer version is free. Features inside Word and Excel need a Microsoft 365 subscription.

Is Copilot free to use?

Yes for the web, app and Edge versions. There are usage limits, and signing in unlocks history, images and voice.

Is Copilot part of Microsoft 365?

Copilot features are included in Personal, Family and Premium subscriptions within usage limits. The free Copilot also exists on its own.

Is copilot good?

It is useful for everyday tasks, especially if you work in Word, Excel or Outlook. Test the same request on several assistants to judge.

Is Copilot the same as ChatGPT?

No. They are different products from different companies, Microsoft and OpenAI.

Is Copilot AI?

Yes, it is an artificial intelligence assistant made by Microsoft.
