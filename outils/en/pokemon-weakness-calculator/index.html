Type a Pokémon or pick its two types: you instantly see what knocks it out and what it shrugs off.

How to use the calculator

  1. Type a Pokémon's name (all 1,025 species, official English names): its types fill in on their own. Or pick one or two types by hand.
  2. Read the six groups: ×4 and ×2 attacks are the ones to avoid, while ×½, ×¼ and ×0 are the ones your Pokémon takes best.
  3. At the bottom, the “on offense” line shows the types that its same-type attacks hit for super effective damage: handy for picking moves.

How a double weakness works

Each defending type applies its multiplier, and the two multiply together. A Bug/Grass Pokémon like Paras takes ×2 from Fire on its Grass type and ×2 again on its Bug type: ×4 in total. On the other hand, a weakness and a resistance cancel out: Charizard (Fire/Flying) takes ×2 from Water on Fire and ×1 on Flying, so ×2; but Ground, super effective against Fire, does not hit it at all because the Flying type is immune (×0).

Examples from the calculator:

  • Fire / Flying (Charizard): ×4 against Rock; ×2 against Water, Electric; immune to Ground.
  • Water / Flying (Gyarados): ×4 against Electric; ×2 against Rock; immune to Ground.
  • Bug / Grass (Paras): ×4 against Flying, Fire; strongly resists (×¼) Ground, Grass.

Key takeaways

  • Immunities to know: Normal and Fighting cannot hit Ghost, Ground cannot hit Flying, Electric cannot hit Ground, Psychic cannot hit Dark, Poison cannot hit Steel, Dragon cannot hit Fairy, Ghost cannot hit Normal.
  • Steel resists 10 types and is only weak to Fighting, Ground and Fire: it is the best defensive type.
  • Before Gen 6, the Fairy type did not exist and Steel also resisted Ghost and Dark, so older games use a slightly different chart.
  • Some Abilities change everything: Levitate makes a Pokémon immune to Ground, Water Absorb or Flash Fire cancel a type. The calculator only takes types into account.

What about the trading card game?

The Pokémon TCG does not use this chart. It has only 10 types (Grass, Fire, Water, Lightning, Psychic, Fighting, Darkness, Metal, Dragon and Colorless; the Fairy type was dropped with the Sword & Shield series), and each card shows its own Weakness (often ×2) and sometimes a Resistance (−30) at the bottom of the card. Read the card, not the video game chart. To get started with the TCG, see our guide how to play Pokémon cards.

Frequently asked questions

What is a ×4 weakness?

It is a dual-type Pokémon whose two types are both weak to the same attacking type: the two ×2 multipliers multiply. For example, a Grass/Flying Pokémon takes ×4 from Ice attacks.

Is the Fairy type included?

Yes. The calculator uses the chart of the games since Gen 6 (Pokémon X and Y), with the Fairy type. Older games had a slightly different chart.

Are Abilities and held items taken into account?

No: only types count. An Ability such as Levitate, Water Absorb or Wonder Guard can cancel a weakness shown here.

Which type has no weakness?

No single type has zero. Some dual types keep only one, such as Ghost/Dark (weak only to Fairy since Gen 6) or Water/Ground (weak only to Grass, ×4).
