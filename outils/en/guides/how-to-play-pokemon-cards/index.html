How to Play Pokémon Cards: The Basic Rules

In short The Pokémon Trading Card Game is a two-player duel played with a 60-card deck. Here is the setup, how a turn works and how to win, without the jargon.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Playing the TCG · by the MaxiCards team

Start a Pokémon card game in 7 steps

  1. Prepare your deck. Each player shuffles their 60-card deck. A coin flip decides who goes first.
  2. Draw 7 cards. If your hand has no Basic Pokémon, show it, shuffle it back into your deck and draw 7 new cards. Your opponent may draw one extra card each time you do this.
  3. Place your Active Pokémon and Bench. Put one Basic Pokémon face down in the Active spot. You may place up to 5 more Basic Pokémon on the Bench, but you do not have to.
  4. Set your 6 Prize cards. Put the top 6 cards of your deck face down to the side: these are your Prize cards. Then flip your Pokémon face up.
  5. Take your turn. Draw a card, then take your actions: play Pokémon, evolve, attach an Energy, play Trainers, retreat. End with an attack if you want to.
  6. Knock Out and take a Prize card. When damage on a Pokémon reaches its HP, it is Knocked Out and its owner replaces it with a Benched Pokémon. The opponent takes one or more Prize cards.
  7. Win the game. You win by taking your last Prize card, by leaving your opponent with no Pokémon, or if they cannot draw a card.

Pokémon cards are not just for collecting. The Pokémon Trading Card Game (TCG) is a real duel game. Each player builds a deck, battles with their Pokémon and tries to Knock Out the opponent's. This guide covers the basic rules. The steps of a game are listed just above; the rest explains what you need to know to avoid mistakes.

What you need to play

  • Two players.
  • A deck of exactly 60 cards per player (Pokémon, Trainer cards and Energy cards).
  • A coin to decide who goes first, and something to count damage (damage counters worth 10 each, or pen and paper).
  • Optional: a playmat and card sleeves. To protect your cards, see our guide on how to protect your Pokémon cards.

When you are starting out, the easiest option is a pre-built deck or a starter deck. When you want to build your own, read how to build a Pokémon deck.

The three ways to win

A game can end in three ways. Only one of them has to happen:

  1. Take all 6 of your Prize cards. Each opposing Pokémon you Knock Out lets you take at least one Prize card. Taking the last one wins you the game.
  2. Leave your opponent with no Pokémon in play. If their Active Pokémon is Knocked Out and they have nobody on the Bench to replace it, they lose.
  3. Run your opponent out of cards. A player who has to draw at the start of their turn and cannot loses the game.

How many Prize cards per Knock Out?

Usually, a Knocked Out Pokémon gives one Prize card. Pokémon ex give two, and the Mega Evolution Pokémon ex of the current series give three. This is what makes big Pokémon powerful but risky: if they fall, your opponent moves ahead much faster. For older cards (V, VMAX, GX and so on), the rule is printed on the card, so always read it.

Reading a Pokémon card during a game

Four pieces of information matter most:

  • HP (hit points): when damage reaches this number, the Pokémon is Knocked Out;
  • attacks and their Energy cost; the Energy stays attached after the attack;
  • Weakness: against the type shown, damage is usually doubled;
  • Resistance and Retreat Cost: Resistance reduces damage (often by 30), and retreating costs as many Energy as the number shown. The value printed on the card always wins.

To decode the other symbols, see how to read a Pokémon card.

TCG types are not the same as video game types

The TCG has ten types: Grass, Fire, Water, Lightning, Psychic, Fighting, Darkness, Metal, Dragon and Colorless. The video games have eighteen. The Fairy type has even disappeared from the cards since the Sword & Shield series. A Colorless Energy requirement can be paid with any Energy, but not the other way around. Our weakness calculator follows the 18 video game types, so it is not the right tool for a TCG card's Weakness, which is always the one printed on the card.

The three families of cards

  • Pokémon: your fighters. A Basic Pokémon goes straight into play; the others evolve from a Pokémon already in play.
  • Trainer: Items (as many as you like per turn), Supporters (one per turn), Stadiums (one per turn) and Pokémon Tools.
  • Energy: they pay for attacks and for retreating. You can attach only one Energy from your hand per turn.

First-turn rules

The player who goes first cannot attack, play a Supporter or evolve a Pokémon on that first turn. The second player does not have these limits on their first turn. After that, a Pokémon can never evolve on the turn it was played.

Special Conditions

Some attacks make the Active Pokémon Poisoned, Burned, Asleep or Paralyzed. These conditions are checked between turns, during Pokémon Checkup. The details of each condition are in the official rulebook: keep it handy for your first few games.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Forgetting that attacking ends your turn: do all your other actions first.
  • Attaching two Energy cards or playing two Supporters in the same turn.
  • Evolving a Pokémon on the same turn it was played.
  • Having no Basic Pokémon on the Bench: one Knock Out and the game is over.
  • Playing cards that are no longer legal in the current format: see the Standard format and rotation.

Play on screen before you play on the table

Pokémon TCG Live lets you practice for free against another player, and your code cards can be used there (see how to redeem a Pokémon code card). Note that Pokémon TCG Pocket, on mobile, has its own shorter rules, covered in the Pocket beginner's guide.

Key points. A 60-card deck, 7 cards in hand, 6 Prize cards, one Energy and one Supporter per turn, and a single attack at the end of your turn. The official rulebook is the reference whenever you are unsure about a specific effect.

Frequently asked questions

How many cards do you need to play Pokémon cards?

Each player needs a deck of exactly 60 cards. You can have up to four cards with the same name, except basic Energy cards.

Who goes first in a Pokémon card game?

A coin is flipped, and the winner chooses to go first or second. The player who goes first cannot attack on their first turn.

Can you play Pokémon cards alone?

The TCG is a two-player game. To practice alone, you can use an online version such as Pokémon TCG Live or Pocket, which offer battles against the computer or other players.

Is a holo card stronger in a game?

No. Shininess and rarity have no effect on gameplay: only the text and values printed on the card matter.
