Shiny Hunting: Methods and Odds for Each Game

In short A shiny Pokémon appears very rarely, but the games offer methods to improve your chances. Here are the main ones, with the odds for each game.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Video games · by the MaxiCards team

Shiny Pokémon have existed since Gold and Silver (generation 2). Each species has only one shiny form; it is not stronger, because its nature and stats are random like any other Pokémon's. The game signals its appearance with a crystal-like sound and sparkles. This guide covers the base odds, the items and methods that improve them, and those specific to each game. To track your attempts, use the shiny hunt counter.

The base odds

From generation 2 to generation 5, the base probability is about 1 in 8,192. Since generation 6 (Pokémon X and Y), it has doubled to about 1 in 4,096 per encounter. Each encounter is a fresh roll. Having done 4,096 encounters guarantees nothing, and having done fewer does not stop you from finding one.

The Shiny Charm

The Shiny Charm is an item that adds extra rolls for every Pokémon you meet. It first appeared in generation 5: in Black 2 and White 2, it brings the probability to about 1 in 2,731. From generation 6 to generation 9, it gives about 1 in 1,365.67. Pokepedia says how to get it, usually by completing a Pokédex:

  • Sword and Shield: catch all 400 species of the Galar Pokédex, then talk to the Game Director in Wyndon.
  • Scarlet and Violet: complete the Paldea Pokédex; Jacq gives you the charm in the Academy's Biology room.
  • Pokémon Legends: Z-A: reach research level 50 in the game's Pokédex research.

The conditions vary from game to game: check the page for your game before you start.

The Masuda method

The Masuda method applies to breeding. It consists of placing two Pokémon from different original languages in the Day Care (for example one game in English and one in Japanese). The game then increases the number of rolls when the egg is created. Approximate probabilities per egg, according to Pokepedia:

Two Pokémon of the same language do not trigger the bonus. In generation 4, using the Masuda method with foreign parents disables the Everstone's 50% chance of passing down a parent's nature.

Which method should you choose?

  • You want a specific Pokémon, hatched from an egg: the Masuda method, with the Shiny Charm if you have it. Generation 6 and later games are the most favorable.
  • You are after wild Pokémon: look for the mechanics specific to your game (SOS chaining, mass outbreaks, Sparkling Power and so on).
  • You are just starting: avoid targeting Pokémon with very low encounter rates or locked ones. Check that a shiny of that species exists in the game you picked.

A few practical tips

  • Save your game before every encounter that matters: if the Pokémon is not shiny, you can reload without wasting time.
  • Count your attempts with a counter, so you do not get discouraged by losing track.
  • Remember that probabilities are averages: some players find a shiny in ten minutes, others after several thousand tries.
  • For a different challenge, combine the hunt with the rules of a Nuzlocke (the Shinylocke variant).

What about shiny cards?

The so-called "shiny" cards in the TCG are rarities in some expansions: they have nothing to do with the odds in the video games. To estimate your chances of pulling a specific card when opening booster packs, use the booster pack odds calculator instead.

Frequently asked questions

What are the odds of finding a shiny Pokémon?

About 1 in 8,192 from generation 2 to generation 5, and about 1 in 4,096 since generation 6, before any bonus.

Does the Shiny Charm double your odds?

Not exactly: it adds extra rolls. Since generation 6, the odds go from about 1/4,096 to about 1/1,366 for wild encounters.

Does the Masuda method work in every game?

It has existed since generation 4 and needs two Pokémon of different languages in the Day Care. It gives about 1/683 per egg since generation 6.

Is a shiny stronger than a normal Pokémon?

No. A shiny Pokémon has the same abilities; only its color changes.
