Pokémon card size and sleeve dimensions: the complete guide

In short A Pokémon card fits a "standard size" sleeve. Here are the measurements to know, how to check them at home and how to double sleeve without damaging the card.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Protection and storage · by the MaxiCards team

How big is a Pokémon card?

A Pokémon trading card measures 63 x 88 mm, or 2.5 x 3.5 inches. Sleeve makers call this "standard size": Dragon Shield, for example, says its standard sleeves fit cards up to 63 x 88 mm and lists Pokémon among the games that use the size, along with Magic, Lorcana and Flesh and Blood. The format is shared with other games, so a sleeve sold as "standard" fits your Pokémon cards.

A card's thickness is small, but it matters when choosing a rigid holder. The standard toploader is made for cards up to 35 pt, which is about 0.9 mm (one point is one thousandth of an inch).

Makers round numbers differently, so the same "standard" size can vary by 1 mm from one brand to another. That matters mostly for sleeves that fit very close to the card.

The three kinds of sleeves

  • Deck sleeve: thick plastic, often with an opaque or decorated back, made for playing. It is the most common type in stores.
  • Penny sleeve: thin and clear on both sides, used to protect collectible cards. It then goes into a toploader or semi-rigid holder.
  • Tight-fit sleeve ("perfect fit", "pro-fit"): slightly smaller than a deck sleeve, it hugs the card and is often used as an inner sleeve. If you plan to send cards to a grader, check its packing rules first, because some companies ask you to avoid very tight sleeves.

How to double sleeve

Double sleeving means putting the card in a first sleeve, then a second, larger one over it. It limits edge wear and protects the opening of the inner sleeve.

  1. Take a tight-fit inner sleeve that does not bend the card's corners.
  2. Insert the card flat, all the way to the bottom.
  3. Take a second, larger standard sleeve and flip it so that the opening of the inner sleeve ends up against the closed end of the outer one.
  4. Slide the first sleeve into the second without bending or creasing it.

Double sleeving is useful for cards you handle, but it makes the card thicker: check that it still fits in the toploader or binder pocket. Do not use it for cards going to a grader, since the company may refuse a sleeve that is too tight.

Check the size at home

With a ruler, measure the card's width and height: you should read about 63 and 88 mm. A "jumbo" card (an oversized card sold in boxes or as a promo) is bigger and will not fit a standard sleeve, so look for jumbo sleeves. For other cards, a ruler also helps spot a trimmed card or a fake whose size is off: see the tests for spotting a fake card.

The most common sizing mistakes

  • Buying sleeves made for other, smaller-format games: the card floats and slides around.
  • Choosing binder pages that are too tight for cards that are already sleeved.
  • Putting a thick deck sleeve in a semi-rigid holder that is too snug, which bows the card.
  • Forgetting that some brands do not make exactly the same size from one line to another.

To decide which protection to use for the value of your cards, read how to protect your Pokémon cards. For binder pages that suit sleeved cards, see the binder guide.

Frequently asked questions

What size sleeve do I need for Pokémon cards?

Standard size, made for cards up to 63 x 88 mm. Makers label it "standard" or sometimes "Magic / Pokémon".

Do Magic sleeves fit Pokémon cards?

Yes. Both games use the standard size, as Dragon Shield and other makers state on their pages.

Are Pokémon cards bigger than regular playing cards?

Regular playing cards often come in a different size. Always measure before buying sleeves for another use.

Can a sleeve bend the card?

A sleeve that is too tight can bend the corners as you insert the card. Choose the right size and slide the card in without forcing it.
