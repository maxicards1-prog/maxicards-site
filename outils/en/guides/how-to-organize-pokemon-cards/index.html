How to organize and sort your Pokémon cards

In short Three steps are enough to bring order: sort, pick a system, store. Here are the most popular methods and how to avoid sorting mistakes.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Protection and storage · by the MaxiCards team

Before you organize: sort

Start by splitting your cards into four piles, so you do not mix up what to keep, play, sell or throw away:

  • To keep in your collection: cards you love or that are rare.
  • To play: cards for a deck (see below).
  • To sell or trade: duplicates and cards you have no use for.
  • Damaged cards: bent or torn cards, for use as markers or for kids.

To decide what is worth keeping or selling, our guide on how to tell if a Pokémon card is valuable offers a quick sort.

The four sorting methods

MethodHow it worksAdvantageLimit
By set (series)Group by set symbol, then by numberFollows the official order and shows what you are missingYou need to know how to read the symbol and number
By Pokédex numberAll cards of the same Pokémon together, by national numberHandy for collectors of one Pokémon familyVersions pile up
By typeFire, Water, Grass and so onUseful for building decksNot suited to a set-by-set collection
By rarityCommons, uncommons, rares, special cardsPuts the best-looking cards firstRarity is hard to read on older cards

To read the set and number, see our guide on how to read a Pokémon card. Rarity symbols are explained in the rarity symbols guide.

The simplest method for a mixed collection

  1. First sort by set: one box, one binder section or one divider per series.
  2. Within each set, sort by card number in ascending order: the number is printed at the bottom of the card.
  3. Keep duplicates in a separate box labeled "to sell or trade".
  4. Set aside valuable cards in stronger protection, as described in the protection guide.

This method has one big advantage: at any time, you know which cards you still need to finish a set.

Keep an inventory

A spreadsheet is enough. Use one row per card, with these columns: card name, set, number, language, condition (see Mint, Near Mint and Excellent conditions), number of copies, purchase price if you know it, and storage location. Update values only now and then: prices move every day, so do not waste time tracking them. The inventory helps you track purchases, prepare a sale or support an insurance claim. For selling, see where to sell your cards.

Storage: boxes, dividers, binders

  • Cardboard storage boxes: cheap, and suited to stacks of several hundred cards. Use card dividers labeled by set.
  • Binders: perfect for browsing. See how to choose a binder.
  • Toploaders or slabs: for valuable cards.

For all of them: store upright, in a room with a stable temperature, away from light and damp. Paper conservation specialists recommend relative humidity of 30% to 60% and avoiding swings in temperature and humidity, like those in an attic or basement.

What to do after opening a pack

Build one simple habit: lay the cards on a clean table, pick out the ones you like or that look rare, and slide them into a sleeve right away. Cards you do not care about go straight into a bulk box, sorted by set. The faster you sort, the less of a pile you build up. For a set you are collecting, keep a list of missing numbers: it also helps you spot the cards to look for at flea markets or online. Basic Energy cards are best kept apart: they are for decks and do not belong in the collection binder.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Stacking cards flat under heavy objects.
  • Putting rubber bands directly on cards, which marks the edges.
  • Mixing playing cards and collection cards in the same box, which leads to damage on the collection cards.
  • Writing on cards or their sleeves in ink, which can bleed through.
  • Forgetting to label: an unlabeled box always ends up sorted from scratch.

Finally, if a particular set interests you, the price-per-booster calculator helps you compare what you paid with the boxes on sale today.

Frequently asked questions

How do you sort Pokémon cards by set?

Find the set symbol and the number at the bottom of the card, group cards with the same symbol, then put them in ascending number order.

How should you store cards you play with often?

Keep them separate, in sleeves, in a deck box. Do not mix them with collection cards, so your valuable cards do not get damaged.

Should you sort cards alphabetically?

It works for small collections built around a Pokémon, but sorting by set and number is more reliable, because the number is printed on the card.

What is the best storage for cards you no longer touch?

A box with a rigid bottom and dividers, stored upright in a room with a stable temperature, away from light and damp.
