Gold Pokémon Cards: How to Spot a Real Gold Card

In short Between the gold Hyper Rares in boosters, the gold plated cards of 1999 and the metal cards sold for a few dollars, not everything gold is rare. Here are the reliable markers.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Authenticity and condition · by the MaxiCards team

Check a gold Pokémon card in 5 steps

  1. Identify the family. Cardboard or metal? A metal card is not a booster Hyper Rare: it comes from a promotion or is a novelty item.
  2. Read the rarity symbol. For a recent cardboard card, three gold stars mean a Hyper Rare, two gold stars a Special Illustration Rare, one gold star an Illustration Rare.
  3. Compare the number and the total. A secret card has a number higher than the set total. A consistent number is a good sign, but not proof.
  4. Look the card up in the set list. Check on an official list or a fan wiki that this card really exists, with this name and this image.
  5. Check the back, the text and the light. Apply the fake card tests: accent on Pokémon, back, fonts, light test, holo texture.

Three families of “gold” cards

When you search for a gold Pokémon card, you run into three very different things. Know which one is in front of you before you talk about value.

  1. Gold cards from boosters: cardboard cards with a gold background or frame. These are real cards from the game.
  2. The gold plated cards from the 1999 Burger King promotion: real promotional metal cards, but a special one-off campaign.
  3. Gold metal cards sold as novelty items: unless proven otherwise, these are decorative objects with no link to the game.

Gold cards from boosters: the Hyper Rare

Since the Scarlet & Violet series, Pokepedia links the three gold stars symbol to the Hyper Rare rarity. Illustration Rares carry one gold star and Special Illustration Rares two gold stars. These cards come in ordinary boosters, at low odds: the list of symbols is in Pokémon card rarity symbols. Before that series, gold cards were called something else: Cardmarket mentions “Secret Rare Gold” cards as having a real gold border. In Japan, Cardotaku links the UR (Ultra Rare) code to gold-type cards.

A handy marker: secret cards have a number higher than the set total (for example 83/82 for an older secret card, according to Pokepedia). That is a simple check, but it does not prove authenticity on its own: a counterfeiter can copy the number.

The 1999 Burger King gold plated cards

According to Bulbapedia, the Burger King promotion ran from November 8 to December 31, 1999: toys came with exclusive cards, each in 23-karat gold plated metal inside a plastic case. Dexerto adds that the cards were shown in a plastic Poké Ball with a certificate of authenticity. These are real objects, but very particular ones: if someone offers you one, ask for photos of the original packaging and the certificate. To set a price, compare with real sales of the same card in the same packaging, using the method in how to estimate the value of a Pokémon card; we give no price here.

Novelty “gold” metal cards

Marketplaces and shop windows are full of gold metal cards at throwaway prices, often presented as “rare cards” or “collector gifts”. We found no official source describing them as cards from the regular game. Treat them as decorative objects, do not confuse them with the official promotions described above, and never buy them thinking you are making an investment. If a listing promises great value for a metal card, that is a warning sign: read how to avoid Pokémon card scams.

Why a gold border can be suspicious

Cardmarket notes that real gold borders only appear on certain cards, such as the Delta Species holo rares and the gold secret rares. A gold border on an ordinary card should therefore make you cautious, especially since counterfeits often have sloppy holo effects. To recognize counterfeits more generally, go to how to tell if a Pokémon card is fake.

Is a gold card always worth a lot?

No. Rarity shows how often a card appears in boosters, not demand. A gold card that few people want may sell for little, and ordinary cards in high demand can be worth more. Cards are not a safe investment: only buy what you would like to keep. To work out what you keep when you resell, use the Cardmarket and eBay fee calculator, and to see your odds of pulling one, the booster pull chance calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Are gold metal Pokémon cards real cards?

Apart from official promotions like the 1999 Burger King gold plated cards, we found no source presenting the gold metal cards sold online as cards from the game. Check the origin before you buy.

What do three gold stars mean on a Pokémon card?

Since Scarlet & Violet, it is the symbol of the Hyper Rare rarity, according to Pokepedia.

When was the Burger King gold card promotion?

From November 8 to December 31, 1999, according to Bulbapedia.

Is a gold card more expensive than a regular card?

Not automatically: the price depends on demand, condition and the exact card, not on the color.
