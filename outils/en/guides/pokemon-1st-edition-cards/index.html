Pokémon 1st Edition Cards: How to Recognize Them

In short A small black stamp and a missing shadow: here is how to tell a real 1st Edition from a reprint, what “Shadowless” means, and why a stamp alone proves nothing.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Authenticity and condition · by the MaxiCards team

Recognize a Pokémon 1st Edition card in 5 steps

  1. Identify the set. The stamp only exists from Base Set to Neo Destiny (except Base Set 2). A more recent set cannot have an English-language 1st Edition.
  2. Look for the stamp in the right place. It is a “1” in a black circle, with the word EDITION above it. On a Base Set Pokémon card it is on the left side, halfway down; on a Trainer card, bottom left; on an Energy card, top right.
  3. Check that there is no shadow. Look at the right side of the artwork frame: on a Base Set 1st Edition, there is no drop shadow. A shadow points to a later print run.
  4. Read the copyright line. The words “1999-2000” point to the 2000 UK print run, not a 1st Edition.
  5. Compare, and have important cards authenticated. Compare with a reference photo of the same card, then consider grading if the card matters to you.

What “1st Edition” means

In the Pokémon Trading Card Game, 1st Edition means the first print run of a set. Later print runs are called “Unlimited”. Pokepedia explains that Wizards of the Coast, the original Western publisher, printed a symbol under the artwork on the left side, opposite the set symbol. Boosters and booster boxes carried the same logo. Later editions do not have this logo.

Which sets have 1st Edition cards?

According to Pokepedia, Wizards published stamped cards for every set from Base Set to Neo Destiny, except Base Set 2, which is a reprint. CGC says the symbol appeared on first print runs for about three years and ten sets, and that no English-language card carried it afterward. During the Neo series, Wizards struggled to get its cards out on time: some 1st Editions came out at the same time as the Unlimited editions, or even after them. Once the license passed to The Pokémon Company International in 2003, no more “1st Edition” cards were published outside Japan.

In Japan, the stamp still exists: according to CGC, Japanese 1st Editions were printed from the VS set (2001) through the 20th Anniversary set (2016). They are often more common than their unlimited versions, which shows that a stamp alone does not mean a card is rare.

The 1999 Base Set: three print runs

CGC distinguishes three main prints of the English Base Set:

  • 1st Edition: the stamp is present and there is no shadow on the artwork frame.
  • Shadowless: the second print run, same design but no stamp. Collectors call it “shadowless” because the frame does not yet have the drop shadow of later print runs.
  • Unlimited: the third print run, with changes meant to improve readability, including a drop shadow on the right side of the artwork frame.

A later English print run in 2000 in the United Kingdom reuses the Unlimited changes, but its copyright line reads “1999-2000”, and the cards often look lighter than in earlier print runs.

Practical consequence: a card that has both a “1st Edition” stamp and a shadow on the frame does not fit this description. Collector guides see that as a red flag. Do not conclude it is fake from that point alone, though. Go through the other clues in how to tell if a Pokémon card is fake.

What this guide does not cover

We give no price here: the price of a 1st Edition depends on the card, the set, the language and above all the condition. To estimate a card, follow the method in how to estimate the value of a Pokémon card. For a high-value card, independent grading is often more reassuring than a stamp: see what PSA grading is and which grading company to choose.

One more strong point from Cardmarket: a “2nd Edition” label is impossible, since there is only 1st Edition and Unlimited. Avoid any listing that mentions a second edition.

Cards in other languages

The rules above describe the English-language Base Set print runs. For a card in another language, the page of the set on a fan wiki such as Pokepedia is the best starting point to find out which print runs exist and what they look like. If your card is from a recent set, the stamp does not exist: do not buy a “1st Edition” card from a modern set.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Shadowless Pokémon card?

It is a card from the second Base Set print run: same design as the 1st Edition but with no stamp, and without the drop shadow added in later print runs.

Does every set have a 1st Edition?

No. The stamp stopped after Neo Destiny. Base Set 2 has none, and there have been none in English since.

Is a 1st Edition card with a shadow on the frame fake?

It is an inconsistency for the Base Set, since the 1st Edition has no shadow. Look for other clues before you conclude.

Where is the stamp on a Trainer or Energy card?

According to CGC, on a Base Set Trainer card it is at the bottom left, and on an Energy card at the top right.
