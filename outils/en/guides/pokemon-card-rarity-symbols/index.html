Pokémon Card Rarity Symbols: What Do They Mean?

In short The small symbol at the bottom of the card shows its rarity, meaning how likely it is to show up in a booster. Here is the list of symbols since Scarlet & Violet, the Japanese codes (AR, SAR, RR and more), and what rarity does not tell you.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Reading a card · by the MaxiCards team

What does rarity actually mean?

According to Pokepedia, a French fan wiki, rarity is the probability of pulling a card in a given set. It describes how often a card appears in boosters, not its price. A very rare card can still be unpopular, while a common card from a very old set can be hunted by collectors. Rarity is a clue, never a price.

Since Scarlet & Violet: one symbol per rarity

Before the Scarlet & Violet series, symbols were mostly generic. Since then, each rarity has its own symbol. According to Pokepedia's table for international sets:

RaritySymbol on the card
CommonA black circle
UncommonA black diamond
RareA black star
Double RareTwo black stars
Ultra RareTwo silver stars
Illustration RareOne gold star
Special Illustration RareTwo gold stars
Hyper RareThree gold stars
Shiny RareThe outline of a gold star (since Paldean Fates)
Shiny Ultra RareThe outline of two gold stars (since Paldean Fates)
ACE SPEC RareA raspberry-colored star (since Temporal Forces)
Black White RareOne white star and one black star (Black Bolt and White Flare)
Mega Hyper RareA yellow and black four-pointed star (since the Mega Evolution series)
Mega Attack RareA pink star and a green star (Mega Evolution: Ascended Heroes)

The Mega Evolution series then brought other rarities tied to the 30th Anniversary set, according to Pokepedia: a Pikachu rarity (a black Pikachu head), a futuristic rarity (a turquoise star) and an RGB rarity (a red, green and blue symbol). Official English names may differ from this plain description. Promo cards carry a “Promo” symbol, which is not a rarity: that is why a promo has no rarity symbol and can still be holographic.

Before Scarlet & Violet

In the previous era, symbols were more generic: circle (Common), diamond (Uncommon), star (Rare), then variants for higher rarities, such as three stars for a shiny holo, a holo star for cards that are always holographic, a gold star for LEGEND cards, and a colorful “M” for Amazing Rares, according to Pokepedia. If you have an older card whose symbol is not listed here, open its set page on a fan wiki to see which symbols that set uses.

Japanese codes: C, U, R, RR, AR, SAR…

In Japan, rarities are mostly letters, since the Black & White series. Pokepedia gives this list:

CodeMeaning
C, U, RCommon, Uncommon, Rare
RRDouble Rare (since XY1)
RRRTriple Rare (since S1W)
SRSuper Rare
URUltra Rare
HRHyper Rare (since SM1S)
ARArt Rare (since S12a), the equivalent of Illustration Rare
SARSpecial Art Rare (since S12a), the equivalent of Special Illustration Rare
ACE SPECACE SPEC Rare
BWRBlack White Rare
MURMega Ultra Rare (Mega Hyper Rare)
MAMega Attack Rare

Other codes exist (PR, S, SSR, TR, CHR, CSR, K, A…) for older or special rarities: the full list is on the “Rarity” page of Pokepedia. Be careful: the matches between Japanese and Western rarities are not always exact from one set to the next, as a comparison of several guides shows. When in doubt, check the official list for the set in question.

What about the yellow star? The Pokémon TCG Pocket case

Searches for a “yellow star” often come from the mobile game. In Pokémon TCG Pocket, Pokepedia says one yellow star marks a Full Art Pokémon that is not a Pokémon ex, as well as Item cards; two yellow stars mark Full Art Pokémon ex and Supporter cards; and three yellow stars mark immersive cards. These symbols are not the same as those of the physical card game. See also our guide to Pokémon TCG Pocket for beginners.

What rarity does not tell you

  • The price. It depends on demand, condition, language and set. To measure it, follow how to estimate the value of a Pokémon card.
  • Authenticity. A fake can copy a symbol: see how to tell if a Pokémon card is fake.
  • The type of finish. Holo, reverse holo, full art: these are different things, explained in holo, reverse holo, full art and alternate art.

To find the symbol on the card and the other printed details, read how to read a Pokémon card. And to see how likely you are to hit a given rarity when opening boosters, try the booster pull chance calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What does AR mean on a Japanese Pokémon card?

AR stands for Art Rare. It is the Japanese code for a card with expanded artwork, introduced with the S12a set, according to Pokepedia. Its “Special Art Rare” version is written SAR.

Why do some Pokémon cards have no rarity symbol?

Promo cards carry the Promo symbol, which does not indicate a rarity. Some older or special cards also have unusual numbering.

What does a gold star mean on a Pokémon card?

Since Scarlet & Violet, one gold star means Illustration Rare, two mean Special Illustration Rare and three mean Hyper Rare. Before that series, a gold star marked a LEGEND card.

Is a rare card always worth a lot?

No. Rarity measures how often a card appears in boosters, not demand or price.
