Selling Your Pokémon Cards on Cardmarket

In short Cardmarket is the most used European marketplace for single cards. Here is how to sell there cleanly, with the published fees and the rules that prevent disputes.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Value, buying and selling · by the MaxiCards team

How to sell a card on Cardmarket in 7 steps

  1. Create your account. Sign up, fill in your address and choose a private account for occasional selling.
  2. Find the right page. Search for the card and open the page of the exact version: set, number, language.
  3. Grade the condition cautiously. Choose the condition without rounding up: when in doubt, take the lower one.
  4. Set the price. Compare with offers in the same language and condition, then subtract commission, packaging and postage to check your margin.
  5. List it. Add the card to your stock, with a comment only for useful details.
  6. Prepare the shipment. Check the order, protect the cards (sleeve and rigid holder) and choose tracked shipping, registered above EUR 25.
  7. Mark it as sent. Enter the shipment and tracking number on Cardmarket, then answer the buyer's questions quickly.

Selling on Cardmarket is free to sign up for and has no listing fee: the site takes a commission only when a card sells. But the platform is strict about card condition and the quality of shipping, because buyers are collectors and players who compare quickly. Read the steps above together with what you need to know before you start. To compare with other platforms, see where to sell your Pokémon cards.

The fees, with their date

Cardmarket’s official fee table states, as read in November 2026:

  • a commission of 5% of the item value, capped at €100 per item, the same for private and professional sellers;
  • fees rounded up to the next cent;
  • 3% currency conversion fee if the currencies differ;
  • packaging fees added to the order depending on the shipping type (€0.30, €0.50 or €1.00 according to the table).

The “Trustee Service”, which holds the payment until receipt of tracked shipments is confirmed, is charged to the buyer (0.5% to 1% of the item value depending on the shipping method). Cardmarket can change these figures: reread the table before setting your prices. The fee calculator applies the 5% rate and the cap to give you your net.

Card condition: the golden rule

The official seller guide is clear: when in doubt, use the lower condition, and only call a card “Near Mint” if it looks new on both front and back. A gap between the listed condition and the card received leads to disputes; the guide mentions a practical rule of 10% of the card’s value for a one-level difference. Photograph any flaws and use the comment only for extras (stamp, light mark), since the name, set, language, foil or signature cannot be specified in the comment. To learn how to grade, read Mint, Near Mint and other conditions.

Setting the price without mistakes

On each card’s page, Cardmarket shows offers, a trend price and recent price information. Do not simply match the lowest one: check that it matches your language and condition. Take the commission, packaging and postage into account before going below a certain price: on a card worth a few euros, postage can exceed your margin. For a rare card, look at the gap between conditions: a Near Mint copy can be worth much more than an Excellent one. The valuation method explains how to compare real sales.

Private or professional?

A private account is enough to sell a few cards from your collection. A professional account is for people who sell regularly as a business, with extra formalities (identification, a registered business). Cardmarket details the conditions in its help pages, and the tax rules depend on your country.

Shipping: the rules that matter

  • Check each order before shipping: if an item is missing or something does not match, do not send it and contact the buyer. Shipping mistakes are at your expense, according to the seller guide.
  • Add a return address inside or outside the package, and copy the buyer’s full address carefully.
  • Registered shipping is compulsory for any order over €25, according to Cardmarket’s fee table.
  • Answer messages within 24 hours as a rule; more than two working days is not acceptable, according to the seller guide.

Postage rates depend on your national postal service, so check your carrier’s current tariffs. For the packing, use a card in a sleeve plus a toploader or a rigid sleeve, and a stiff envelope.

The most common mistakes

  • Listing a card on the wrong page (wrong set or wrong language): the seller guide insists on this.
  • Overrating the condition out of optimism.
  • Forgetting postage in the calculation and selling at a loss.
  • Leaving a listing up when the card has sold elsewhere, which forces you to cancel an order.
  • Accepting payment outside the platform: you lose all protection.

With a little rigor, each listing takes a minute and returns stay rare. For more on what to watch out for, see how to avoid scams.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to sell on Cardmarket?

Cardmarket states a commission of 5% of the item value, capped at EUR 100 per item. There is no listing fee. Check the fee table for current figures.

When do I get paid for a sale?

For tracked shipments, Cardmarket holds the payment until the buyer confirms receipt. You can then withdraw your balance to your payment method.

Can I sell Japanese cards on Cardmarket?

Yes, as long as you pick the right page and language. Japanese cards have their own versions on the site.

What if the buyer disputes the condition?

Reply quickly and offer a friendly solution (a partial refund or a return). The seller guide mentions a practical rule of 10% of the value for a one-level condition gap.
