Nuzlocke Rules: The Pokémon Challenge Explained

In short A Nuzlocke is a challenge players set for themselves to make a Pokémon game much harder. Two core rules are enough; everything else is optional.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Video games · by the MaxiCards team

A Nuzlocke is not a game mode: it is a set of rules the player chooses to follow, on any main series game. It started with a comic about Pokémon Ruby by Nick Franco. The name comes from Nuzleaf, the evolution of the first Pokémon he caught in it, combined with "Locke", the name of a character from the TV series Lost whose face was given to that Pokémon in the comic, according to Pokepedia.

The two core rules

For a "true" Nuzlocke, both of these rules must be followed:

  1. Permadeath. A Pokémon that faints is considered dead and can no longer be used. You release it or store it in the PC for good.
  2. One catch per area. You can only catch the first wild Pokémon you meet in each area. If it faints or runs away, you get no second chance in that area.

A very common tip, though not an official rule: give every Pokémon a nickname so you get attached to it and feel the losses more. Our Pokémon nickname generator gives you ideas (reminder: 12 characters at most since generation 6).

What counts as an "area"?

The community often relies on the Pokémon's summary page: two places count as different when the "Met at" text changes. The "Pinwheel clause" lets you split a very large area into sub-areas that differ in look and Pokémon, such as Pinwheel Forest or Route 1 in Alola. In some games, the first routes are crossed before you have Poké Balls: they can be exempt. Facing a double battle or a horde, the player chooses which one to catch.

Optional rules

Each player can adjust the difficulty. Here are the most common ones:

  • Dupes Clause: if the first Pokémon you meet belongs to a species or evolution line you have already caught, you can reroll the encounter. The number of rerolls can be limited.
  • Shiny clause: allow catching a shiny even in an area you have already used, with or without the right to use it. For a classic shiny hunt, see shiny hunting methods and the shiny hunt counter.
  • Items: ban healing items outside Pokémon Centers, or on the contrary allow specific items; ban the Master Ball; ban held items; limit how many Poké Balls you can buy.
  • Gift or exception catches: allow a Pokémon to get past a mandatory obstacle, such as a field move, then release or store it.
  • Softlock: treat a situation with no way out as a game over.
  • Legendaries: ban them.
  • Other limits: no Exp. Share, no evolving, no Repels, a single species, or a limited number of Revives.

The best-known variants

  • Hardcore: no items in battle except Poké Balls and held items, a level cap before each Gym Leader, and starting over after a total failure.
  • Soul Link: two players progress together; linked Pokémon from the same area share the same fate (if one dies, so does the other).
  • Wonderlocke: every catch is immediately sent to Wonder Trade and you only use the Pokémon you receive.
  • Giftlocke: you only use Pokémon given by characters.
  • Monotype, Duotype, Colorlocke: one or two types, or one color, are imposed.
  • Sololocke: a single Pokémon for the entire adventure; if it falls, it is over.
  • Wedlocke: Pokémon are formed into pairs, often by gender, that replace each other in battle.
  • Generationlocke: survivors carry over from one game to the next.
  • Shinylocke: you only keep the first shiny of each area; Pokepedia recommends it on games that make encounters easier, such as HeartGold and SoulSilver.

Getting ready before you start

  1. Choose your game and your rules before you start: a rule added midway feels like cheating.
  2. Write down your catches and deaths in a notebook or spreadsheet, so you do not mix up areas.
  3. Know the types. A Pokémon lost to a forgotten weakness is the number one cause of defeat. Our weakness calculator helps you choose who to send out in battle.
  4. Balance your team. If luck gives you six Pokémon of the same type, a team that similar is fragile. You can also challenge yourself with a randomly drawn team using the random Pokémon team generator, which has a mode suited to Nuzlocke.
  5. Keep replacement Pokémon. A team of six that dies quickly ends the run; a reserve often saves the challenge.

Golden rule. A Nuzlocke is a game: if a rule stops being fun, change it before the run, not during.

Can you do a Nuzlocke on any game?

Yes, on any game where you catch Pokémon: the rules adapt. On open-world games, split the areas in advance, because their limits are less clear than in older games. If you like challenges, you can also try a shiny hunt alongside, but be careful not to mix up the counters.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Nuzlocke in Pokémon?

It is a challenge added on top of the game: a Pokémon that faints is considered dead, and you can only catch the first wild Pokémon you meet in each area.

Who invented the Nuzlocke?

The challenge was created by Nick Franco, in a comic based on his game of Pokémon Ruby.

Are nicknames required in a Nuzlocke?

No. Nicknaming your Pokémon is a very common habit to get more attached to them, but it is not a core rule.

Can you use healing items in a Nuzlocke?

It depends on the rules you pick: the basic version allows them, while the Hardcore variant bans items in battle other than Poké Balls and held items.
