Booster Box, ETB, Collection Box: What Each Pokémon Product Contains

In short Booster box, Elite Trainer Box, booster bundle, Build & Battle Box: the names sound alike, the contents do not. Here is what each product holds according to the official pages, and how to compare their prices.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Value, buying and selling · by the MaxiCards team

On the shelves, every product looks alike: big colorful boxes, a star Pokémon, sometimes a promise of an “exclusive card”. Yet a booster box, an Elite Trainer Box and a booster bundle do not hold the same things, and the price per pack varies. The figures below come from the official Pokémon.com pages for the Mega Evolution series, read in November 2026; the exact contents change from set to set, and the product page is what counts.

The booster pack: the basic unit

According to the official page for a Mega Evolution display, each booster pack holds 10 cards and one basic Energy card. Depending on the set, it may also hold a code card for Pokémon TCG Live: the official Phantasmal Flames page says it does not for that set. So contents vary slightly from series to series. The odds of pulling a given rarity are covered in the booster pull-chance calculator.

The booster box (display): 36 packs

The booster box (also called a display) holds 36 booster packs of a single set, which is 360 set cards and 36 basic Energy cards if you apply the 10 cards and one Energy per pack from the official page. It is the cheapest way to buy many packs, but also the one that needs the biggest budget at once. Sealed products are also the most exposed to fraud: see how to avoid scams.

The Elite Trainer Box (ETB)

The ETB is the best-known box for players and collectors. For Mega Evolution – Phantasmal Flames, the official page lists:

  • 9 booster packs of the set;
  • 1 full-art foil promo card (a Charcadet for this set);
  • 65 card sleeves;
  • 40 Energy cards;
  • 1 player’s guide to the set;
  • 6 damage-counter dice, 1 competition-legal coin-flip die and 1 plastic coin;
  • 1 storage box with 6 dividers;
  • 1 code card for Pokémon TCG Live.

The basic contents are the same from set to set, with a different promo and artwork. The ETB suits people who want both packs and accessories (sleeves, storage box); if you only want cards, subtract the value of those accessories from the price to compare fairly.

The booster bundle

The official page for a Mega Evolution booster bundle lists 6 booster packs. It is cheaper than buying packs one by one and a smaller commitment than a booster box. The pages for other sets may show a different number: check the packaging.

The Build & Battle Box

This product holds a ready-to-play 40-card deck with one foil promo card (out of four), 4 booster packs and a code card for Pokémon TCG Live. According to Pokémon.com, it is sold ahead of the official release at pre-release events and then in stores. It is a good product for learning to play; see also how to play the Pokémon TCG.

Collection boxes and anniversary products

For Pokémon’s 30th anniversary, the official list of November 2026 products includes, for example, a Ditto Premium Collection (acrylic stand, promo card and 8 30th-anniversary boosters), a figure collection (promo, oversized card and 5 boosters) and an Ultra-Premium Collection (playmat, sleeves, deck box and 30 boosters). These boxes cost more than the same packs bought on their own: part of the price pays for the accessories and exclusive cards.

Three-packs, blisters, mini tins: read the packaging

Small formats (three-pack blisters, mini tins) change from set to set: number of packs, promo card, stickers. Since no official page was checked for each of them, this guide gives no figures: read the text on the back of the packaging.

Comparing fairly: the price per pack

To compare a booster box, an ETB and a bundle, divide the product price by the number of packs, then subtract the value of the accessories you can use. With an ETB, for example, you also pay for 65 sleeves, 40 Energy cards, dice and a box: the price per pack is therefore higher than for a booster box. The price-per-booster calculator does the division for you and shows the gap between two products. Reminder: buying many packs guarantees no rare card, and cards are not a safe investment.

Where to buy

Prefer retailers and specialist shops that buy from official distributors. A price far below the market should make you suspicious.

Frequently asked questions

How many booster packs are in a Pokémon booster box?

A booster box holds 36 packs of a single set. For Mega Evolution sets, each pack holds 10 cards and one basic Energy.

How many booster packs are in an Elite Trainer Box?

A recent ETB holds 9 booster packs, a promo card, 65 sleeves, 40 Energy cards, dice, a coin and a storage box.

What is an ETB in Pokémon?

ETB stands for Elite Trainer Box: a box with booster packs and play accessories.

Is a booster box better value than an ETB?

It usually offers a lower price per pack, since you are not paying for accessories. Compare with the price-per-booster calculator before buying.
