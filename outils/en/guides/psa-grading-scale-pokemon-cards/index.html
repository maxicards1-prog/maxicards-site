PSA and Pokémon cards: what a "graded" card really means

In short Seen a Pokémon card in a plastic case with a "PSA 9" or "PSA 10" label? Here is what those grades mean, what PSA actually checks and what a grade does not tell you.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Grading · by the MaxiCards team

PSA, a card authentication and grading company

PSA is a US company that receives collectible cards, checks that they are authentic, judges their condition and seals them in a tamper-evident plastic case, often called a slab. The label shows the card, a grade and a certification number. Pokémon is one of the biggest categories PSA handles, next to sports cards.

The idea is simple: an independent third party gives an opinion on the card's condition. As a buyer, you no longer have to rely only on the seller's photos. PSA describes its case as sonically sealed and tamper-evident, so opening it should leave visible signs.

The PSA grading scale

PSA grades from 1 to 10, with 10 the best. Here are the official names, taken from PSA's grading standards page:

10Gem Mint
9Mint
8Near Mint-Mint
7Near Mint
6Excellent-Mint
5Excellent
4Very Good-Excellent
3Very Good
2Good
1.5Fair
1Poor

PSA also uses half-point grades for some levels. These names do not line up with the conditions used on marketplace listings: a PSA "Near Mint" is not the same thing as a seller's "Near Mint". To compare them, read our guide on Mint, Near Mint and Excellent conditions.

What does it take to get a PSA 10?

According to PSA, a Gem Mint 10 card is virtually perfect: four sharp corners, a sharp image, original gloss and no stains. A slight printing imperfection can be allowed if it does not distract from the overall look. Centering must stay within roughly 55/45 on the front (for example 55% of the border on one side and 45% on the other) and 75/25 on the back. PSA notes that the grader keeps some room for judgment.

A card pulled straight from a pack is therefore not automatically a 10. Slightly white edges, off-center printing or a tiny surface scratch can all lower the grade.

Special labels: Authentic, Altered, marks

  • Authentic (N0): the card is judged genuine but receives no numerical grade.
  • Altered Authentic (AA): genuine, but altered.
  • MK: the card has marks such as ink, pencil or other writing.

PSA will not grade a card that shows signs of trimming, restoration, recoloring or doubtful authenticity. Those cards come back without a grade, and the grading fee is still due. It is better to check a card before sending it: see how to spot a fake Pokémon card.

How much does PSA grading cost?

PSA sets its prices by service level: the higher the maximum insured value of the card and the faster the turnaround, the higher the fee. Prices and turnaround times differ by region and change often, and PSA says its estimated times are not guaranteed. On PSA's European store (prices in euros), checked on November 23, 2026, the range ran from €34.99 per card at the entry level to €599 per card at the top level. Always check PSA's current price list for your region before you decide.

Where to send your cards also depends on your region, so follow PSA's shipping guide. Our guide on how to get a Pokémon card graded covers the steps.

What a grade does not guarantee

  • It is not 100% objective. PSA itself talks about "eye appeal" and acknowledges some subjectivity. Two companies can grade the same card differently.
  • It does not set the price. A card's value depends first on the card, its set and current demand.
  • It does not protect against a price drop. Cards are not a safe investment, and grading costs money before the card is sold.

Before you send anything, work out what you would keep after fees with the resale fee calculator and its grading fee field. To compare PSA with other companies, read our grading company comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Is a PSA 10 the same as a "mint" card?

No. "Mint" in a listing is the seller's own opinion of the condition. PSA 10 is a grade given by PSA under its own criteria, with centering and corners checked. The two scales do not match exactly.

Can you crack a PSA slab open or get a card regraded?

PSA offers services such as reholdering, review and crossover from another grader. Opening the case yourself breaks the tamper-evident seal and removes the point of the certification.

How do you check that a PSA slab is real?

Take the certification number from the label and enter it in PSA's online cert verification tool. Compare the card and grade shown there with the slab in your hands.

Is a graded card always authentic?

The company judged it authentic, but fake slabs exist. Always verify the cert number online and buy from sellers you trust.
