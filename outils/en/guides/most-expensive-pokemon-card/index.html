The Most Expensive Pokémon Card in the World

In short In February 2026, a Pikachu Illustrator sold for more than $16 million, a record confirmed by Guinness. Here are the highest sales, with their dates, and what they teach us.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Value, buying and selling · by the MaxiCards team

Records make headlines, but they describe a world far from ordinary binders. The sales below involve extremely rare cards, often unique in their condition, sold at specialist auction houses. They help you understand why a card can be worth a fortune, not to value your own. Figures read in November 2026; a new record can be set at any time.

The record: the PSA 10 Pikachu Illustrator (February 2026)

On February 16, 2026, a copy of Pikachu Illustrator graded PSA 10 (“Gem Mint”, the top grade) sold for $16,492,000, buyer’s premium included, at the auction house Goldin. According to Guinness World Records, it is the most expensive Pokémon card ever sold at auction. The winning bid was $13.3 million, with a 24% buyer’s premium according to Journal du Geek, which puts the total at about 15.3 million euros.

The seller was the YouTuber Logan Paul, who had acquired the card in July 2021 in a trade for a PSA 9 copy plus $4 million, for a total of $5,275,000. So the price roughly tripled in five years, but that is the story of one card, not of a market.

Why is this card worth so much?

  • A tiny print run. The card was created in 1998 as a prize for illustration contests run by the Japanese magazine CoroCoro Comic. It was never sold in stores. Journal du Geek speaks of 39 copies handed out; other outlets give a slightly different figure, a sign that the exact number is not settled.
  • A unique grade. Again according to Journal du Geek, it is the only copy graded PSA 10: of the 39, PSA has graded 24 in total. A maximum grade on a card nearly thirty years old changes everything.
  • An iconic character and a story. Pikachu, the origin as a contest prize and a widely covered sale all pushed the bidding up.

Second place: a Raincoat Pikachu

According to Journal du Geek, a PSA 10 Raincoat Pikachu sold for $8.4 million on September 28, 2026, at Fanatics Collect. This card was created in 2015 for a “Dress-Up Pikachu” contest tied to the game Pokémon Art Academy, and only 100 official copies exist, none sold in stores. The same article notes that another PSA 10 copy sold for $19,200 in 2025: proof that the market can be very volatile, and that a card’s price can change by an order of magnitude in a short time.

Other sales reported in the press

  • A PSA 10 Legendary Collection Pikachu (2002): $780,000, according to Journal du Geek.
  • A Pikachu Gold Star from the Holon Phantoms set: $480,000, according to the same source.
  • A Charizard sold for more than 418,000 euros on eBay in February 2021, reported by Que Choisir; the article does not say which edition or grade.

These are isolated sales: a card being mentioned does not mean every card with the same name is worth as much.

What these records do not tell you

  • Your card is not worth that. Records involve copies graded at the highest level, from extremely limited print runs. A common or worn card of the same character is generally worth far less. To value yours, follow the valuation method.
  • A record is not a trend. A multi-million sale does not mean the market is rising, or will rise. Cards are not a safe investment, and this guide is not investment advice.
  • The grade changes everything. A PSA 10 can be worth many times a PSA 9. See what PSA is.
  • Fakes exist. The more expensive a card, the more it is copied: see how to tell if a card is fake.

Key takeaways

The most expensive card in the world is a PSA 10 Pikachu Illustrator sold for $16.49 million in February 2026, according to Guinness. A card reaches such prices by combining three factors: very few copies, a certified top condition and an iconic character. For your own cards, start with the quick binder sort.

What is the most expensive Pokémon card ever sold?
