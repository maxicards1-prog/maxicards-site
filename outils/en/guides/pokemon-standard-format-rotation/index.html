Pokémon Standard Format: Rotation and Regulation Marks

In short In the Standard format, only cards with certain regulation marks are allowed. Here is what has been legal since the 2026 rotation, how to check a card and what it means for your purchases.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Playing the TCG · by the MaxiCards team

If you are buying cards to play, the first question is: "are they still legal?" The Pokémon TCG uses a letter printed on every recent card for this, the regulation mark. This guide explains how it works and which letters have been allowed in the Standard format since the 2026 rotation. If you are new to the game, start with the basic rules.

What is the Standard format?

A format is a set of cards allowed in a game or tournament. Standard is the format of official Play! Pokémon events and of ranked mode in Pokémon TCG Live: it only includes recent cards, and the allowed cards change regularly at rotation.

The regulation mark: a letter on the card

Each rotation of the Standard format removes the oldest letter that is still allowed. A card's legality depends on the letter printed at the bottom of the card, not on the expansion it comes from. To find out where it is and what the other symbols mean, see how to read a Pokémon card.

What is legal since the 2026 rotation

The 2026 rotation was announced on January 9, 2026. It took effect:

  • on March 26, 2026 in Pokémon TCG Live;
  • on April 10, 2026 for in-person Play! Pokémon events.

Cards with regulation mark G are no longer legal in Standard. Cards with marks H, I and J remain playable, as do future marks. According to Pokepedia, this means all expansions from Temporal Forces onward (Scarlet & Violet series), as well as promos with the same letters, are allowed. The season is therefore called "2026-2027".

Legal in Standard (2026-2027 season): marks H, I, J and later. No longer legal: mark G and older letters. Always check the official Pokémon announcements before a tournament: this guide was checked on November 24, 2026.

Cards with the same name

A card that is no longer legal can sometimes be replaced by a legal version with the same name. For Trainer cards, the name is what counts: a mark G Ultra Ball can be played because a legal-mark Ultra Ball exists, according to Bulbapedia. For Pokémon, it is stricter: a mark G promo Pikachu cannot be used in place of a legal promo Pikachu with identical text. When in doubt, look at the letter printed on the card in your hand, and check the official tournament rules.

When does a new card become legal?

New cards generally become legal about two weeks after release, according to the announcement relayed by PocketMonsters.net. The exact dates are given in each new expansion's announcement: follow our articles on the list of expansions.

What about cards that are no longer legal?

They are not useless: they remain collectible cards, and they work in other formats or in casual play with friends. The Expanded format, for example, accepts much older cards (from the Black & White series in 2011). According to Pokepedia, however, it has not been used in organized events since the pandemic and survives mainly through its community. Some cards are banned there to keep the game balanced. In Standard, Pokepedia says no card is currently banned.

In every format, the four-copy rule applies: you cannot put more than four cards with the same name in a 60-card deck, except basic Energy.

What to do before you buy

  • Look at the letter on each card, not just the expansion name.
  • Keep in mind that rotation happens once a year: a very expensive deck can lose cards the following season.
  • To compare the price of a bundle, Elite Trainer Box or booster box, use the price-per-booster calculator.

Once your list is ready, follow the method in how to build a deck.

An April rotation since 2023

According to Pokepedia, the rotation has coincided with an April expansion release since 2023; before 2022, it took place in August, before the World Championships. No date for the next rotation is given here: wait for the official announcement from Pokémon (the 2026 one was published on January 9, before the March and April effective dates).

Frequently asked questions

Which regulation marks are legal in Standard in 2026?

Since the rotation on March 26, 2026 in TCG Live and April 10, 2026 at events, marks H, I and J are legal, as are future marks. Mark G is no longer allowed.

Where is the regulation mark on a Pokémon card?

It is printed at the bottom of the card. See our guide on how to read a Pokémon card to spot it among the other symbols.

Can a mark G card still be used?

Not in Standard for a Pokémon, unless a legal version with identical text exists for a Trainer card of the same name. It is still playable in other formats or with friends.

Is the Expanded format still played?

It still exists, but according to Pokepedia it has not been used in organized events since the pandemic and survives mostly through its community.
