Pokémon TCG Pocket: A Beginner's Guide

In short Pokémon TCG Pocket is the Pokémon card game for mobile. Here are its rules, how you get cards, how trading works since the 2025 overhaul and what recent expansions change.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Video games · by the MaxiCards team

Pokémon TCG Pocket is a mobile game on iOS and Android. It keeps the idea of the card game, but with shorter rules, fully virtual cards and booster packs you open in the app. Do not confuse it with Pokémon TCG Live, the digital version of the "classic" game: see where to enter a Pokémon card code for that one. The information below was checked on November 24, 2026; the game changes fast, so always check the announcements in the app.

A game released in late 2024

According to Wikipedia, Pokémon TCG Pocket launched worldwide on October 30, 2024, on iOS and Android, with the Genetic Apex expansion and promo cards. New expansions have come out regularly since. The most recent one we checked is Dual Fusion, which launched on October 28 in the US evening (October 29 in other time zones). It adds the first dual-type cards, such as Mega Mewtwo X ex (Psychic and Fighting) and Mega Greninja ex (Water and Darkness), according to PocketMonsters.net and other coverage.

The rules: shorter than the paper TCG

Pocket battles keep the main principles of the TCG (Active Pokémon, Bench, evolution, attacks), but with important differences:

  • a 20-card deck (instead of 60), with at least one Basic Pokémon;
  • at most two copies of the same card;
  • no Energy cards in the deck: an Energy Zone generates Energy each turn, and you choose up to three Energy types for your deck;
  • the player who goes first gets no Energy on their very first turn;
  • a Bench of three spots;
  • a hand of 10 cards at most;
  • a Pokémon cannot evolve on the turn it is played, or during the very first turn of the game;
  • victory is decided by points: you earn them by Knocking Out opposing Pokémon, and the first player to reach the total wins (three points, according to Wikipedia).

If you already know the physical game, the turn logic is the same. If not, read the TCG rules first: the ideas of Active Pokémon, Bench and evolution are explained there.

Getting cards

You get cards by opening virtual booster packs. The game offers free packs at regular intervals, events and mission rewards. Pack Hourglasses speed up opening, and Shinedust is the currency tied to trading (see below). The exact rhythm of free packs, events and gifts changes regularly: the most reliable way is to read the game's home screen. Pocket has no link to physical cards: its cards have no resale value, and its codes do not work in Pokémon TCG Live.

Trading since July 2025

The trading system overhaul arrived on July 29, 2025. According to Pokebip and Journal du Geek (French outlets):

  • Trade Tokens are gone;
  • you now pay in Shinedust to trade 3-diamond, 4-diamond and 1-star rarity cards;
  • a duplicate in your collection gives twice as much Shinedust as before;
  • leftover tokens could be converted in the shop (1 token for 10 Shinedust, or 1 token for 1 Pack Hourglass while stock lasted);
  • a wanted-cards tab lets you list up to 20 cards you want, 3 of which are shown on your profile.

The exact Shinedust amounts required per card are not detailed in these sources, so check the game for current prices. Wikipedia adds that some very rare or promotional cards cannot be traded: check in the game before counting on a trade.

Tips for getting started

  • Play a few battles against the computer first to learn the Energy Zone.
  • Build a deck with one or two Energy types: it is more consistent, just like in the paper game (see how to build a deck).
  • Keep your duplicates: they produce Shinedust, which is useful for trading.
  • Do not spend anything until you know what you enjoy in the game: take time to see what the free packs and events bring you.
  • Be wary of sites that promise free cards or packs in exchange for your login details: no legitimate service asks for them.

Pocket or paper TCG?

Pocket is ideal for playing quickly, with no equipment, and for discovering the game without spending money. The paper TCG offers the fun of collecting, trading with friends and playing in stores or at tournaments. The two complement each other: many players discover an expansion's Pokémon on Pocket before buying cards. To understand the paper game's rarities, see the rarity symbols.

Frequently asked questions

What devices can you play Pokémon TCG Pocket on?

The game launched on iOS and Android on October 30, 2024 worldwide. It offers free packs at regular intervals, plus events and missions.

How many cards are in a Pocket deck?

A deck has 20 cards, with at most two copies of the same card and at least one Basic Pokémon.

How do you trade cards in Pocket since 2025?

Since July 29, 2025, Trade Tokens are gone: you use Shinedust to trade 3-diamond, 4-diamond and 1-star rarity cards.

Can you use your physical cards in Pocket?

No. Pocket is a separate game: its cards are virtual and its codes do not work in Pokémon TCG Live.
