Where to Sell Your Pokémon Cards

In short There is no single best place to sell everything: the right choice depends on the value of your cards, how many you have and how much time you want to spend. Here are the options and their limits.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Value, buying and selling · by the MaxiCards team

A $2 card and a $300 card do not sell in the same place. The more expensive the card, the more protection and visibility matter; the cheaper it is, the more fixed fees and your time matter. Here are the main channels. Fees change often, so always check the official fee page of a platform before you sell. The only fee figures quoted below are Cardmarket’s, read on the official table in November 2026.

Cardmarket: for single cards

This is the main European marketplace for trading cards. Each copy is listed on the page of its exact version (set, language) with a standardized condition. According to Cardmarket’s fee table, the commission is 5% of the item value, capped at €100 per item, the same for private and professional sellers. For tracked shipments the site holds the payment until the buyer confirms receipt. Advantage: an audience of collectors who know what they want. Limit: you need to be rigorous about condition and handle the shipping. The step-by-step is in how to sell cards on Cardmarket.

eBay: for rare and graded cards

eBay is a good fit for rare, graded or older cards, because auctions can push a much-wanted card higher. Fees and buyer-protection charges differ from country to country and change over time, so read eBay’s current seller fee page for your country before listing. Advantage: huge audience. Limit: a lot of competition and scams on both the buying and selling side; see how to avoid scams.

Vinted and similar apps: for small lots and affordable cards

General second-hand apps have a very large audience, which is handy for lots of common cards. Fee rules differ by country (sellers often pay little or nothing, while buyers pay a protection fee), so check the current rules where you live. Limit: buyers there rarely look for collectible cards in a precise condition, and prices tend to be pushed down. Do not list a high-value card there without photographing its condition from every angle.

Card shops and buyback

Some specialist shops buy cards or whole collections. It is quick and needs no shipping, but the shop has to resell with a margin, so the offer will be below the market price. For common cards or an old binder, that can be fine. For an expensive card, ask for several offers and compare them with the net you would get on Cardmarket using the fee calculator. Beware of buyers who pay “by weight”: a good card can get lost in the lot.

Card shows, trade nights and local groups

Collector shows and trade events let you sell face to face, with no platform fees and no shipping. Bring protected cards, prices you have already researched and a way to check values on the spot. Social media selling groups call for caution: payment outside a platform and the absence of protection are risk factors. An in-person sale is better than a bank transfer to a stranger.

How to choose in practice

  • Cards worth only a few dollars: sell as a lot (a second-hand app, a shop, a trade night) rather than one by one.
  • Mid-value cards: Cardmarket, with a cautious condition grade.
  • Very expensive or graded cards: eBay or Cardmarket, with front and back photos and tracked, insured shipping.
  • In every case, value your cards first with the valuation method, and check the tax rules in your own country if you sell often.

Fees change: the figures above are dated, and each site’s official page is what counts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best site to sell Pokémon cards?

There is no single one: Cardmarket suits single cards, eBay suits rare or graded cards, and second-hand apps suit affordable lots. Compare the fees and the audience of each.

Can I sell Pokémon cards on local classified sites?

Yes, especially for in-person sales. Their conditions and fees change, so read their help pages before posting.

Do card shops pay market price for cards?

Usually not: they have to resell with a margin. Ask for an offer and compare it with the net you would get by selling yourself.

Do I have to report income from selling cards?

That depends on your country and on whether your sales are occasional or regular. Check the rules of your local tax authority.
