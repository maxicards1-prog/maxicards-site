How to Read a Pokémon Card: Every Printed Detail

In short Name, HP, weakness, number, symbols, letter: a Pokémon card packs a lot of information into a few centimeters. Here is where to find each detail and what it means.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Reading a card · by the MaxiCards team

The top and middle of the card

Pokepedia's description puts the Pokémon's name in the top left corner. It gives the species, sometimes with a prefix or suffix that says what type of card it is. The top right corner shows hit points (HP), always a multiple of ten, followed by the Pokémon's type. The artwork takes up roughly the top half of the card. Below it come the attacks and, depending on the card, abilities, then the rules text.

The bottom strip: weakness, resistance, retreat

Weakness is in the bottom left corner; resistance is in the middle, with a value that can reach 30; the retreat cost area is always present. If the Pokémon has no weakness or resistance, the area is still there and stays empty. This information is mostly used for playing. To look it up for the video games, use the weakness and resistance calculator instead, which covers the games and not the card game.

The card number: 023/198, and numbers higher than the total

The number is printed at the bottom of the card, bottom right in the layout Pokepedia describes. It has two parts: the card's position in the set, then the number of cards in the set, secret cards not counted. A card numbered above the total (for example 83/82 on an older card) is therefore a secret rare, not a mistake. Some cards use special numbering: promos use formats like 023/P or TPCi01, and some old cards carry a word in place of the number.

If you are looking up a card's price, the combination of name, number and set is the key: the same Pokémon often exists in several versions, and each has its own number. That detail keeps you from comparing different cards.

The rarity symbol

The rarity sits to the right of the number. A circle means a Common card, a diamond an Uncommon, a star a Rare, and variants (colored stars, double stars) mark higher rarities. The full list, with the symbols used since Scarlet & Violet and the Japanese codes, is in Pokémon card rarity symbols. A promo card has no rarity symbol: it carries a Promo symbol.

The set symbol

Each set has its own small symbol, printed near the number. It lets you trace which block a card comes from, even when the set name is not written out: compare it with the set's page on a fan wiki or with the official list. For recent releases, our page on the list of Pokémon TCG sets helps you find them.

The regulation mark letter

The regulation mark is a small symbol with a letter, printed at the bottom of the card. It shows whether the card is allowed in tournaments. Pokepedia lists the letters A to C for Japanese sets and A to J for international sets. The series involved are Sword & Shield, Scarlet & Violet, then Mega Evolution.

In the Standard format, the letter is what counts, not the set name. According to announcements reported by the specialist press, the spring 2026 rotation removed cards with the G mark, while the H, I and J marks stay legal, along with later ones: on March 26, 2026 on Pokémon TCG Live and on April 10, 2026 for in-person events. This rule changes every season: check it in our guide to the Standard format and rotation before building a deck. A card that is out of Standard is not worthless: it stays playable in other formats and collectible.

The illustrator

Pokepedia notes that the artwork is always signed: the illustrator's name is printed somewhere at the bottom of the card. That is handy for collectors who follow a particular artist, and for telling apart several versions of the same Pokémon drawn by different hands.

Older cards: watch for variations

Before the HeartGold & SoulSilver series, the spelling of Pokémon names (accents, capital letters) was not strictly controlled, according to Pokepedia: a name that differs slightly from one card to another is not a sign of a fake. For other clues, compare with a card you trust and read how to tell if a Pokémon card is fake. For the special stamps on the first sets, see Pokémon 1st Edition cards.

Left or right: how do you find your way?

Many searches ask about “the symbol at the bottom left” or “bottom right”. The honest answer is that the layout varies from one series to another. Rather than counting on the side, look for the shapes. A circle, diamond or star is the rarity; a small emblem specific to the set is the block; a symbol with a letter is the regulation mark; a fraction is the number. If you are unsure, take a card from the same set whose identity you know and compare.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the number on a Pokémon card?

At the bottom of the card, written as “number / set total”. Pokepedia places it at the bottom right in the layout it describes, with the rarity symbol next to it.

What does the letter on a Pokémon card mean?

It is the regulation mark. It shows which rotation block the card belongs to and whether it is allowed in the Standard format. Letters A to J exist for international sets.

Why is my card's number higher than the total (for example 83/82)?

It is a secret rare, numbered beyond the set's official total. That is normal and not a sign of a fake.

Where can I see the illustrator's name?

Pokémon card artwork is always signed, but the exact spot varies by series. Look for a line of text at the bottom of the card.
