How to Build a Pokémon Deck That Actually Works

In short A good deck is not a pile of pretty cards: it is an engine that gets its attackers out on time. Here are the mandatory rules, a starting split and the most common mistakes.

Updated November 24, 2026 · Playing the TCG · by the MaxiCards team

Build your Pokémon deck in 6 steps

  1. Choose your main attacker. Pick a Pokémon that can Knock Out opposing Pokémon in one or two hits; the rest of the deck is built around it.
  2. Build your evolution lines. Play more copies of the lower cards in the line (for example 4-3-3 for a Stage 2) so the evolution comes out on time.
  3. Add a backup attacker. Include a second Pokémon, with a few support cards, so you can keep playing if the first one is Knocked Out.
  4. Set up the draw and search engine. Add draw Supporters, search Items and a few cards to switch your Active Pokémon easily.
  5. Count your Energy. Aim for 8 to 12 Energy cards, with as few different types as possible.
  6. Test and adjust. Play about ten games, then change only one or two cards at a time.

Building a deck is the part of the TCG that many players enjoy most. Before you pick your favorite cards, you need to know the rules that apply to everyone, then a simple method to make the deck consistent. If you have not played yet, start with the basic TCG rules.

The rules you must follow

  • A deck has exactly 60 cards, no more and no less.
  • It needs at least one Basic Pokémon; in practice, many more.
  • You can play up to 4 copies of cards with the same name, whatever their version or rarity. Basic Energy cards are the only exception.
  • A deck can contain only one ACE SPEC card.
  • All cards must be legal in the format you play: see the Standard format and rotation before you buy.

The name, not the artwork, counts toward the limit of four: four different cards with the same name count as four copies.

A starting split

There is no single recipe, but Pokemon-France's learning guide suggests a classic skeleton: about 15 Pokémon, 35 Trainer cards and 10 Energy cards. There are many Trainers because they draw cards and search for the cards you need. Treat these numbers as a base to adjust, not as a law.

Evolution lines

An evolution line is written as three numbers: for example "4-3-3" means 4 Basic Pokémon, 3 Stage 1 and 3 Stage 2. The lower a card is in the line, the more copies you need, because it has to come out early. Some common reference points for a Stage 2 Pokémon: 4-3-3, or 4-2-3 if you have cards that speed up evolution. For a Stage 1 Pokémon, 4-3 or 3-3 is common. Also try to have at least ten or so Basic Pokémon: fewer, and the risk of starting with no Pokémon to play goes up.

The engine: drawing and searching

A consistent deck is a deck that finds its cards. Plan on plenty of draw Supporters (about ten), search Items, a few switching cards to change your Active Pokémon without paying the Retreat Cost, and one or two effects to pull a specific Pokémon out of your opponent's Bench. Reminder: you can play only one Supporter per turn, so there is no point stacking them without thinking.

Energy: fewer than you think

Between 8 and 12 Energy cards is enough for most decks. Above 14, you risk drawing Energy instead of useful cards. Stick to one or two Energy types: the more there are, the harder it is to have the right one at the right time. Attacks with a Colorless cost can be paid with any Energy, which helps multi-type decks.

The most common mistakes

  • Playing lots of different Pokémon with one copy each: you never find them.
  • Not enough draw and search cards.
  • Too much Energy (more than 14) or too many different types.
  • Fewer than 10 Basic Pokémon, which means more mulligans.
  • Copying a list you saw online without understanding each card's role: you will not know what to change when it does not work.

Buying smart

Do not rush toward the most expensive cards: a well-supported attacker beats a pretty rare Pokémon with no help. To get an idea of what an expansion's boosters cost, use the price-per-booster calculator. To see how many boosters you would open on average to find a specific card, try the booster odds calculator. Cards can also be bought as singles: always check that they are legal before you buy.

Tip. After each game, change only one or two cards at a time. If you change everything, you will not know what improved (or hurt) the deck.

Frequently asked questions

How many cards are in a Pokémon deck?

A Pokémon TCG deck has exactly 60 cards. You need at least one Basic Pokémon, and you can have at most 4 cards with the same name, except basic Energy.

How many Energy cards should a Pokémon deck have?

About ten is often enough, between 8 and 12 depending on how fast the deck is. Above 14, you risk drawing too much Energy and not enough useful cards.

Can you play more than one ACE SPEC card in a deck?

No, a deck can contain only one ACE SPEC card.

Does a beginner deck have to be expensive?

No. The rarest cards are not necessarily the best for playing: a consistent deck matters more than rare cards.
