Why timesheets use decimal hours

Payroll and billing software multiplies hours by a pay rate, which is easy with 7.75 and awkward with 7 h 45 min. A decimal hour is simply a fraction of 60 minutes, so 0.5 is 30 minutes and 0.25 is 15 minutes. It is not the same as the minutes after the colon: 7:45 is not 7.45.

The two formulas

  • Time to decimal: hours + minutes ÷ 60. For 7 h 45 min: 7 + 45 ÷ 60 = 7.75.
  • Decimal to time: multiply the decimal part by 60. For 7.8 hours: 0.8 × 60 = 48, so 7 h 48 min.

Another example

8 hours 20 minutes is 8 + 20 ÷ 60 = 8.3333, usually rounded to 8.33 hours. Rounding to two decimals can shift a total by a minute or so over a long period, so check whether your employer rounds each day or only the weekly total.

A common mistake

Typing 7.45 for 7 hours 45 minutes understates the time by 18 minutes, because 7.45 hours is 7 h 27 min. Over a 40-hour week, small slips like that add up to real money, so convert before you enter hours into a payroll form.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert 15 minutes to a decimal?

Divide by 60. 15 ÷ 60 = 0.25 hours.

How do I convert 7.5 hours to hours and minutes?

Multiply the decimal part by 60: 0.5 × 60 = 30. So 7.5 hours is 7 hours 30 minutes.

Is 7:45 the same as 7.45 hours?

No. 7:45 means 7 hours and 45 minutes, which is 7.75 in decimal hours. 7.45 hours is 7 hours 27 minutes.

How many minutes is 0.1 hours?

0.1 × 60 = 6 minutes.
