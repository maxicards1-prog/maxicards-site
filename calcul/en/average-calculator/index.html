Simple average (mean)

Add up the values and divide by how many there are. For 85, 92 and 78: (85 + 92 + 78) ÷ 3 = 255 ÷ 3 = 85.

Weighted average

When some values count more than others, multiply each value by its weight, add the results and divide by the total weight: weighted average = Σ(value × weight) ÷ Σ(weights).

Example: a course grade

Suppose homework is worth 30%, a midterm 20% and the final exam 50%. With scores of 85, 92 and 78:

(85 × 30 + 92 × 20 + 78 × 50) ÷ (30 + 20 + 50) = (2,550 + 1,840 + 3,900) ÷ 100 = 82.9.

The simple average of the same scores is 85, higher than 82.9 because the lowest score has the biggest weight.

Where it is used

Weighted averages are behind many everyday numbers: a course grade where exams count more than homework, a portfolio return where bigger holdings count more, or the average price you paid when you bought the same item at different prices. In each case the weight says how much a value should count. If every weight is 1, the weighted average equals the simple average.

Good to know

  • Weights do not have to add up to 100. The calculator divides by their total, so 3, 2 and 5 work the same as 30, 20 and 50.
  • Rows with an empty value are ignored.
  • The mean is pulled by extreme values. For skewed data, the median calculator gives the middle value instead.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate an average?

Add all the values and divide by the number of values. The average of 4, 8 and 9 is 21 ÷ 3 = 7.

What is a weighted average?

An average in which each value counts according to its weight, such as a course grade where the final exam is worth more than homework.

Do the weights have to add up to 100?

No. The weighted average divides by the total of the weights, whatever that total is.

What is the difference between mean, median and mode?

The mean is the sum divided by the count, the median is the middle value in sorted order and the mode is the most frequent value.
