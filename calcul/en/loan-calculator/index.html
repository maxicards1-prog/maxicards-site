The loan payment formula

For a fixed-rate loan repaid in equal monthly installments, the payment is M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the amount borrowed, r is the annual rate divided by 12 and n is the number of monthly payments.

Worked example

Borrow 250,000 at 6.5% for 30 years. Here r = 0.065 ÷ 12 and n = 360, so the payment is 1,580.17 a month. Over 360 payments you repay 568,861.22, of which 318,861.22 is interest.

In the first month, interest is 250,000 × 0.065 ÷ 12 = 1,354.17, so only 226.00 of the first payment reduces the balance. Each month the interest share shrinks and the principal share grows.

Why the term matters

The same 250,000 at 6.5% over 15 years costs 2,177.77 a month, but total interest drops to 141,998.31. A shorter term means a higher payment and a much lower total cost.

Reading the amortization table

The table below the results shows, for each year, how much of your payments went to interest and how much to principal, and the balance left at the end of the year. Early years are mostly interest. Later years are mostly principal, which is why paying a loan off early saves the most when you do it in the first years.

What this calculator leaves out

  • Origination fees, closing costs and other charges, which are included in a loan's APR but not in the interest rate used here.
  • Property tax, homeowners insurance or mortgage insurance, which are added to many mortgage payments.
  • Variable rates and extra payments: the schedule assumes a fixed rate and the same payment every month.

A longer term also keeps your payment lower, which can help your budget, but it keeps you in debt longer.

The result is an estimate. Your lender's disclosure is the final word on your actual payments.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly loan payment calculated?

With M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n the number of payments.

How much interest will I pay on a 250,000 loan at 6.5% over 30 years?

About 318,861, on top of the 250,000 you borrowed, for total payments of roughly 568,861.

Does a shorter loan term save money?

Yes. The payment is higher, but you pay interest for fewer months, so the total interest is much lower.

Can I use this for a car loan or personal loan?

Yes, for any fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments: mortgage, auto, student or personal loan.
