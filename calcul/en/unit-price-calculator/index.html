How unit price works

Unit price is the price divided by the quantity: price ÷ size. It puts products of different sizes on the same footing, so you can see which one costs less for the same amount. For a set of packs, multiply the size by the number of packs first.

Worked example

Product 1 is 12 oz for 3.49. Since 1 lb = 16 oz, that is 3.49 ÷ 12 × 16 = 4.65 per lb. Product 2 is 1.5 lb for 5.99, which is 5.99 ÷ 1.5 = 3.99 per lb. Product 2 is about 14.2% cheaper per pound, even though it costs more on the shelf.

Comparing different units

The calculator converts both products to the unit you choose, so you can compare a 12 oz box with a 1.5 lb bag, or a 750 mL bottle with a 1 liter bottle, without doing the conversion yourself. Both products must measure the same kind of thing: weight with weight, volume with volume or items with items. For a multipack, enter the size of one pack and the number of packs.

Tips for comparing

  • Ounces (oz) measure weight and fluid ounces (fl oz) measure volume. Compare weight with weight and volume with volume.
  • A bigger size is not always cheaper per unit, so check both products.
  • A cheaper unit price only saves money if you will actually use the whole pack before it spoils.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate unit price?

Divide the price by the quantity. A 24 oz jar at 4.80 costs 4.80 ÷ 24 = 0.20 per oz.

How many ounces are in a pound?

There are 16 ounces in a pound.

Is the bigger pack always cheaper per unit?

No. Sales, store brands and odd pack sizes can make a smaller pack cheaper per unit, so compare the unit prices.

What is the difference between oz and fl oz?

An ounce (oz) is a unit of weight. A fluid ounce (fl oz) is a unit of volume. They are not interchangeable, except roughly for water.
