How to work out your mpg

  1. Fill the tank to the click and reset the trip meter.
  2. Drive as usual, then fill up again to the click and note the fuel added.
  3. Miles per gallon = miles driven ÷ gallons added.

The default example, 410 miles on 13.2 US gallons, gives 410 ÷ 13.2 = 31.1 mpg. Measuring this way is usually more honest than the dashboard display.

MPG (US), MPG (UK) and L/100 km

The US gallon is 3.785 liters and the UK (imperial) gallon is 4.546 liters, so the same car has a higher mpg figure in the UK. L/100 km goes the other way: lower is better.

  • L/100 km = 235.215 ÷ mpg (US)
  • L/100 km = 282.481 ÷ mpg (UK)
  • mpg (UK) = mpg (US) × 1.20095

Example: 30 mpg (US) = 235.215 ÷ 30 = 7.84 L/100 km = 36.0 mpg (UK).

What a trip costs

Fuel needed = distance ÷ mpg. Cost = fuel needed × price per gallon. For 300 miles at 30 mpg (US) and a price of 3.50 per US gallon, that is 10 gallons and 35.00. The calculator has no currency symbol, so use any currency.

Real consumption changes with speed, load, tire pressure, air conditioning and short cold-engine trips.

Tips

Compare several fill-ups and average them, since pump cutoffs are never exactly the same. The trip cost covers fuel only: tolls, parking, wear and insurance are extra. If your price is per liter, select liter and the calculator converts it for you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate miles per gallon?

Divide the miles driven by the gallons of fuel used. 300 miles on 10 gallons is 30 mpg.

How do I convert mpg to L/100 km?

Divide 235.215 by the mpg (US) figure. 30 mpg is 7.84 L/100 km. For UK mpg, divide 282.481 instead.

Why is UK mpg higher than US mpg?

The UK gallon is larger, 4.546 liters against 3.785, so the same car covers more miles per gallon on the UK scale.

How do I split the fuel cost of a trip?

Divide the total by the number of people. The calculator does this with the people field.
