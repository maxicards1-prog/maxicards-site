A time card without a spreadsheet

For each day, enter when you started, when you finished and how many minutes of unpaid break you took. The calculator subtracts the break, adds up the week and shows the total as hours and minutes and as decimal hours, the format payroll systems expect. The "Copy Monday" button fills Tuesday to Friday with the same times.

Worked example

Starting at 8:00 AM and finishing at 5:30 PM with a 30-minute break is 9 h 30 min minus 30 min = 9 hours. Five such days make 45 hours, which is 5 hours above a 40-hour week. At an hourly rate of 20, the straight-time pay is 45 × 20 = 900.00.

Overnight shifts

If the end time is earlier than the start time, for example 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM, the calculator treats the end as the next day and counts 8 hours before any break.

Overtime in the United States

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, covered non-exempt employees must be paid at least one and a half times their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. With a rate of 20, the example above would be 40 × 20 + 5 × 30 = 950.00. State rules and employer policies can differ, and this tool shows straight time only, so use it as a worksheet, not as payroll advice.

Tips for accurate hours

Record times as soon as you clock in and out rather than from memory. Enter only unpaid breaks in the break column: a paid 15-minute rest period stays inside your hours. Keep the page address from the copy link button if you want to save the week and come back to it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate hours worked in a day?

Subtract the start time from the end time, then subtract unpaid breaks. 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 30-minute break is 9 hours.

How do I convert hours and minutes on a time card to decimal?

Divide the minutes by 60 and add them to the hours. 38 h 45 min is 38 + 45 ÷ 60 = 38.75.

How do I calculate hours for a shift that passes midnight?

Enter the real start and end times. If the end is earlier than the start, the calculator counts it as the next day.

Are my times saved?

They stay in your browser. Use the copy link button to keep the whole week in the page address.
