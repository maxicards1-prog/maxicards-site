How BMI is calculated

Body mass index is your weight divided by the square of your height. In metric units: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². In imperial units: BMI = 703 × weight (lbs) ÷ height (in)². A person who is 5 ft 9 in (69 inches) and weighs 160 lbs has a BMI of 703 × 160 ÷ 69² = 23.6.

What BMI does not tell you

BMI is a quick screening number, not a diagnosis. It does not separate muscle from fat, so athletes can show a high BMI, and it does not apply to children, teenagers or pregnant women. Waist size, activity and medical history matter too: talk to a doctor about your own situation.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For adults, the World Health Organization classes a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 as a healthy weight.

How do I calculate BMI in pounds and inches?

Multiply your weight in pounds by 703, then divide by your height in inches squared. 150 lbs at 66 inches gives 703 × 150 ÷ 4,356 = 24.2.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle is heavier than fat, so a muscular person can have a high BMI without excess fat.

Can children use this BMI calculator?

No. Children and teenagers are assessed with age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles, not adult categories.
