Reaction time test

Click to startWhen the box turns green, click as fast as you can.

    Nothing is sent or stored on a server: everything stays in your browser.

    Click as soon as the box turns green: the test measures your reaction in milliseconds.

    Updated November 26, 2026 · the test runs in your browser, nothing is sent.

    How to take the test

    1. Click inside the box. It turns dark blue: wait.
    2. At a random moment (between 1.5 and 5 seconds) it turns green. Click as fast as you can.
    3. Your time appears in milliseconds (1,000 ms = 1 second). If you click too early, the attempt is canceled.
    4. Do 5 attempts: the test gives you the average, the median and your best time.

    The color only changes once per attempt, at an unpredictable moment, so nothing flashes.

    What your result means

    Lab measurements on students put the simple reaction time to a visual stimulus at about 190 ms on average, in the region of 200 ms. Your result here will usually be a little higher: the screen, the mouse, the browser and the computer each add a small delay to what is measured. There is no official “good time”. Compare your attempts with each other instead, on the same equipment. For more, read what is a good reaction time.

    How to measure more fairly

    • Do all 5 attempts in a row, rested and focused.
    • Keep your hand ready on the mouse without gripping it hard.
    • Use the median rather than the best time: it is less sensitive to one lucky or unlucky attempt.
    • A high-refresh-rate screen and a wired mouse reduce the device's own delay a little. You can check your screen with the refresh rate test.

    What this test is not

    It is a simple click-on-green test, not a medical or professional assessment, and it can't diagnose anything. Tiredness, caffeine, practice and distraction all change your numbers from one day to the next.

    Try a related test

    Want to measure raw speed instead of reflexes? Try the CPS test.

    Why the wait is random

    If the green signal came at a fixed moment, you could simply count in your head and click on time. A random delay makes the test measure your reaction instead. If you click before the box turns green, the attempt is canceled and you simply start again, so don't worry about a false start.

    Privacy

    The test runs in your browser. Your times are not recorded or sent.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the average reaction time?

    Laboratory studies give roughly 200 ms for an image that appears. Online results run a bit higher because of the equipment.

    Why does my result change every attempt?

    Your attention varies and the wait is random. That is why the test gives an average and a median over 5 attempts.

    Do my screen and mouse change the result?

    Yes, a little: each device adds a small delay. To compare results, always use the same equipment.

    How do I check my reaction time online?

    Click the box, wait for it to turn green, then click as fast as you can. After 5 attempts you get your average, median and best time in milliseconds.
