How to check a new monitor: 6 tests

Run six checks as soon as a monitor arrives: faulty pixels, uniformity, gradients, refresh rate, settings and the return window.

Page updated November 26, 2026.

Test it now: Refresh rate test · Dead pixel test

The method in 6 steps

  1. Connect it with the right cable: Use the cable and port the manual recommends for the resolution and refresh rate you want. A wrong cable can quietly limit what you get.
  2. Set the refresh rate in the system: In your display settings, choose the highest refresh rate offered. Then measure it with the refresh rate test. See also check a 144 Hz monitor.
  3. Hunt for faulty pixels: Run the dead pixel test full screen, going through black, white, red, green and blue. Look closely across the whole surface.
  4. Check uniformity: In the refresh rate test, show black, then the grays, in a dim room. Look for patches and glow along the edges.
  5. Look at the gradients: Compare the smooth gradient with the stepped one. The smooth one should stay smooth. Note any heavy banding, or a tint that shifts from one side to the other.
  6. Decide quickly: Keep photos and notes of what you see, and check the seller's return window and the maker's warranty before you throw the box away.

Why test right away

Return windows for online purchases are limited, and screen flaws are easiest to spot on day one, before your eyes get used to the picture. A full check takes about fifteen minutes.

Preparation

  • Clean the screen with a soft cloth.
  • Choose a room where you can dim the lights.
  • Let the monitor warm up for a few minutes: some uniformity problems change when it is warm.
  • Turn off automatic picture modes such as dynamic contrast and eco modes so you judge the raw display.

Faulty pixels

See dead pixel vs stuck pixel. Each manufacturer has its own tolerance for faulty pixels, written in its warranty. Read it before buying if you can, because that policy decides whether you get a replacement, not your impression of the screen.

Uniformity and backlight

Some glow along the edges on a black screen in a dark room is common on many panels. Look for what actually bothers you: a visible patch on a mid-gray background is a bigger problem than a faint glow in one corner on pure black. Photos capture these flaws poorly, so trust your eyes, knowing that judging them is somewhat subjective.

Refresh rate

A monitor sold as 144 Hz that measures at 60 Hz is very often just set to 60 in the system, or connected with the wrong cable. Fix those before you conclude it is defective. The test reports a measurement based on how often your browser draws frames, and it is our own reference reading, not an official rating.

A simple viewing routine

Sit at your normal distance and view each test screen for a few seconds before moving on. Look at the corners and edges as well as the center. Move your head slightly left, right, up and down to see how the picture changes with angle, since some panels shift color when viewed from the side. If you use the monitor for photo or video work, compare it with a screen you already trust before you decide.

Cables and ports

If the refresh rate or resolution is lower than expected, check the port on both the monitor and the computer. Some ports on a monitor support only certain modes, and the cable that shipped in the box is often the right one to start with. Your graphics settings may also need a restart before they apply.

Specs on the box

Brightness, contrast and response time on the box are measured by manufacturers under their own conditions. We give no reference figure here. Compare what you see with what you expected, and look for recent independent reviews of the specific model.

Spotting problems in games

After the tests, play something you know well for a short while. Dark scenes show backlight glow, fast motion shows how smooth the refresh rate feels, and bright flat colors expose uneven patches. Pay attention to anything that distracts you more than a few seconds, and write down which scene it was. That note is useful if you need to explain the problem to the seller.

If you find a defect

Write it down, take photos, and contact the seller within the return window. Keep the packaging until the trial period ends. If you need help with what to do next, the device troubleshooting guide covers keeping proof and isolating a fault.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to return a monitor bought online?

It depends on the seller and local law. Read the return conditions of your purchase and act quickly.

Is glow around the edges a defect?

It is common on many monitors. Judge whether it bothers you in normal use; a clear patch on a gray background is more of a problem.

How many dead pixels are allowed?

It depends on the manufacturer. Look for the pixel policy in the warranty of your model.

Why does my 144 Hz monitor show 60 Hz?

Most often the system is set to 60 Hz or the cable does not support more. Check your display settings and cable first.
