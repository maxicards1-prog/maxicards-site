How to type faster: 6 steps to a higher WPM

You get faster at typing by aiming for accuracy first, practicing in short regular sessions and sitting well: speed follows.

Page updated November 26, 2026.

Test it now: Typing test · Keyboard tester

The method in 6 steps

  1. Measure your starting point: Take the typing test three times on the same text and write down the median words per minute and accuracy.
  2. Set up your posture: Support your back, relax your shoulders, keep your forearms roughly level and your wrists free, with the screen at eye level. Poor posture tires you and slows you down.
  3. Place your fingers: Rest your index fingers on the small bumps on the F and J keys, and keep your other fingers on the home row. Each finger has its own keys.
  4. Accuracy before speed: Type slowly, without looking at the keys, and fix every mistake. Speed arrives once the movements feel certain. A fast score full of errors is not worth much.
  5. Practice in short, regular sessions: Ten to fifteen minutes most days beats one long session a week. Stop when your hands get tired.
  6. Vary the texts and track progress: Mix plain text with text that has numbers, capitals and punctuation. Repeat the test each week on the same text to see how you are doing.

What you can reasonably expect

We do not promise a timeframe or a gain. It depends on the person, how often they practice and where they start, and no reliable source lets us promise a figure. What we can offer is the method above, which is the one found in touch-typing instruction: accuracy, regularity and posture.

Typing without looking

Typing without watching the keyboard means memorizing where the keys are. Keep your eyes on the screen, or drape a light cloth over your hands. The first few days feel slower, and that is normal. To check where each key sits, the keyboard test shows the layout and lights up the keys you press.

What counts as a word

Typing tests usually count a word as five characters, counting spaces and punctuation. Your result is the correct characters divided by five, divided by the minutes. That is a convention, not a standard. To see why we do not give an average speed, read average typing speed.

Common mistakes

  • Looking at your hands for every letter.
  • Using only two or three fingers.
  • Tensing up and raising your shoulders.
  • Rushing from the start, which locks in bad habits.
  • Not correcting errors, which teaches the wrong movement.

Short drills that work

Pick a small set of letters and repeat easy words that use only those letters, then add a few more. Slow, steady repetition builds muscle memory better than random fast attempts. When you notice a specific mistake, such as swapping two letters, type that word ten times slowly. Rhythm matters too: try to keep a steady beat between keystrokes rather than bursts and pauses.

Your keyboard and layout

A comfortable keyboard of your own helps. Key feel, height and size all matter. Learn the layout you actually use, whether that is QWERTY or another one. The test reads physical keys, so it works with other layouts too. If a key does not respond or sticks, see keyboard key not working and how to clean a keyboard. If you get wrong characters, check keyboard typing wrong characters.

A simple weekly plan

Pick three short sessions a week. In the first, copy a slow text with perfect accuracy. In the second, practice trouble spots, such as keys you tend to miss or capital letters and punctuation. In the third, take the typing test and record your result. Keep a note of your results so you can see the trend, and ignore a single bad day: scores move up and down.

Practice that feels like real work

Once the basics feel comfortable, type things you actually write: emails, notes or messages. It is useful practice because it includes your own vocabulary and habits. Do not rush, and if you notice that you miss the same keys over and over, slow down on those words until they feel easy.

Reading your result

The test reports words per minute and accuracy. Look at both: a lower speed with high accuracy is a better base than the reverse. When your accuracy is steady, push the pace a little and watch whether your error count stays the same.

A word on health

Stop if you feel pain in your wrists or fingers. Take breaks and change your position from time to time. If discomfort persists, talk to a health professional.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to type faster?

It varies by person and practice. Track your progress weekly instead of aiming for a date.

Should I learn a new keyboard layout?

Learn the layout of the keyboard you use every day. The test reads physical keys and works with different layouts.

Is accuracy or speed more important?

Accuracy first. Correcting errors slows you down more than typing carefully does, and speed grows from reliable movements.

How is words per minute calculated?

Usually as correct characters divided by five, divided by minutes. It is a common convention, not a formal standard.
