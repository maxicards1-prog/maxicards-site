Check if your monitor is running at 144Hz

Run the refresh rate test: if it reads about 144 frames per second, your monitor runs at 144Hz; if not, check the system setting and the cable.

Page updated November 26, 2026.

Start with a quick test

Open the refresh rate test and start the measurement. Keep the tab in the foreground for about three seconds. The page counts the frames your browser draws, reports the most frequent value and the nearest standard rate. A value close to 144 means the monitor runs at 144Hz.

Check the setting in the system

- Windows 11: Settings > System > Display > Advanced display, then choose a refresh rate for the selected display. - Windows 10: Settings > System > Display > Advanced display settings. With several monitors, pick the right one under Choose Display, then use the Refresh rate list. - macOS: Apple menu > System Settings > Displays, then the Refresh Rate menu. Apple lists this for certain displays, such as 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro models and Apple's Pro displays, so the menu may not appear on every Mac or monitor. Menu labels change between versions.

If the test reads 60

This is the usual outcome on a monitor sold for more. Typical reasons: 1. The system is set to 60Hz. Pick the highest rate offered, as above. 2. The cable or port limits it. Some cables and ports cap the rate at a given resolution. Check your monitor's manual for the cable and port that support its top rate. 3. Power saving on a laptop can lower the rate. Plug it in. 4. Several monitors each have their own rate. 5. The graphics driver is missing or out of date. Microsoft also notes that a rate marked with an asterisk does not support your current resolution; choosing it changes the resolution too.

Why the result may not be exactly 144

Your browser draws a frame at each screen refresh, but it can miss a few if the tab is busy or in the background. The test shows the most common value, which is more reliable than a raw average, but it is not a laboratory instrument. A reading such as 143.9 is normal on a 144Hz monitor.

Variable refresh rates

Some monitors and Windows 11's Dynamic Refresh Rate change the rate depending on activity. In that case the test shows the rate at that moment. Run it during an animation to see the highest. Microsoft says Dynamic Refresh Rate can limit games that do not use variable refresh, and that turning it off may help.

Seeing the difference

In the test, a moving block looks smoother at 144Hz than at 60Hz, mostly when your eyes follow it. The gain is clearer in fast games or scrolling than on a still image.

If nothing works

Try another cable, another port and a direct connection to the graphics card. If you are shopping, our guide to checking a new monitor lists the tests to run when it arrives.

Frequently asked questions

What does 144Hz do?

The monitor updates the picture 144 times a second instead of 60, so motion looks smoother, particularly in fast games.

Why does my 144Hz monitor show 60Hz?

Most often the system setting is on 60Hz, or the cable or port cannot carry 144Hz at your resolution.

Can my browser limit the reading?

Yes, in a background tab or in power saving mode. Keep the tab in front while measuring.
