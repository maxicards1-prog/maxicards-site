Dead pixel vs stuck pixel

A dead pixel stays off and shows as a black dot, mostly on white; a stuck pixel stays lit in one color and stands out most on a black background.

Page updated November 26, 2026.

The difference in one minute

  • Dead pixel: the dot never lights. On a white or colored background, it looks like a tiny black point.
  • Stuck pixel: the dot stays lit in one color, such as red, green, blue or white. On black, it stands out like a small star.
  • Defective sub-pixel: only one of the three color parts (red, green, blue) is stuck or off, so the dot shows an odd tint.

Start with a quick test

Open the dead pixel test in full screen and step through the colors with the arrow keys or the mouse. Look closely and sweep the whole screen. On black, a lit pixel appears. On white, an unlit one appears. On red, green and blue, a faulty sub-pixel shows up. The colors are solid and nothing flashes.

Is it really a pixel?

  • Clean the screen. A speck of dust or a smudge can look like a bad pixel.
  • Move your head or change the color. A true pixel stays in the same place on the panel.
  • A larger glowing area near the edges is backlight bleed, not a pixel. Use the uniformity checks in the refresh rate test page instead.

What to do about it

  1. Write it down: the color, the position and the background where you see it. Take a close-up photo.
  2. Read your warranty. Each manufacturer has its own policy on how many bad pixels justify a replacement, and some do not count a single one. Read the terms before contacting support.
  3. If you just bought it, check the seller's return window, because a quick return is easier than a warranty claim later.

Can you fix a stuck pixel?

You will find advice that promises to revive a pixel with rapidly flashing patterns, or by pressing on the screen. Results are not guaranteed, pressing can damage the panel, and flashing patterns can be a problem for people sensitive to flicker. That is why this site only shows still, solid colors, and does not offer any of those tools.

When you are shopping

Our guide to checking a new monitor lists the tests to run as soon as a monitor arrives, within the return period.

If nothing works

If a bad pixel sits in the middle of your work area and bothers you, ask the seller or manufacturer about an exchange. If they refuse, their policy is the deciding factor, so keep your photos and the test result.

Frequently asked questions

How many dead pixels are acceptable?

It depends on the manufacturer and the model. Read the warranty terms for your monitor.

Can a dead pixel spread?

A faulty pixel does not infect its neighbors, but others can fail with time. Test when you receive it, then now and then.

Is a stuck pixel the same as burn-in?

No. A stuck pixel is a single dot that stays lit. Burn-in is a faded patch that comes from a static image shown for a long time.
