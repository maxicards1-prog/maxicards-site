Left and right headphone test

Play a sound on the left, then the right, at low volume: each earpiece should only sound on its own side.

Page updated November 26, 2026.

Volume first

Always start quiet. The speaker test begins at 30% on its own slider and shows a warning, because a sudden sound can startle you and high volume can hurt your ears. Raise the volume in small steps. The sound is generated in your browser and is never uploaded.

Left, right, center

Put your headphones on, open the speaker test and press Left. The sound should come from the left earpiece only. Then press Right, then Center, where the sound should appear in the middle of your head. If one side is silent or weak, try this: 1. Reseat the headphones, or remove and refit the ear tip. 2. Clean the earpiece grille gently. Wax and dust can block it. 3. Check the balance in your system sound settings, which can shift all sound to one side. Names and locations of this setting differ by system and version. 4. Try another device. If the problem follows the headphones, the headphones are the cause.

The frequency sweep

A sweep rises slowly from a low pitch to a high one. It lets you hear whether the sound is even, or whether there are rattles, crackles or gaps. Headphones do not all reproduce the extremes: a very low tone can be faint on small earbuds, which is normal. What people can hear also varies from person to person.

Low tones

The bass button plays a steady low note. If it rattles or buzzes in the earpiece, turn the volume down: small drivers are not made to push hard at low frequencies. On over-ear headphones, a buzz that remains at low volume can point to a damaged driver.

Bluetooth

A delay or lower quality can come from the Bluetooth link rather than the headphones. Test with a cable if you can.

Speakers

The test works with speakers too. Sit between them. If the Left sound comes from the right speaker, the cables are swapped.

A simple routine

Test one side at a time, at the same low volume, then swap the headphones around on your head in case the problem is your ears or fit rather than the headphones. If the quiet side stays on the same earpiece, the earpiece is the cause. If it follows the side of your head, it is probably fit or the position of the ear tip. Write down what you hear before changing anything, so you can compare afterward.

Wired problems

A crackle when you move the cable usually means a worn cable or plug. A plug that is not fully seated can also silence one side.

Test the mic as well

If your headphones have a microphone, check it with How to test a headset mic or the mic test.

Frequently asked questions

What volume should I use for the test?

Start low and raise it slowly. Avoid high volume.

My right earpiece is quieter. Could it be the balance setting?

It could. Check your system sound balance and try the headphones on another device to see whether the problem follows them.

Why do my earbuds sound weak on the lowest tones?

Small earbuds often cannot reproduce very low tones at full strength. That is normal.
