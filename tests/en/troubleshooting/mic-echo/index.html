Echo in your mic: how to stop it

Echo almost always means your mic is picking up your speakers: wear headphones or turn the speaker volume down.

Page updated November 26, 2026.

Where echo comes from

On a call, the other person's voice comes out of your speakers. If your mic hears it, it goes back to them a moment later and they hear themselves as an echo. The fault is on your side, but they are the ones who hear it, which makes it hard to notice.

Fixes, from most to least effective

  1. Wear headphones. The sound no longer travels through the air, so the mic cannot pick it up. This is the most reliable fix.
  2. Turn the speakers down and move the mic away from them.
  3. Point the mic away from the speakers, or use a directional mic close to your mouth.
  4. Use echo cancellation in your meeting app if it offers one. It helps, but it is not as dependable as headphones.
  5. One app at a time. Two programs using the mic can feed sound back to each other.

Check it at home

Open the mic test, record a few seconds and play it back with your speakers on and no headphones. You will hear how the sound loops. Then repeat with headphones on: the loop disappears. The test page recommends headphones so you do not create the very echo you are testing.

Echo, feedback and sidetone are different

  • Echo is a delayed repeat of a voice.
  • Feedback is a rising squeal: the mic hears the speakers in a loop. Turn the volume down right away.
  • Sidetone is your own voice played in your headphones. Some mics and sound cards let you adjust it.

When the echo is not yours

If other people hear their own voice but you already wear headphones, it may be one of them who is using speakers. Ask them to put headphones on.

A quick habit that prevents most echo

Before a call, run the recording test once with your usual headphones on. If the playback is clean, you are ready. If you hear your own voice repeating, one of your audio devices is still in the loop, so check which speakers and which mic the app has selected.

Check your meeting app

Most apps have their own audio settings, including echo cancellation and noise suppression. If the browser test is clean but the app still echoes, review those settings: see Zoom mic not working, Teams mic and camera not working or Discord not picking up your mic.

If nothing works

Try a different mic, such as a headset. Echo that survives a headset on both sides is rare and points to a software setting or a second device still running in the room, such as another laptop on the same call.

Frequently asked questions

Why do other people hear an echo and I do not?

Their own voice is coming back through your mic. You do not hear it, but a headset on your side usually solves it.

Can software remove echo?

Many meeting apps have echo cancellation. It helps, but headphones are more reliable.

Why does my laptop echo when someone else joins the same call?

Two devices in the same room can pick up each other's speakers. Keep only one device's mic and speakers on, or use headphones.
