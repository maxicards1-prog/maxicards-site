Mouse double-clicking on a single click

Most often the button's switch is worn and sends two clicks for one press; the mouse test shows it within seconds.

Page updated November 26, 2026.

Start with a quick test

Open the mouse test and click once, cleanly, on the button that misbehaves. The page records the time between clicks. If two clicks arrive very close together, it flags a possible unintended double-click. The default limit is 80 milliseconds, and you can change it. That number is our own reference value, not a standard: below it, a deliberate click is unlikely.

Likely causes

  1. A worn switch. After heavy use, the contact inside bounces and registers a second signal. This is the most common reason.
  2. Dirt under the button.
  3. A system setting. If your double-click speed is set too slow, two deliberate slow clicks count as one double-click.
  4. Software. Mouse utilities, macros or a driver update can repeat clicks.
  5. A connection problem. An unstable wireless receiver or a low battery.

Fixes to try

  • Plug the mouse into another computer. If the problem follows the mouse, the mouse is at fault. If not, it is your system.
  • Check the double-click speed. On Windows 11, go to Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Mouse, then open Additional mouse settings and use the Buttons tab to move the Double-click speed slider. On Windows 10, the path starts at Settings > Devices > Mouse. On a Mac, open System Settings > Accessibility > Pointer Control and adjust Double-click speed. The exact labels can differ by version.
  • Replace the battery, move the receiver closer, or try a cable.
  • Blow gently around the button with dry air. Avoid opening a mouse that is still under warranty.
  • Uninstall or update mouse software, then test again.
  • Use the warranty. A double-clicking switch is a well-known defect, and the manufacturer may cover it depending on its terms, so read them.
  • If you know how to solder, the switch can be replaced. Otherwise, a new mouse often costs less than your time.

What about a ghost click?

If the mouse clicks when you are not touching it, check whether a keyboard accessibility feature is simulating clicks, and whether the surface under the mouse is stable.

For gamers

An unintended double-click also skews your CPS test. Run the mouse test first, then compare. Our page on what counts as a good CPS explains how to read the result.

Wired or wireless?

If you can, test the same mouse on a cable. A wireless receiver in a crowded USB hub, or a battery near the end of its life, can mimic a worn switch. When the cable version behaves, you have found the real cause, and the fix is probably a new battery or a better receiver position rather than a new mouse.

If nothing works

If the mouse test still flags double-clicks on another computer, treat the switch as worn. Contact the seller or manufacturer, or replace the mouse.

Frequently asked questions

How fast does a double-click have to be to look like a fault?

This site uses 80 ms as a rough reference, and you can change it. It is our own value, not a standard.

Why does my mouse double-click only in games?

Games read raw clicks, so a bouncing switch shows up there first. Run the mouse test to confirm.

Can I fix a double-clicking mouse myself?

If you can solder, you can replace the switch. Often it is quicker to use the warranty or buy a new mouse.
