    How to test a mouse

    1. Click inside the box with each button: left, right and middle (press the wheel down). If your mouse has side buttons, try them too: they are buttons 4 and 5.
    2. Turn the wheel up, then down. The counters go up.
    3. Each button lights up while it is pressed. A button that never lights up is dead or badly connected.

    Spotting unwanted double clicks

    This is the classic fault of a worn mouse: one press is counted twice, so a folder opens by itself or a dragged item drops too early. The test records the time between two clicks of the same button. Click slowly, about ten times. If two clicks arrive closer together than the threshold (80 ms by default), the test flags them.

    The threshold is our own reference value, not a standard. A person doesn't click twice on purpose that quickly, so a very short interval during slow clicking is a warning sign. To confirm, repeat the test and try the mouse on another computer. See mouse double-clicking by itself.

    Buttons 4 and 5

    These are the side buttons, often used for “back” and “forward” in a browser. Inside the box their usual action is blocked, so the test doesn't take you away from the page.

    The scroll wheel

    The up and down counters go up with each notch. If the page or the counter jumps the wrong way, or the count is erratic, the wheel's sensor may be dirty or worn. Wheel speed can also be changed in your system or mouse software, which affects how many events one notch produces.

    Tips for reliable results

    Test on a flat, clean surface and close any mouse software that remaps buttons while you test. If your mouse has a polling-rate or debounce setting in its own software, note it before you compare results. Gaming mice sometimes mark side buttons as macros, which can hide them from the browser. When in doubt, plug the mouse straight into the computer, not through a hub.

    Privacy

    The test only counts clicks inside the box, in your browser. Nothing is recorded or sent.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why is my mouse double-clicking on its own?

    Most often the switch under the button is worn and bounces, sending two signals for one press. The test shows the shortest interval it measured.

    How do I test my mouse buttons online?

    Click inside the box with each button, one at a time. Each one lights up and its counter goes up. Buttons 4 and 5 are the side buttons.

    My wheel button doesn't light up.

    Press the wheel straight down without turning it. If nothing happens, check in your mouse software that the button isn't assigned to another function.

    Does the test work with a laptop touchpad?

    Yes for left and right clicks. There are no buttons 4 or 5, and the wheel corresponds to two-finger scrolling.
